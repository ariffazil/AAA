## PATTERN 1 — PREMATURE COMMITMENT

### CODE / SOFTWARE ENGINEERING

**1. Brooks, F. P., Jr. (1986). "No Silver Bullet — Essence and Accidents of Software Engineering." *Proceedings of the IFIP Tenth World Computing Conference*, pp. 1069–1076; UNC Technical Report TR86-020, Sept 1986. Also *IEEE Computer*, April 1987.**
URL: https://www.cs.unc.edu/techreports/86-020.pdf · mirror: https://worrydream.com/refs/Brooks_1986_-_No_Silver_Bullet.pdf
- **Claim (INT, with OBS and SPEC components):** Following Aristotle, Brooks divides the difficulty of software into "essence, the difficulties inherent in the nature of the software, and accidents, those difficulties which today attend its production but which are not inherent." The essence is "a construct of interlocking concepts: data sets, relationships among data items, algorithms, and invocations of functions," which "is abstract, in that the conceptual construct is the same under many different representations." (INT — an interpretive framework). Verbatim: "I believe the hard part of building software to be the specification, design, and testing of this conceptual construct, not the labor of representing it and testing the fidelity of the representation. We still make syntax errors, to be sure; but they are fuzz compared to the conceptual errors in most systems." (INT, stated as belief). The four irreducible properties are "complexity, conformity, changeability, and invisibility" (INT). §2.3 Changeability: "The software entity is constantly subject to pressures for change" (OBS, with supporting industrial comparison to buildings/cars/computers). The productivity forecast is SPEC and is the part that has aged worst.
- **Relevance:** The load-bearing line for premature commitment is §5.2: **"The hardest single part of building a software system is deciding precisely what to build. … No other part of the work so cripples the resulting system if done wrong. No other part is more difficult to rectify later."** That is a 1986 statement of premature commitment as an engineering law, not a model-behaviour curiosity: the cost of settling the conceptual construct early is that the error is *hardest to undo*, and the agent that commits before the requirements are settled pays that price in exactly the way Brooks describes. Note also the direction of the fix Brooks gives — "the iterative extraction and refinement of the product requirements. For the truth is, the client does not know what he wants." This anchors the *sequencing* point in the base document §1 (Mehta et al.: failure comes from sequencing, not from commitment per se) in pre-LLM software engineering.
- **Receipt status: VERIFIED** — primary PDF read in full; all quotations above extracted verbatim from the UNC TR86-020 text.

**2. Reeves, J. W. (1992). "What Is Software Design?" *C++ Journal*, Fall 1992. Reprinted in *Code as Design: Three Essays by Jack W. Reeves*, developer.\*, 2005.**
URL: https://www.developerdotstar.com/mag/articles/reeves_design.html
- **Claim (INT, containing one strong OBS):** "the only software documentation that actually seems to satisfy the criteria of an engineering design is the source code listings" — i.e. **source code is the design**, and "actually building software is done by compilers and linkers" (INT). Derives: "it is cheaper and simpler to just build the design and test it than to do anything else" (INT/DER). Rejects phase-freezing: "If any aspect of the design is frozen out of the refinement process, it is hardly surprising that the final design will be poor or even unworkable" (DER from his premise). On type systems: C++ "allows us to directly express high level information about design components … With its stronger type checking, it also helps the process of detecting design errors" (INT). Contains an explicit OBS first-person case study (below).
- **Relevance:** This is the single best CODE anchor for premature commitment, because Reeves supplies a *documented incident* of an abstraction locked in too early. Verbatim: **"I recently worked on a project where a timing dependency was discovered between the internals of module A and another module B. Unfortunately, the internals of module A were hidden behind an abstraction that did not permit any way to incorporate the invocation of module B in its proper sequence. Naturally, by the time the problem was discovered, it was much too late to try to change the abstraction of A. As expected, what happened was an increasingly complex set of 'fixes' applied to the internal design of A. … Every new fix was likely to break some older fix."** Note that this one passage anchors *two* of the three patterns: the frozen abstraction is premature commitment, and "every new fix was likely to break some older fix" is action bias compounding across a sequence — described in 1992, in a human team, with no LLM involved. It also directly contradicts the base document's framing that these are novel: Reeves' closing summary line "Coding actually makes sense more often than believed" and "we should not be afraid to code our designs as we derive them" is a *pro*-early-action position, so the CODE literature is not univocal on which direction the error runs.
- **Receipt status: VERIFIED** — full essay text read; quotations verbatim.

**3. Brooks, F. P., Jr. (1975). *The Mythical Man-Month: Essays on Software Engineering*, Addison-Wesley. Ch. 2 "Aristocracy, Autocracy, and Decency"; ch. 5 "The Second-System Effect."**
URL: https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~weimerw/2018-481/readings/mythical-man-month.pdf
- **Claim (INT for conceptual integrity; OBS for the second-system effect):** "I will contend that conceptual integrity is the most important consideration in system design" and "Conceptual integrity in turn dictates that the design must proceed from one mind, or from a very small number of agreeing resonant minds" (INT). Architecture is distinguished from implementation: "By the architecture of a system, I mean the complete and detailed specification of the user interface" (INT). Second-system effect, verbatim: "As he designs the first work, frill after frill and embellishment after embellishment occur to him. These get stored away to be used 'next time.' … This second is the most dangerous system a man ever designs. … The general tendency is to over-design the second system, using all the ideas and frills that were cautiously sidetracked on the first one." (OBS — grounded in the IBM OS/360 / System 360 history Brooks managed).
- **Relevance:** Two distinct anchors. (a) *Conceptual integrity* is the CODE-domain name for the state a premature-committing agent destroys: it is the property that the design reflect a single coherent model, and it is exactly what is lost when an agent settles on an interpretation locally and then defends it against a whole repository that encodes a different model. (b) *The second-system effect* is premature commitment to generality — committing to abstractions, plugin systems, and configuration frameworks before any second instance has produced evidence that they are needed. This is the 1975 antecedent of the base document's §6 sin #2 "Speculative Architecture" and of Metz 2016 below.
- **Receipt status: VERIFIED** — primary PDF text read; quotations verbatim.

**4. Lehman, M. M. (1980). "Programs, Life Cycles, and Laws of Software Evolution." *Proceedings of the IEEE* 68(9): 1060–1076, doi:10.1109/proc.1980.11805. (Laws first formulated 1974: Lehman & Belady, *IBM Systems Journal* 13(3): 227–243.)**
URL: https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1456074 · laws text: https://www.cs.kent.edu/~jmaletic/cs63902/Papers/Lehman96.pdf
- **Claim (DER from OBS):** Two laws, verbatim from the 1996 "Revisited" restatement of the 1980 text: **Law I, Continuing Change** — "An E-type program that is used must be continually adapted else it becomes progressively less satisfactory." **Law II, Increasing Complexity** — "As a program is evolved its complexity increases unless work is done to maintain or reduce it." The laws are derived from multi-decade measurement of real E-type systems including IBM OS/360 (OBS → DER). E-type = programs that "embody" a model of some real-world activity and are therefore judged against that world, as distinct from S-type (fully specified) and P-type (partly specified).
- **Relevance:** This is the CODE-domain anchor for **action bias as refusal to refactor**. Law II is asymmetric in a way that matters for the pattern: complexity does not merely fail to decrease, it *increases* by default, and the only counterforce is deliberate maintenance work. An agent that keeps acting (adding fixes, adding branches, adding patches) without ever doing the restructuring work that Law II demands is not neutral — it is on the wrong side of an empirical law. And premature commitment maps onto Law I: the wrong abstraction locked in early is precisely what makes the system "progressively less satisfactory" as the world it models changes. Lehman also gives the review a *measurement tradition* (version-history metrics over decades) rather than an opinion tradition.
- **Receipt status: HYPOTHESIS** — law statements and citation details verified verbatim against Lehman 1996 (*Laws of Software Evolution Revisited*, Kent State mirror) and lawsofsoftwareengineering.com; the 1980 *Proc. IEEE* primary is paywalled and was not read in this pass.

**5. Cunningham, W. (1992). "The WyCash Portfolio Management System." Experience Report, OOPSLA '92, pp. 29–30. ACM. (Origin of the technical-debt metaphor.)**
URL: http://c2.com/doc/oopsla92.html · Cunningham's own retrospective: http://wiki.c2.com/?WardExplainsDebtMetaphor · Fowler's account: https://martinfowler.com/bliki/TechnicalDebt.html
- **Claim (OBS/INT):** Cunningham's own words on coining the metaphor: "I coined the debt metaphor to explain the refactoring that we were doing on the WyCash product … if we failed to make our program align with what we then understood to be the proper way to think about our financial objects, then we were gonna continually stumble over that disagreement and that would slow us down which was like paying interest on a loan. I thought borrowing money was a good idea, I thought that rushing software out the door to get some experience with it was a good idea, but that of course, you would eventually go back and as you learned things about that software you would repay that loan by refactoring the program to reflect your experience as you acquired it." And the failure mode: "there were plenty of cases where people would rush software out the door and learn things but never put that learning back into the program, and that by analogy was borrowing money thinking that you never had to pay it back. In other words, the interest is total — you'll make zero progress."
- **Relevance:** Directly contradicts a common misreading and thereby sharpens the pattern. Cunningham's original metaphor **endorsed** committing early: shipping fast to acquire experience is "a good idea," provided the learning is paid back. The defect he names is not early commitment — it is *early commitment without repayment*. That distinguishes premature commitment as a *sequencing* error (base document §1, Mehta et al.) from early action as such, and it makes "refusal to refactor" the specific action-bias failure rather than action itself. "The interest is total — you'll make zero progress" is the CODE-domain statement of compounding.
- **Receipt status: CLAIM** — Cunningham's own first-person account read verbatim, but via the C2 wiki and Fowler's secondary account, not the OOPSLA '92 proceedings paper (paywalled/short).

**6. Metz, S. (2016). "The Wrong Abstraction." sandimetz.com, 20 Jan 2016.**
URL: https://sandimetz.com/blog/2016/1/20/the-wrong-abstraction
- **Claim (INT):** "duplication is far cheaper than the wrong abstraction"; therefore "prefer duplication over the wrong abstraction." The argument is that a wrong abstraction is worse than the duplication it was created to remove, because every new case gets bent to fit an abstraction that does not actually hold, and undoing an abstraction that has already been adopted at many call sites is harder than never having made it.
- **Relevance:** The most-cited contemporary CODE-domain name for premature commitment to a model. It also gives the review a *reversibility asymmetry* that the AI literature does not usually state: the cost of a wrong abstraction is not symmetric with the cost of duplication, because the abstraction propagates. Related practitioner reception is itself evidence the idea is contested — codewithjason.com argues "the wrong abstraction" is just "poorly-de-duplicated code," and the Hacker News thread (id 48620090, 2026) still relitigates it. Useful for showing the pattern is live and unresolved in human engineering, not settled doctrine.
- **Receipt status: CLAIM** — practitioner essay; title and both verbatim slogans confirmed at the canonical URL; full argument read via the site plus secondary discussion.

**7. Parnas, D. L. (1972). "On the Criteria To Be Used in Decomposing Systems into Modules." *Communications of the ACM* 15(12): 1053–1058, doi:10.1145/361598.361623. (Earlier as Purdue/Khoury TR, Aug 1971.)**
URL: https://prl.khoury.northeastern.edu/img/p-tr-1971.pdf · https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/361598.361623
- **Claim (DER):** Parnas compares two decompositions of the same system (a "line storage" module example): one by flowchart-derived functional decomposition, one by **information hiding**. Verbatim from the TR: "Note, however, that nothing is said about the criteria to use in dividing the system into modules." He argues decomposition criteria should be chosen so that each module hides a design decision likely to change, and demonstrates that flowchart-based decomposition yields modules coupled to every other module's internals.
- **Relevance:** The formal CODE-domain criterion for *what should be committed to early and what should not*. Information hiding is a theory about which decisions are safe to freeze: hide the ones likely to change, expose the ones that are stable. Premature commitment, in this vocabulary, is committing to an interface that encodes a design decision which then changes. Parnas is also the source of the "module = a design decision" framing that makes an abstraction a *commitment* at all rather than a mere convenience.
- **Receipt status: HYPOTHESIS** — citation details, publication venue, and the two-decomposition argument verified via the 1971 TR text, ACM DL record, and ResearchGate design-concern summary; full 1972 CACM paper not read line-by-line in this pass.

**8. Cardelli, L. & Wegner, P. (1985). "On Understanding Types, Data Abstraction, and Polymorphism." *ACM Computing Surveys* 17(4): 471–523, doi:10.1145/6041.6042.**
URL: https://www.classes.cs.uchicago.edu/archive/2012/spring/22300-1/papers/Cardelli-Wegner.pdf
- **Claim (DER):** Verbatim abstract: "Our objective is to understand the notion of *type* in programming languages, present a model of typed, polymorphic programming languages that reflects recent research in type theory, and examine the relevance of recent research to the design of practical programming languages." They "develop a λ-calculus-based model for type systems" and give "a unifying framework for polymorphic type systems … in terms of the typed λ-calculus augmented to include binding of types by quantification as well as binding of values by abstraction," obtaining "a simple and precise characterization of a powerful type system that includes abstract data types, parametric polymorphism, and multiple inheritance in a single consistent framework."
- **Relevance:** The brief asked for "type systems as constraints" — this is the canonical formal treatment. Its relevance to premature commitment is that a type system is a machine-checked commitment: declaring a type is freezing a claim about the domain, and the checker enforces it thereafter. That is a case where early commitment is *desirable and productive* (contra Metz), because the type is checkable. The distinction that follows — commitments that are machine-checkable vs. commitments that are only defended — is the sharpest line this slice offers, and it separates the good and bad cases of the same behaviour. Cardelli & Wegner also give the historical framing (type theory from Frege/Russell through λ-calculus) that ties this domain to the SYMBOL section below.
- **Receipt status: VERIFIED** — abstract read verbatim from ACM DL record and the UChicago PDF mirror; body not read in full.

**9. Boehm, B. & Basili, V. R. (2001). "Software Defect Reduction Top 10 List." *IEEE Computer* 34(1): 135–137, Jan 2001. (Restates Boehm 1981, *Software Engineering Economics*.)**
URL: https://www.cs.umd.edu/users/basili/publications/journals/J81.pdf

**10. Formal language theory: undecidability of semantic properties, and inherent ambiguity.**
Rice, H. G. (1953). "Classes of Recursively Enumerable Sets and Other Decision Problems." *Transactions of the AMS* 74(1): 358–366. → **Rice's theorem**: "all non-trivial semantic properties of programs are undecidable" (a non-trivial property is "one that is neither true for every program, nor false for every program"); proved by reduction from the halting problem. URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice%27s_theorem
Ambiguity: a context-free grammar is ambiguous if some terminal string has two distinct parse trees; a CFL is **inherently ambiguous** if every grammar for it is ambiguous; and it is undecidable whether a given CFG generates an inherently ambiguous language. URLs: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambiguous_grammar · https://www.brics.dk/RS/07/10/BRICS-RS-10.pdf (Schnatmann & Schütt 2007, BRICS RS-07-10, "Analyzing Ambiguity of Context-Free Grammars") · https://cstheory.stackexchange.com/questions/4352
- **Claim (DER — proved):** No algorithm can decide arbitrary non-trivial semantic properties of programs. Separately: some languages admit no unambiguous grammar at all, and ambiguity is itself undecidable in general.
- **Relevance:** These are the two formal-language-theory anchors for premature commitment, and they say something the AI literature does not: **the ambiguity an agent must resolve before acting is not always resolvable by inspection.** Rice's theorem means the semantic property the agent is trying to establish ("does this function get called anywhere?", "is this field actually used?", "will this change break that caller?") is in general undecidable — so the base document's §6 sin #5 ("Deletion Disasters: the assistant decides a function is 'not used' and deletes it. Sometimes right. Often, wrong.") is not sloppiness but the predictable output of an agent guessing at an undecidable predicate. Inherent ambiguity means that for some inputs there is no single correct parse to wait for, so *some* commitment is forced — which is why the base document's sequencing framing (commitment is only premature if evidence that *would have changed the decision* was skipped) is the right one and not a blanket "never commit."
- **Receipt status: VERIFIED** for Rice's theorem statement and proof method (read via Wikipedia statement + proof sketch); **HYPOTHESIS** for the inherent-ambiguity/undecidability attributions (verified via three independent secondary sources including BRICS RS-07-10; classical attributions to Ginsburg & Ullian 1966 not confirmed against primary).

### SYMBOL / LANGUAGE MEANING

**11. Stalnaker, R. C. (1978). "Assertion." In P. Cole (ed.), *Syntax and Semantics* Vol. 9: *Pragmatics*, pp. 315–332. New York: Academic Press.**
URL: https://archive.nytud.hu/program/szuperkurzus2006/Stalnaker_78.pdf
- **Claim (DER):** Verbatim: **"To make an assertion is to reduce the context set in a particular way, provided that there are no objections from the other participants in the conversation. The particular way in which the context set is reduced is that all of the possible situations incompatible with what is said are eliminated. … the essential effect of an assertion is to change the presuppositions of the participants in the conversation by adding the content of what is asserted to what is presupposed. This effect is avoided only if the assertion is rejected."** Presupposition defined dispositionally: "A proposition is presupposed if the speaker is disposed to act as if he assumes or believes that the proposition is true, and as if he assumes or believes that his audience assumes or believes that it is true as well." Context set = "the set of possible worlds recognized by the speaker to be the 'live options'." **Defective context**: "A DEFECTIVE CONTEXT will have a kind of instability, and will tend to adjust to the equilibrium position of a nondefective context. Because hearers will interpret the purposes and content of what is said in terms of their own presuppositions, any unnoticed discrepancies between the presuppositions of speaker and addressees is likely to lead to a failure of communication." Three principles of assertion: (1) "A proposition asserted is always true in some but not all of the possible worlds in the context set"; (2) any assertive utterance should express a proposition with a truth value in each world of the context set; (3) "The same proposition is expressed relative to each possible world in the context set."
- **Relevance:** This is the formal model of what "commitment" means in language, and it supplies the machinery the brief was looking for. Three things transfer directly. (a) **Assertion is irreversible-by-default**: once asserted and unobjected-to, the content is added to what is presupposed, and the alternatives are *eliminated* from the context set — the speaker cannot cheaply un-say it. That is the SYMBOL-domain analogue of premature commitment, and it has an explicit escape hatch ("This effect is avoided only if the assertion is rejected") that maps onto the base document's "wait for feedback" correction philosophy. (b) **The defective context** is the precise name for what happens when an agent commits to a referent the human has not established: the two participants now carry different presupposition sets, the context is unstable, and "unnoticed discrepancies … is likely to lead to a failure of communication." Premature commitment in dialogue is not a politeness failure; it is a formally characterized defective context. (c) **Principle 3** is the strongest single anchor in this whole slice: Stalnaker shows that if an utterance expresses *different* propositions in different live worlds, "the audience does not know which of those worlds we are in, and if it did the assertion would be pointless. So the statement, made in that context, expresses an intention that is essentially ambiguous." An agent that names a referent while multiple referents remain live in the context set produces exactly this — an essentially ambiguous intention that cannot do the job an assertion is for. Stalnaker's prescribed repair is *diagonalization* (the dagger operator): reinterpret the utterance to express the diagonal proposition. That is a formal account of "surface the assumption instead of resolving it silently."
- **Receipt status: VERIFIED** — full primary PDF read; all quotations verbatim.

**12. Lewis, D. (1979). "Scorekeeping in a Language Game." *Journal of Philosophical Logic* 8: 339–359. Reprinted as ch. 13 in *Philosophical Papers* Vol. I, Oxford UP, 1983.**
URL: https://semantics.uchicago.edu/kennedy/classes/f09/semprag1/lewis79.pdf
- **Claim (DER):** Conversations have a **conversational score**, with components including what is presupposed and the **standards of precision** in force; utterances shift the score, and the score is tracked by all participants. The **rules of accommodation** govern shifts that happen without anyone asserting anything. Rule 1, for presupposition, verbatim as quoted in von Fintel 2000: **"If at time t something is said that requires presupposition P to be acceptable, and if P is not presupposed just before t, then — ceteris paribus and within certain limits — presupposition P comes into existence at t."** (Lewis, 1979)
- **Relevance:** Accommodation is the mechanism by which an unestablished commitment becomes established *without ever being asserted or defended*. This is the most precise available SYMBOL-domain account of premature commitment in dialogue, and it is more specific than Stalnaker: the agent does not merely assert a referent, it *presupposes* one, and the default behaviour of a cooperative interlocutor is to accommodate — to silently add the presupposition to the common ground in order to keep the conversation moving. Von Fintel's analysis makes the asymmetry explicit: assertion is "subject to discussion," whereas presupposition is added "tacitly and without fuss" when the hearer is "genuinely agnostic" and trusts the speaker "not to speak inappropriately or falsely," or simply does not want to disrupt the smoothness of the exchange. That is the authority structure of premature commitment: the commitment is smuggled in on the low-friction channel precisely because the low-friction channel does not require evidence. Lewis also supplies the *standards of precision* component, which is how a language game absorbs vagueness — relevant to how an agent's loose claim gets tightened by the hearer rather than challenged.

Bibliographic details (1979, J. Phil. Logic 8: 339–359) independently confirmed at four sources. **Flag the date correction: 1979, not 1980.**

**13. Brandom, R. B. (1994). *Making It Explicit: Reasoning, Representing, and Discursive Commitment*. Cambridge MA: Harvard University Press.**

- **Claim (INT):** Brandom develops a **deontic scorekeeping** model (1994, pp. 141–271) in which conceptual content is constituted by two deontic statuses — **commitment** and **entitlement** — tracked perspectively by participants. The key normative asymmetry, verbatim from the NDPR précis discussion: the normativity that makes conceptual content possible is "the 'I-Thou' interpretive difference in social perspective between … **acknowledging** a commitment (oneself) and **attributing** a commitment (to someone else)." **14. Frege, G. (1892). "Über Sinn und Bedeutung." *Zeitschrift für Philosophie und philosophische Kritik*, NF 100: 25–50.**

**16. Kripke, S. A. (1972/1980). *Naming and Necessity*. Lectures delivered Princeton, January 1970; first published 1972 in D. Davidson & G. **18. Peirce, C. S. (1931–58). *Collected Papers*, esp. CP 2.228–2.229 and CP 5.473 (icon/index/symbol trichotomy); *Writings of Charles S. Peirce* Vol. 2 (1984).**
URL: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/peirce-semiotics/ · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiotic_theory_of_Charles_Sanders_Peirce
- **Claim (INT):** Signhood is an **irreducibly triadic** relation among sign, object, and interpretant — "Signhood is a way of being in relation, not a way of being in itself." Per Peirce's Reduction Thesis, triads are necessary because "genuinely triadic relations cannot be completely analyzed in terms of monadic and dyadic predicates." The trichotomy: an **icon** denotes its object "by virtue of a quality which is shared by them"; an **index** denotes "by virtue of an *actual connection* involving them, one that he also calls a *real relation* in virtue of its being irrespective of interpretation … in contrast to the icon, which has only a *ground* for denotation of its object, and in contrast to the symbol, which denotes by an interpretive *habit or law*." - **Relevance:** Peirce gives the review the sharpest available taxonomy for *how* an identifier can mean. The distinction that matters is index vs symbol: an index denotes by an **actual connection** irrespective of interpretation — which is exactly what a real file path, a live process id, or an actually-imported module is. A symbol denotes by **habit or law** — convention only — which is what a name in a natural-language plan or a hallucinated API is. Premature agent independence is the substitution of a symbol for an index: the agent produces something that functions, in its own output, as though it had an actual connection to the referent, when it only has a habitual/conventional one. Peirce's irreducible-triadicity claim reinforces Ogden & Richards from a different direction: the meaning relation *cannot* be reduced to the dyad sign↔object, so no amount of inspection of the emitted symbol can reveal whether the third term (interpretant, or in Ogden & Richards' terms the thought/reference) is present. Peirce's unending interpretant chain is also the semiotic counterpart of the compounding action sequence in Pattern 2.

Compounding action bias therefore has a precise conversational model — it is score drift, not merely accumulated momentum.

**30. Peirce, C. S. — the unending interpretant chain.** (Full citation at entry 18.)
- **Claim (INT):** "a sign of an object leads to interpretants, which, as signs, lead to further interpretants … The process is logically structured to perpetuate itself." That is a structural, not psychological, account of why a sequence of interpretive acts keeps going: termination is not built into the relation. For Pattern 2 this matters because it locates the compounding in the *sign relation* rather than in the actor's disposition. **31. Stalnaker, R. C. (1978) — the context set only shrinks.** (Full citation at entry 11.)
- **Claim relevant to Pattern 2 (DER):** "To make an assertion is to reduce the context set … all of the possible situations incompatible with what is said are eliminated." - **Relevance:** Stalnaker's model is **monotone**: the moves of the game shrink the context set, and the participants share an interest in shrinking it. Nothing in the model naturally expands the set again — rejected assertions merely *block* the reduction ("If an assertion is rejected, the context remains as it was"), they do not restore eliminated worlds. So a conversation in which an agent keeps acting and asserting is a ratchet: each move permanently removes live alternatives, and there is no move in the calculus that puts them back. This is the formal reason action bias compounds rather than self-corrects, and it explains why the base document's §1 evidence-conditioned-execution framing (separate evidence-gathering from action) has a counterpart in pragmatics: evidence-gathering is the only phase in which the context set is still large.

### CODE / SOFTWARE ENGINEERING

**32. Miller, M. S., Yee, K.-P. & Shapiro, J. (2003). "Capability Myths Demolished." Technical Report SRL2003-02, Systems Research Laboratory, Johns Hopkins University.**
URL: https://papers.agoric.com/assets/pdf/papers/capability-myths-demolished.pdf · mirror: https://classpages.cselabs.umn.edu/Fall-2021/csci5271/papers/SRL2003-02.pdf
- **Claim (DER):** The paper distinguishes **four models** that have all been called "capability": Model 1 *ACLs as columns* (standard Unix filesystem, Unix with setfacl(), NT ACLs), Model 2 *capabilities as rows* (**POSIX capabilities**), Model 3 *capabilities as keys* (SPKI, Unix file descriptors), Model 4 *object capabilities* (CAP, Hydra, KeyKOS, W7, EROS, E). Seven properties (A–G) separate them. It demolishes three myths — the **Equivalence Myth** (ACL and capability systems are formally equivalent), the **Confinement Myth**, and the **Irrevocability Myth**. Central conceptual claim, on **designation and authority**, verbatim: "The ACL model presumes some namespace, such as the space of filenames, that subjects use to designate resources. This namespace must necessarily be separate from the representation of authority in an ACL system, since the arrows representing authorities point in the opposite direction. Looking only at the matrix without considering the direction of the arrows **assumes away the designation problem, which is arguably one of the deepest problems in computer security.**" And: "In a capability system, each capability points from a subject to a resource. Consequently **every capability can serve both to designate which resource to access, and to provide the authority to perform that access.**" Conclusion: "capabilities provide much better support for least-privilege operation and for avoiding confused deputy problems." On POSIX specifically: it "fits the capabilities-as-rows model … However, there is one very important difference: the set of resources (POSIX capability flags) is finite and fixed. At the level of detail that we typically care about (individual files, programs, and so on) resources are created and destroyed all the time. There is a bounded set of POSIX capability flags only because they do not express authorities [at that granularity]."
- **Relevance:** This is the single most precise anchor available for the authority gap, and it names the mechanism: in ACL/ambient-authority systems, **designation** (which thing you mean) and **authority** (whether you may act on it) are two separate questions answered by two separate mechanisms, and the gap between them is not an implementation defect but a structural property of the model. Miller's property table makes this measurable — the *Confused Deputy* row is scored "danger" for Models 1 and 2 (including POSIX capabilities) and "best" for Model 4 (object capabilities); *Least Privilege* is scored "infeasible" for Model 1 and Model 2/POSIX. That is the base document's §5 claim ("capability surface vs authorization surface") stated as an evaluated formal property, with POSIX capabilities landing on the *wrong* side. For a coding agent, this is the exact structure of the gap: the agent designates a path by name (a string), and the ambient authority of its process decides whether the operation succeeds — the two are unconnected, which is why "can edit" and "may edit" come apart.

**33. Hardy, N. (1988). "The Confused Deputy (or why capabilities might have been invented)." *ACM SIGOPS Operating Systems Review* 22(4): 36–38, October 1988.**
URL: https://css.csail.mit.edu/6.858/2015/readings/confused-deputy.html
- **Claim (OBS):** A documented production incident at Tymshare, ~1977. Verbatim: "A compiler was installed in a directory called SYSX. A user would use the compiler by saying 'RUN (SYSX)FORT', and could provide the name of a file to receive some optional debugging output. … The billing information file (SYSX)BILL was also stored in SYSX. Some user came to know the name (SYSX)BILL and supplied it to the compiler as the name of the file to receive the debugging information. The compiler passed the name to the operating system in a request to open that file for output. The operating system, observing that the compiler had home files license, let the compiler write debugging information over (SYSX)BILL. **The billing information was lost.**" Diagnosis, verbatim: **"The fundamental problem is that the compiler runs with authority stemming from two sources. (That's why the compiler is a confused deputy.) … The compiler serves two masters and carries some authority from each to perform its respective duties. It has no way to keep them apart. When it produces statistics it intends to use the authority granted by its home files license. When it produces its debugging output it intends to use authority from its invoker. The compiler had no way of expressing these intents!"** On the attempted fix: "The system was modified by providing a new system call to *switch hats* which could be used to select one of its two authorities. **Note the increase in complexity!** … It soon became clear, however, that more than two 'authorities' were necessary for some of our applications. … Generalizations were not obvious and the modifications to the system were not localized." On rule accretion: "The rules were suffering from the law that complex things become more complex. Every time we added a clause enabling the opening of a file in a categorical situation we would introduce security problems in programs that had been secure. … The last time that I wrote down the requirements for a program to open a file, it required fourteen boolean operators ('and's & 'or's)!" And: "When the code was written to produce the output it was correct! What happened to make it wrong? The precise answer is that it became wrong when we added home files license to (SYSX)FORT."
- **Relevance:** This is the founding OBS of the whole capability tradition and it is an *incident report*, not a theory — the billing file was actually destroyed. Three features make it the strongest anchor in the slice. (a) The deputy is **not malicious and not buggy**: "When the code was written to produce the output it was correct!" The failure appears only when a second authority source is added elsewhere. The authority gap is thus a property of the composition, invisible at any single component. (b) Hardy names the missing faculty exactly: the deputy "had no way of expressing these intents" — it could not say *which* of its authorities it was acting under. That is a formal statement of the capability/authority conflation in the base document §5. (c) The attempted remedy — a `switch hats` syscall — is documented as *increasing complexity* without generalizing, and the rule-set accreted to "fourteen boolean operators." This is the CODE-domain evidence that patching an authority gap by adding conditions compounds rather than resolves, which ties Pattern 3 back to Pattern 2. Hardy's closing exercise is worth carrying verbatim: "(Exercise for the reader: Show that access lists do not solve this problem.)"

**34. Lampson, B. W. (1973). "A Note on the Confinement Problem." *Communications of the ACM* 16(10): 613–615, doi:10.1145/362375.362389. (Received July 1972; revised January 1973.)**
URL: https://www.cs.cornell.edu/andru/cs711/2003fa/reading/lampson73note.pdf
- **Claim (OBS → DER):** Verbatim: "This note explores the problem of confining a program during its execution so that it cannot transmit information to any other program except its caller." Lampson enumerates six concrete leakage channels and classifies them into three categories: *storage* channels, *legitimate* channels (e.g. the bill), and *covert* channels "not intended for information transfer at all, such as the service program's effect on the system load." His rules, verbatim: "*Total isolation*: A confined program shall make no calls on any other program" — which he immediately rejects: "Unfortunately this rule is quite impractical." Then "*Transitivity*: If a confined program calls another program which is not trusted, the called program must also be confined." Then "*Masking*: A program to be confined must allow its caller to determine all its inputs into legitimate and covert channels." Then "*Enforcement*: The supervisor must ensure that a confined program's input to covert channels conforms to the caller's specifications." On cost, verbatim: "The cost of enforcement may be high. A cheaper alternative (if the customer is willing to accept some amount of leakage) is to bound the capacity of the covert channels." And the key structural observation: "**being confined is not an intrinsic property of a program but a constraint which can be imposed on it when it runs.**" Also: "There is not likely to be any rigorous way of identifying every channel in any system of even moderate complexity."
- **Relevance:** Lampson establishes the two facts that make the authority gap intractable rather than merely unimplemented. First, **confinement is extrinsic** — it is a property of the run, not of the code — so nothing about an agent's own behaviour reveals whether it is confined; the constraint must be imposed from outside, by something the agent does not control. That is the formal content of "Can edit ≠ May edit." Second, **total isolation is impractical and transitivity is required**, so any useful agent necessarily calls out to things it does not fully control, and its confinement depends on the confinement of everything downstream. Lampson's honesty about covert channels and about the impossibility of enumerating all channels is directly transferable: an agent constrained to a stated tool set still leaks through timing, resource use, error messages, and side effects on shared state. His "bound the capacity" fallback is the pre-existing formal justification for bounding an agent's action budget rather than trying to eliminate leakage.

**39. Austin, J. L. (1962). *How to Do Things with Words*. Oxford: Clarendon Press / Cambridge MA: Harvard University Press. (William James Lectures, Harvard, 1955; 2nd ed. 1975, eds. J. O. Urmson & M. Sbisà.)**
URL: https://silverbronzo.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/austin-how-to-do-things-with-words-1962.pdf
- **Claim (DER):** Performative utterances are not true or false but **felicitous or infelicitous**. Six conditions, in two classes. Class A (**misfires** — "the act purported but void"): **A.1** "there must exist an accepted conventional procedure having a certain conventional effect, that procedure to include the uttering of certain words by certain persons in certain circumstances"; **A.2** "the particular persons and circumstances in a given case must be appropriate for the invocation of the particular procedure invoked"; B.1 and B.2 are **misexecutions** ("the procedure is all right, and it does apply all right, but we muff the execution of the ritual"). Class Γ (**abuses** — "act professed but hollow"): Γ.1 **insincerities**, Γ.2. Verbatim on the A/Γ distinction: "in both of the cases labelled A there is misinvocation of a procedure — either because there is, speaking vaguely, no such procedure, or because the procedure in question cannot be made to apply in the way attempted. Hence infelicities of this kind A may be called Misinvocations. … By contrast with the A cases, the notion of the B cases is rather that the procedure is all right, and it does apply all right, but we muff the execution of the ritual with more or less dire consequences." On voidness, verbatim: "This does not mean, of course, to say that we won't have done anything: lots of things will have been done — we shall most interestingly have committed the act of bigamy — but we shall not have done the purported act, viz. marrying. … Further, 'without effect' does not here mean 'without consequences, results, effects'." On A.1 failure: "If somebody issues a performative utterance, and the utterance is classed as a misfire because the procedure invoked is not accepted, it is presumably persons other than the speaker who do not accept it." Example: "'I divorce you', said to a wife by her husband in a Christian country … he has not (successfully) divorced her: we admit only some other verbal or non-verbal procedure." And on A.2: "we might have the wrong name and the wrong cleric — that is, someone entitled to name babies but not intended to name this one."
- **Relevance:** **This is the authority gap as a theory of language, published in 1962.** Austin's condition A.2 — "the particular persons … must be appropriate for the invocation of the particular procedure invoked" — *is* an authority condition: the utterance is well-formed, the words are right, the procedure exists, and it still fails because the speaker lacks standing. Three features make it a precise rather than merely suggestive anchor. (a) The failure mode is **misfire, not falsity**: "the act purported but void." An unauthorized agent action is not a *false* action; it is a void one — which is why the base document's §5 framing needs a category other than correctness. (b) Austin's clarification that void ≠ inconsequential is the exact shape of the coding-agent problem: "lots of things will have been done — we shall most interestingly have committed the act of bigamy — but we shall not have done the purported act." An agent that writes files, runs migrations, and pushes commits without authority has produced real effects while performing no authorized act. The damage and the voidness are simultaneous. (c) Acceptance is located **outside the speaker**: "it is presumably persons other than the speaker who do not accept it." Authority is not self-certifiable in Austin's framework — which is the SYMBOL-domain reason an agent's own confidence is not evidence of its standing, and the reason the base document §5's "weaker trusted model supervising a stronger untrusted model" is a felicity condition rather than a redundancy. Note the limits of the mapping honestly: Austin is describing conventional human institutions, and whether machine acts admit of felicity at all is an open interpretive question, not something Austin settles.

**40. Searle, J. R. (1969). *Speech Acts: An Essay in the Philosophy of Language*. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press.**
URL: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/speech-acts/ · https://books.google.com/books/about/Speech_Acts.html?id=t3_WhfknvF0C
- **Claim (INT/DER):** Speech acts are governed by **constitutive rules** of the form **"X counts as Y in context C"** (1969, pp. 33–34), as against regulative rules which "regulate pre-existing or antecedently-existing forms of behaviour." Constitutive rules "create the possibility of new forms of behavior"; behaviour governed by them constitutes **institutional facts**, as against **brute facts**. Each illocutionary act has an illocutionary point, a direction of fit, conditions of satisfaction, and a sincerity condition. For **directives** (orders, requests), verbatim from Searle's analysis: "The preparatory conditions include that **the speaker should be in a position of authority over the hearer**, the sincerity condition is that the speaker wants the ordered act done, and the essential condition has to do with the fact that the speaker intends the utterance as an attempt to get the hearer to do the act." For **assertives**: "the preparatory conditions include the fact that the hearer must have some basis for supposing the asserted proposition is true, the sincerity condition is that he must believe it to be true." Assertions commit: Searle's assertive illocutionary point is "to commit the hearer to the truth of the proposition" (as rendered in the secondary literature; Searle's own formulation is to commit the *speaker*). With Vanderveken (1985) Searle defines **illocutionary commitment**: "an illocutionary act S₁ commits a speaker to another illocutionary act S₂ iff it is not possible to perform S₁ without performing S₂."
- **Relevance:** Searle converts Austin's felicity conditions into a rule structure, and in doing so makes authority an explicit **preparatory condition** of directive acts: you must be in a position of authority over the hearer *before* the order can be a successful order. That is the cleanest SYMBOL-domain statement of "May ≠ Can": the authority condition is a precondition of the act's existence, not a constraint on its execution. The constitutive-rule schema "X counts as Y in context C" is also directly useful for synthesis, because it locates the missing element: for an agent's action to count as an authorized act, there must be a *context C* in which the counting-as holds, and the agent does not control C. Searle's **institutional vs brute fact** distinction is the same cut — writing bytes to a file is a brute fact; committing a change under a mandate is an institutional fact requiring constitutive rules. Note that Searle's commitment analysis (with Vanderveken) is also a Pattern 1 anchor: performing one illocutionary act *commits* you to others whether or not you intend it, which is the deontic version of presupposition projection.

- **Relevance:** This is the substituted anchor for the phantom "Cogito/Lucy semantic gap" citation, and it is a better one: Harnad is a peer-reviewed *Physica D* paper that states the conceptual/symbolic gap formally rather than as a project report. Two claims transfer directly. (a) **"Parasitic on the meanings in our heads"** is the exact status of an LLM's symbol use — its interpretation is not intrinsic but borrowed from the humans who read its output. An agent that emits an identifier whose referent is absent is producing a token that is meaningful *to us* and ungrounded *for it*, which is why the agent shows no local signal of the error. (b) The **Dictionary-Go-Round** is the formal description of what happens when an agent resolves a referent by consulting only other symbols — documentation, type signatures, other identifiers — with no path to the actual object. It "never com[es] to a halt on what anything meant." Harnad's remedy is also transferable as a diagnosis: grounding requires an iconic/categorical path to the world, i.e. *observation* of the actual artifact, which is precisely what the base document's §1 evidence-conditioned execution reintroduces. Note Harnad's companion piece, "The Frame Problem as a Symptom of the Symbol Grounding Problem" (southampton.ac.uk/~harnad), which connects the grounding failure to the frame problem — relevant to an agent that cannot tell which parts of the repository are affected by a change.

## What these two domains contribute that AI-only literature misses

Three sentences, as specified.

The CODE domain supplies **dated, quantified, pre-LLM evidence that these are structural properties of software rather than emergent properties of models**: Brooks (1986) and Boehm & Basili (1981/2001) make the cost of early commitment a measured curve rather than a disposition, Lehman (1974/1980) makes compounding complexity an empirical law with a stated asymmetry, and Hardy (1988) reports an actual destroyed billing file caused by a deputy that was correct, non-malicious, and had "no way of expressing these intents" — which removes the option of explaining the authority gap as model misalignment, since it is documented in a 1977 timesharing compiler with no learned component at all. The SYMBOL domain supplies what none of the AI literature has: a **formal semantics of commitment and authority themselves** — Stalnaker's monotone context-set reduction and defective contexts, Lewis's accommodation rule and score drift, Brandom's separation of commitment from entitlement, Austin's misfire/void-with-real-consequences analysis, and Thomason's documentation that authority can be acquired by acting as if one has it when no one objects — so that "premature commitment" and "capability without authority" become nameable states in a rule system with stated failure modes, rather than behavioural tendencies inferred from benchmarks. Together they also supply the two **impossibility results and limits** that discipline any remedy: Rice's theorem and inherent ambiguity mean the predicates an agent needs before acting are in general undecidable and some inputs admit no unique parse, while Harnad's grounding argument and the 2026 Faithfulness-Gap measurement (typecheck catches 41.2% of semantic drift) mean that neither local consistency nor well-formedness can ever certify that a symbol has its referent — so the choice is never between committing and not committing, but between committing on evidence and committing on fluency.
