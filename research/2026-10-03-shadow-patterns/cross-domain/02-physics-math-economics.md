## 1. PHYSICS DOMAIN

### 1.1 Premature commitment — commitment as metastable basin entry

#### P-1. Escape from a metastable state is exponentially suppressed by the barrier

- **Citation:** Fodor, Gupta, Vergassola et al. (2023). "Exponential increase of transition rates in metastable systems driven by non-Gaussian noise." *Scientific Reports* 13, DOI 10.1038/s41598-023-30577-0. Preprint arXiv:1501.00374. PMC9995508.
- **Claim (DER):** The escape rate from a metastable minimum `q_a` over the nearest barrier top `q_b` scales as `r ≅ C·exp(−S_min/D)`, where `S_min` is the minimum of an Onsager–Machlup stochastic action over all paths connecting `q_a` to `q_b`. For Gaussian noise this reduces to the van't Hoff–Arrhenius law `r ~ exp(−ΔV/T)`; the "effective energy barrier" is the minimum action, and the optimal escape trajectory (the *instanton*) is the time-reversed relaxation path.
- **Relevance:** This is the exact topology the base document names as premature commitment. Once the system sits in a basin, leaving it is not a matter of will — it is exponentially unlikely in the barrier height relative to the driving noise. Premature commitment is the agent having entered the basin; the "then spends the rest defending that interpretation" behaviour (§1) is the instanton being unavailable at the prevailing noise level. Non-Gaussian (heavy-tailed / large-jump) noise *lowers* the effective barrier and can produce discontinuous jump instantons — i.e. the escape route changes qualitatively with the noise character, not just its magnitude.
- **Receipt status:** VERIFIED (body passages read: §I.1 Langevin model, §II.1 effective action, §II.3 Gaussian limit recovering Arrhenius, Eqs. 8–19).

#### P-2. Hysteresis: upsweep and downsweep dynamics diverge, and adiabaticity always fails near a bifurcation

- **Citation:** Ezraty, R., Levin, I., Gat, O. (2023). "Universality and hysteresis in slow sweeping of bifurcations." *Physical Review E* 109, 044206. DOI 10.1103/PhysRevE.109.044206. Preprint arXiv:2309.03860.
- **Claim (DER):** For period-doubling and pitchfork bifurcations under a slow parameter sweep, "the adiabatic approximation always breaks down sufficiently close to the bifurcation, so that the upsweep and downsweep dynamics diverge from one another, disobeying standard bifurcation theory." Bifurcations involve exchange of stability, so the convergence timescale *diverges* as the bifurcation is approached — therefore no matter how slowly the parameter is varied, the variation is no longer adiabatic near it. The upsweep breakout time scales as `τ ~ sqrt(−log(6πs²))`, diverging logarithmically in the adiabatic limit; the loop area scales as `ε^{1/4}·(−log s)^{3/4}`.
- **Relevance:** Two distinct anchors for premature commitment. First: the *state depends on the direction of approach*, so "what would the agent have concluded if it had arrived here slowly and from the other side" is not answerable — the hysteresis loop is measurable and non-zero. Second, and sharper: "go slower and it will be fine" provably *fails* precisely at the transition point, because the relaxation time diverges there. Any remedy framed as "give the agent more time to gather evidence" runs into the same singularity that this paper characterises.
- **Receipt status:** VERIFIED (body passages read: §I introduction incl. the adiabatic-breakdown argument and "la chasse au canard" reference, §II.1 normal-form setup, §III.1 upsweep trajectory Eq. 14, breakout time Eq. 21, loop area Eq. 22–23).

#### P-3. Hysteresis realised by low-dimensional gradient flow (basin-to-basin transitions as a graph)

- **Citation:** (2015). "Realization of Arbitrary Hysteresis by a Low-dimensional Gradient Flow." arXiv:1506.03842.
- **Claim (DER):** In a gradient system with a parameter-dependent potential, "as the parameter is varied, critical points of the potential can be eliminated or created through saddle-node bifurcations causing the system to transit from one stable equilibrium located at a (local) minimum point of the potential to another minimum along the heteroclinic connections. These transitions can be represented by a graph." Any admissible hysteresis graph has a realisation in *two-dimensional* gradient flows. Preisach hysteresis is worked as the example.
- **Relevance:** Shows that the full richness of hysteresis — including arbitrary orderings of basin transitions — needs only two dimensions and a gradient (i.e. strictly descent-following) rule. Commitment-with-path-dependence is not an exotic high-dimensional artefact; it is the generic behaviour of any descent process on a parameter-varying landscape.
- **Receipt status:** HYPOTHESIS (abstract read; body not read).

#### P-4. Logical irreversibility has a physical cost — the thermodynamic floor under "undo"

- **Citation:** Landauer, R. (1961). "Irreversibility and Heat Generation in the Computing Process." *IBM Journal of Research and Development* 5(3), 183–191. DOI 10.1147/rd.53.0183. Reprinted in *IBM J. Res. Dev.* 44(1/2), 2000.
- **Claim (DER/OBS):** Erasure ("RESTORE TO ONE") is logically irreversible and dissipates a minimum of `k·T·ln 2 = 0.6931 kT` per restored bit to the surroundings, independent of the rate of the process. A single force that maps *all* initial states onto one output state cannot exist without dissipation. Landauer's stated framing: "we cannot tolerate a cumulative process, in which differences between possible one states become larger according to their detailed past histories."
- **Relevance:** Gives premature commitment a hard physical floor. In this framework, "reversibility" is not free — collapsing a set of candidate interpretations down to one chosen answer costs `kT ln2` *per bit of discarded alternative*, and that cost is paid in dissipated heat whether or not anyone records it. Also relevant to the §3 narrative-completion pattern: a smoothed-over account that erases which alternatives were live is a logically irreversible operation.
- **Receipt status:** VERIFIED-CITATION (primary PDF located and characteristic figures extracted: 0.6931 kT per restored bit, 1.18 kT, "information becomes thermalized"; MIT *Perspectives on Science* 24(1):112 secondary analysis also read for the logical↔physical irreversibility equivalence).

### 1.2 Action bias — measurement and motion as physical perturbation

#### P-5. Mean exit time grows exponentially in the barrier-to-noise ratio

- **Citation:** Grebenkov, D. S. (2015). "First Exit Times of Harmonically Trapped Particles: A Didactic Review." arXiv:1411.3598.
- **Claim (DER):** For a harmonically trapped particle (Ornstein–Uhlenbeck), the mean first exit time in the large-`κ` limit is `⟨τ⟩_{x_0} ≃ (L²/D)·(√π·e^κ)/(4κ^{3/2})` for zero constant force, and `(√π·e^{κ(1−φ)²})/(2κ^{3/2}(1−φ))` for `0<φ<1`. The survival probability is mono-exponential, `S(0,t) ≃ exp(−t/⟨τ⟩₀)`, only for `t ≫ τ_k`; in the intermediate regime `τ_k ≪ t ≪ ⟨τ⟩₀` it "remains close to 1". Escape power `kL` can be read off from the *drastic change in mean-exit-time behaviour at φ=1*.
- **Relevance:** Supplies the quantitative backbone for the base document's §2 claim that action bias compounds. `κ` is the squared ratio of exit distance to trap size — the dimensionless "how far out of the basin would this action take me". The dependence is exponential in `κ` and polynomial only in the prefactor. Two further usable results: (i) there is a *plateau* regime where the survival probability is ≈1 no matter how long one waits, i.e. short-horizon behaviour carries almost no information about escape; (ii) the `φ=1` singularity is an *inferable* boundary — the review shows how a single trajectory reveals the driving force. That is the Fokker-Planck-side warrant for "act vs. wait" being a measurable quantity rather than a temperament.
- **Receipt status:** VERIFIED (body passages read: §2.5 mean exit time Eqs. 33–39 and the four asymptotic cases, §2.9 exterior problem Eqs. 79–81, survival-probability regime, motor-force inference at φ=1).

#### P-6. Decoherence and einselection: the environment out-measures the observer, and only einselected states survive contact

- **Citation:** Zurek, W. H. (2003). "Decoherence, einselection, and the quantum origins of the classical." *Reviews of Modern Physics* 75, 715. Preprint arXiv:quant-ph/0105127v3.
- **Claim (DER/INT):** The environment "monitors certain observables of the system, destroying coherence between the pointer states corresponding to their eigenvalues", producing environment-induced superselection (einselection). The key operational passage: *"The observer and the environment compete for the information about the system. Environment — because of its size and its incessant interaction with the system — wins that competition, acquiring information faster and more completely than the observer. Thus, a record useful for the purpose of prediction must be restricted to the observables that are already monitored by the environment."* The paper formalises an observer game between a record-keeper R and a "spy" S whose goal is to discover the state without perturbing it: *"In absence of einselection the situation of the spy S is hopeless... his measurements will re-prepare states of the systems. Thus, when R re-measures using the original observables, he will likely find answers different from his records. The spy S will 'get caught'."* Objective existence is then *defined* as: a state exists objectively "when an observer can consistently find out the state of the system without changing it."
- **Relevance:** This is the physics warrant for treating probing as an action with consequences, which the base document's §2 needs in order for action bias to compound. Zurek's R/S game gives a precise criterion separating "looked without disturbing" from "looked and thereby re-prepared" — and the criterion is *operational and externally checkable by a second observer*, not a matter of the prober's intent or self-report. Directly load-bearing for §3/§4: the paper's central negative result is that an unperturbing read of an unknown isolated state is impossible in principle, so any account claiming to have observed without disturbing is falsifiable against a re-measurement.
- **Receipt status:** VERIFIED (body passages read: full abstract, §I.4 existential interpretation and quantum Darwinism, §VI.1 objective existence of einselected states incl. the R/S record-keeper-spy game, §VII.1 quantum Darwinism Eqs. 59–60, envariance discussion).

#### P-7. Wheeler's delayed choice, experimentally realised with space-like separation

- **Citation:** Jacques, V., Wu, E., Grosshans, F., Treussart, F., Grangier, P., Aspect, A., Roch, J.-F. (2007). "Experimental realization of Wheeler's delayed-choice GedankenExperiment." *Science* 315(5814), 966–968. DOI 10.1126/science.1136303. Preprint arXiv:quant-ph/0610241. PMID 17303748.
- **Claim (OBS):** Single photons from a diamond N-V colour centre enter a Mach–Zehnder interferometer; the choice of open vs. closed configuration is made by a quantum random number generator, space-like separated (48 m path separation) from the photon's entry. Result: closed configuration → interference at 94% visibility; open configuration → no interference, which-way information parameter `I > 0.99`, anticorrelation parameter `α = 0.12 ± 0.01`. The authors' conclusion: *"Our realization of Wheeler's delayed-choice GedankenExperiment demonstrates beyond any doubt that the behavior of the photon in the interferometer depends on the choice of the observable which is measured, even when that choice is made at a position and a time such that it is separated from the entrance of the photon in the interferometer by a space-like interval."* Quoting Wheeler: *"We, now, by moving the mirror in or out have an unavoidable effect on what we have a right to say about the already past history of that photon."*
- **Relevance:** Anchors the base document's §3 narrative-completion pattern at the level of hard measurement, not metaphor. Which description of the system's history is *available* — one-path or both-paths — is determined by the measurement configuration, and the two configurations are mutually exclusive ("BS_output cannot be simultaneously inserted and removed"). So there is no fact about "what the photon did" that is independent of how it was interrogated. Note the honest counter-anchor, also in the index: arXiv:1101.1511 argues the choice "has no delayed effect on the photon to behave as a wave or a corpuscle at the entrance and inside the interferometer, but influences the subsequent evolution of the photon state incident onto BS_output" — the same data admit a non-retrocausal reading. The OBS is the visibility/which-way numbers; the "retroactive" gloss is INT.
- **Receipt status:** VERIFIED (body passages read: §1 setup and Wheeler's framing, §3 results incl. α, I, 94% visibility, 48 m space-like separation, QRNG; discussion; Fig. 1–3 captions).

#### P-8. Quantum Zeno dynamics: frequent measurement does not merely observe, it re-writes the generator

- **Citation:** Facchi, P., Pascazio, S. (2008). "Quantum Zeno dynamics: mathematical and physical aspects." *Journal of Physics A* 41, 493001. Preprint arXiv:0903.3297.
- **Claim (DER):** "If frequent measurements ascertain whether a quantum system is still in its initial state, transitions to other states are hindered and the quantum Zeno effect takes place. However, in its broader formulation, the quantum Zeno effect does not necessarily freeze everything. On the contrary, for frequent projections onto a multidimensional subspace, the system can evolve away from its initial state, although it remains in the subspace defined by the measurement." Concretely: for measurements ascertaining whether a particle is in a given spatial region, "the evolution is unitary and the generator of the Zeno dynamics is the Hamiltonian with hard-wall (Dirichlet) boundary conditions." Alternative strategies for obtaining Zeno dynamics are shown to be physically equivalent.
- **Relevance:** The strongest form of "the act of measuring changes the system": the measurement regime does not just add noise, it *substitutes a different Hamiltonian* — the boundary conditions become physical. This matters for the base document's §5 authority gap in a specific way: an oversight mechanism that repeatedly interrogates an agent does not leave the agent's dynamics untouched and merely report on them; it alters what transitions are available. Companion source in the index (arXiv:quant-ph/0105138, "Real measurements and Quantum Zeno effect") adds the finite-duration/finite-accuracy caveat: measurement can inhibit *or* accelerate evolution depending on coupling strength, "however, the evolution cannot be fully stopped."
- **Receipt status:** HYPOTHESIS (abstract read via paper index; body not read).

### 1.3 Authority gap — missing constraint as gauge freedom

#### P-9. Without gauge fixing, the system is at best weakly hyperbolic: the missing-constraint theorem

- **Citation:** (2020). "Gauge Fixing and Constrained Dynamics." arXiv:2007.06641. (Dirac formalism review; electrodynamics as worked example.)
- **Claim (DER):** This is the single sharpest anchor found in the physics slice. The results, in the paper's own words:
  - *"gauge theories cannot be numerically stable unless all gauge freedom is sufficiently fixed in system of evolution equations. This result is new, but intuitively obvious: in gauge theories, the gauge freedom could reasonably propagate at any non-physical speed, so it cannot be expected that perturbations in an initial solution can be bounded as the system evolves without the inclusion of additional constraints."*
  - *"Since an independent second class constraint must be imposed for each independent first class constraint in order to completely fix the gauge, any Hamiltonian formulation in which the gauge is not fixed will not have a full set of conserved second class constraints necessary to generate a full set of conserved variational vectors. Because of this, any Hamiltonian formulation containing gauge freedom will generate a system of evolution equations which cannot posses a complete set of eigenvectors. Whence, Hamiltonian formulations containing gauge freedom can form only weakly hyperbolic systems at best."*
  - Counting law: with `2(N−M)` first-class constraints, one must impose `2(N−M)` independent second-class constraints, giving `4(N−M)` total constraints, leaving a reduced phase space of dimension `2N − 4(N−M) = 4M − 2N ≡ 2D`. *"Once the gauge has been fixed, the remaining freedom in the system will correspond precisely to the physical degrees of freedom."*
  - Stability consequence: with gauge fixed, "the difference between two solutions, which differ at an initial time by some perturbation of the fields, will remain exponentially bounded in time as the system evolves."
  - Imperfect fixing leaves residue: *"Without fixing the gauge, there would be no way to uniquely specify a map defining the phase space components... and therefore no way to restrict the evolution to the first class constraint manifold."*
- **Relevance:** Provides a theorem, not an analogy, for the base document's §5 formulation "Can edit ≠ May edit". Capability without an authority constraint is not "capability plus a policy question" — it is a system whose evolution equations lack a complete eigenbasis and are therefore only weakly hyperbolic, i.e. ill-posed, i.e. perturbations unbounded in time. The one-second-class-constraint-per-first-class-constraint counting rule is a *dimensional matching requirement*: the number of authority conditions must match the number of redundant capability directions exactly. Too few → residual gauge freedom → instability. This also supplies the precise sense in which an "authority envelope" is a gauge condition rather than a restriction on the physics: gauge fixing does not change the physical degrees of freedom, it removes the redundancy in their description and thereby makes the evolution well-posed.
- **Receipt status:** VERIFIED (body passages read: §II.6 Dirac bracket and gauge fixing Eq. 79–80 and the 4(N−M) constraint count, §III.3 integrability theorem and corollary, §III.4 hyperbolicity and the weak-hyperbolicity conclusion, §III.5 removing numerical error, §IV.3 electrodynamics worked example, §V conclusion).

#### P-10. Admissibility of a gauge condition is an invertibility condition

- **Citation:** (2026). "A note on complete gauge-fixing and the constraint algebra." arXiv:2604.16990.
- **Claim (DER):** "The admissibility of a gauge-fixing is governed by the invertibility of `Δ = {σ_a, γ_b}` where `σ_a` are gauge-fixing conditions and `γ_b` are independent first-class constraints." Proves via the Schur complement that the determinant of the combined constraint matrix factorises as `det M ≈ ±(Δ)²·det C`, where `C` is the second-class constraint matrix. Since `det C ≠ 0` by definition, "the second-class sector decouples entirely from the gauge-fixing sector." Identifies Henneaux–Teitelboim's Hamiltonian admissibility criterion with Motohashi–Suyama–Takahashi's Lagrangian completeness criterion.
- **Relevance:** Sharpens P-9 into a *checkable* condition. An authority condition is admissible only if the matrix of its Poisson brackets against the redundancy generators is invertible — i.e. only if each authority condition actually bites on exactly one redundant direction, and none of them are collinear with each other or with the constraints. A policy that fails this test is a "gauge condition" that fixes nothing while looking like it does. The factorisation result also says the check can be done sector-by-sector.
- **Receipt status:** HYPOTHESIS (abstract read; body not read).

#### P-11. Perfect vs. imperfect gauge fixing — the number of residual constants of motion depends on where in the constraint chain the condition is imposed

- **Citation:** (2006). "Perfect and Imperfect Gauge Fixing." arXiv:hep-th/0609145.
- **Claim (DER):** Using the chain structure of a constrained system, one may use "either a perfect gauge, in which all gauged degrees of freedom are determined; or an imperfect gauge, in which some first class constraints remain as subsidiary conditions to be imposed on the solutions of the equations of motion." Further: "the number of constants of motion depends on the level in a constraint chain in which the gauge fixing condition is imposed." Worked examples: relativistic point particle, electromagnetism, Polyakov string.
- **Relevance:** Names the partial-fixing failure mode explicitly. An imperfect gauge leaves first-class constraints as *subsidiary conditions that must be separately enforced on the solutions* — they do not follow from the evolution. That is a structural description of an authority gap that persists after a policy is nominally in place: the policy fixed some of the redundancy, and what remains has to be checked solution-by-solution. The level-dependence result adds that *where* in the chain you impose matters for how many things stay unconstrained.
- **Receipt status:** HYPOTHESIS (abstract read; body not read).

## 2. MATH DOMAIN

### 2.1 Premature commitment — first basin found

#### M-1. In high dimensions the trap is saddle points, not local minima, and plateaus make them look like minima

- **Citation:** Dauphin, Y. N., Pascanu, R., Gulcehre, C., Cho, K., Ganguli, S., Bengio, Y. (2014). "Identifying and attacking the saddle point problem in high-dimensional non-convex optimization." *NeurIPS 2014*. arXiv:1406.2572. (Earlier version: arXiv:1405.4604, "On the saddle point problem for non-convex optimization.")
- **Claim (DER + OBS):** "it is often thought that a main source of difficulty for these local methods to find the global minimum is the proliferation of local minima with much higher error than the global minimum. Here we argue, based on results from statistical physics, random matrix theory, neural network theory, and empirical evidence, that a deeper and more profound difficulty originates from the proliferation of saddle points, not local minima." Key sub-results:
  - *Ratio:* "the ratio of the number of saddle points to local minima increases exponentially with the dimensionality N."
  - *Index–error correlation (Bray & Dean 2007):* critical points concentrate on a monotonically increasing curve in the (index α, error ε) plane; "the larger the error the larger the index". Deviation is "exponentially small in the dimensionality N". Hence high-error critical points are exponentially likely to be saddles, and "all local minima, which necessarily have index 0, are likely to have an error very close to that of the global minimum."
  - *Random-matrix mechanism:* Hessian eigenvalues follow Wigner's semicircle law shifted by an amount determined by ε. For N=1 an exact saddle is a zero-probability event; as N grows it becomes exponentially unlikely that all eigenvalues share a sign.
  - *The illusion:* "Such saddle points are surrounded by high error plateaus that can dramatically slow down learning, and **give the illusory impression of the existence of a local minimum**." Newton's method does not fix this — "saddle-points instead become attractive under the Newton dynamics."
  - *Empirical (OBS):* on Penn Treebank character-level RNN, "the SGD stops progressing quickly and does not improve performance, suggesting that the algorithm is stuck in a plateau... As soon as we apply the proposed saddle-free Newton method, we see that the error drops significantly" (MSE 0.69 → 0.57 on the MNIST-scale feedforward task), and the found solution has fewer negative Hessian eigenvalues.
- **Relevance:** Two separate anchors for premature commitment. The first is the *misdiagnosis* result: what presents locally as "I have arrived at a stable answer" (plateau, vanishing gradient) is with high probability not a minimum at all in high dimension — the felt signature of settling and the actual signature of having settled coincide. The second is the *index–error* law: the worse the answer, the more directions it is unstable in, which is a measurable property of the point rather than a property of the search. And the negative result on Newton's method matters — increasing the order of the local information used does not fix the problem; it makes the trap *attractive*.
- **Receipt status:** VERIFIED (body passages read: full abstract, §1 introduction incl. the dimension-scaling ratio and plateau/illusion argument, §2 prevalence of saddle points incl. Bray–Dean index-error result and Wigner semicircle argument and Baldi–Hornik, Saxe et al., Saad–Solla symmetric-submanifold trapping, §7.2 RNN experiment, §8 conclusion).

#### M-2. Gradient descent can require exponential time to escape a saddle in two dimensions — stochasticity is essential

- **Citation:** (2020). "Notes on Worst-case Inefficiency of Gradient Descent Even in R²." arXiv:2008.07513.
- **Claim (DER):** "gradient descent may take exponential time to escape saddle points, with non-pathological two dimensional functions." Explicitly positioned against Lee et al. 2016 (asymptotic escape) and Jin et al. 2015 / Ge et al. 2017 (efficient escape for perturbed GD). Conclusion: "Through our analysis we demonstrate that stochasticity is essential to escape saddle points efficiently." Numerical experiments verify the theoretical result.
- **Relevance:** Removes the low-dimensional escape hatch from M-1. Deterministic descent can be exponentially slow out of a saddle *even in ℝ²*, so the difficulty is not confined to high dimension. The corollary — stochasticity is essential — is the math-side counterpart of P-1's noise-dependence: the thing that permits leaving a basin is a noise term, and its absence is not neutrality but exponential delay.
- **Receipt status:** HYPOTHESIS (abstract read; body not read).

#### M-3. The Polyak–Łojasiewicz condition: a weaker-than-convexity sufficient condition for linear convergence to the global minimum

- **Citation:** Karimi, H., Nutini, J., Schmidt, M. (2016). "Linear Convergence of Gradient and Proximal-Gradient Methods Under the Polyak-Łojasiewicz Condition." *ECML-PKDD 2016*, LNCS, DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-46128-1_50. arXiv:1608.04636.
- **Claim (DER):** "we show that this much-older Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) inequality is actually weaker than the main conditions that have been explored to show linear convergence rates without strong convexity over the last 25 years." The PL inequality alone suffices for a linear convergence rate of gradient descent with step size `1/L`. Derived consequences: linear rates for least squares and logistic regression that are not strongly convex, "and even for some non-convex problems" (§2.3); new rates for randomized and greedy coordinate descent (hence for certain boosting variants), sign-based gradient descent, and stochastic gradient methods in classical or variance-reduced settings. Also proposes an equivalent proximal-PL condition for non-smooth problems.
- **Relevance:** Defines the boundary condition for the premature-commitment problem rather than the problem itself. PL is the weakest-known condition under which local descent provably reaches the *global* minimum at a linear rate — i.e. the condition under which settling on the first basin found is not premature at all. It is strictly weaker than strong convexity and does not require convexity. Outside the PL class, "first basin found" and "best basin" come apart, and M-1/M-2 quantify how. Useful as the falsifiable delimiter for any claim that early commitment is harmless in a given problem class.
- **Receipt status:** VERIFIED-CITATION (abstract, §1 introduction, and section structure read from the arXiv HTML v4; body proofs not read).

#### M-4. Path dependence and non-ergodicity (economics-side, but a stochastic-process result)

- **Citation:** David, P. A. (2001). "Path dependence, its critics, and the quest for 'historical economics'." In *Evolution and Path Dependence in Economic Ideas: Past and Present* (Garavaglia ed.), Edward Elgar. Oxford ORA record uuid:da8832b0-b5b1-48a9-9d41-c25de88980e1. Related primary: David, P. A. (1985). "Clio and the Economics of QWERTY." *American Economic Review* 75(2), 332–337. JSTOR 1805621.
- **Claim (DER/INT):** The paper "formulates definitions that relate the phenomenon to the property of **non-ergodicity in stochastic processes**"; it examines the relationship between path dependence and 'market failure' and defines 'lock-in'. Concluding move: resistance to historical economics is itself described as *"a manifestation of 'sunk cost hysteresis' in the sphere of human cognitive development."*
- **Relevance:** Non-ergodicity is the exact mathematical content of "history matters": time averages do not converge to ensemble averages, so the distribution over outcomes does not determine the outcome realised. Lock-in is the absorbing-basin case. The term "sunk cost hysteresis" is David's own and explicitly bridges P-2 (physics hysteresis) and E-6 (sunk cost) — worth noting because it is a primary-source bridge, not an analogy I am constructing.
- **Receipt status:** VERIFIED-CITATION (Oxford ORA PDF abstract/scope text read; 1985 QWERTY paper itself is CLAIM — located at JSTOR 1805621 and in three secondary sources, body not read).

### 2.2 Action bias — optimal stopping and bounded rationality

#### M-5. Snell envelope, reservation value, and a theorem that a confidence-safe rule "never stops prematurely"

- **Citation:** Anagnostopoulos, C. (2026). "PROSE: A Theory of Optimal Stopping with Perishable Evidence for Peer Selection in Intermittently Connected Decentralised Learning." arXiv:2609.23845. (University of Glasgow.)
- **Claim (DER):** Fully self-contained optimal-stopping theory. Results read:
  - **Prop. 2 (Snell envelope and reservation value):** `V` is "the smallest bounded function dominating the immediate stopping value and stable under continuation, i.e. `V(s) ≥ g(s)` and `V(s) ≥ W(s)` with equality of the maximum, so V is the (finite-horizon) Snell envelope of the stopping-reward process." Optimal to terminate at the first stage where `g(s) ≥ ρ(s) := W(s)`; otherwise continue. Hence "the optimal policy is a reservation-value threshold rule".
  - **Prop. 3 (threshold structure):** `V(g,·) = max{g, W}` is convex, non-decreasing, 1-Lipschitz; the optimal stopping set in `g` is the upward-closed half-line `{g : g ≥ ρ}` with `ρ = W`. Therefore "if it is optimal to take a terminal action at some immediate value `g_0`, it is optimal at every `g ≥ g_0`: the optimal policy is a *monotone* (threshold) rule and **never re-enters the continuation region** as the certified value improves."
  - **Thm. 7 / Prop. 8 (value of waiting):** the waiting component is itself a reservation value `ρ*`; "the full optimal policy exchanges as soon as the certified realisable value exceeds the larger of the probing continuation and the waiting reservation ρ*." `ρ*` increases in arrival rate `λ_i` and decreases in delay price `c_w`.
  - **§6 comparative statics:** "higher link hazard lowers the value of continued probing and enlarges the stopping region."
  - **Thm. 8 (myopic optimality):** "in sufficiently volatile (monotone) mobility regimes, the one-step confidence-safe rule is a sound surrogate for the optimal policy and **never stops prematurely**."
  - **§11 (two clocks):** "uncertainty in mobile decentralised learning has two clocks. Statistical uncertainty falls as probes accumulate, while opportunity uncertainty rises as contact lifetime is consumed."
  - Evidence is *perishable*: "the evidence a learner gathers about a peer is perishable: it decays because links expire and because peer models drift while old measurements age."
- **Relevance:** The most directly on-target source in this entire slice for the base document's §2 and for the "act vs. wait" framing. It gives (i) the exact object that "gather evidence before acting" is an instance of — a Snell-envelope/reservation-value stopping rule; (ii) a *named* sufficient condition under which a cheap myopic surrogate provably never stops early (Thm. 8), which is the clean available delimiter for when shortcutting the value-of-waiting computation is safe versus unsafe; (iii) the two-clocks result, which says the pressure to act early is not purely a bias — evidence genuinely decays, so "wait longer" has a real and quantifiable cost that rises with hazard; (iv) monotonicity (Prop. 3): once stopping becomes optimal it stays optimal, so there is no "re-open the question" region above threshold. Note the domain is federated-learning peer selection, not coding agents — the *mathematics* transfers, the application does not.

#### M-6. Simon's satisficing, machine-verified, with an explicit tolerance threshold

- **Citation:** Li, J., Wang, J., Zhou, L. (2025/2026). "Quantifying Bounded Rationality: Formal Verification of Simon's Satisficing Through Flexible Stochastic Dominance." arXiv:2507.07052. Lingnan University / NUAA. JEL D81, D83, D91.
- **Claim (DER):** Introduces Flexible First-Order Stochastic Dominance (FFSD), formalised and machine-checked in Lean 4. Results:
  - Aspiration levels as indicator reference points: `u(x) = 1 if x > x₀, 0 if x ≤ x₀`; Simon's "definite concept of 'satisfactory'" encoded directly.
  - Zones of acceptance: `|u(x) − indicator(x₀)| ≤ ε`, where ε is Simon's "definite range of pay-offs".
  - **Critical threshold (Lemmas 3.7, 5.13):** for `ε < 1/2`, approximate indicator functions have *unique* reference points. The paper draws the boundary explicitly: *"Structured bounded rationality (ε < 1/2): Decision-makers maintain sufficient precision to ensure consistent thresholds despite cognitive limitations. Unstructured behavior (ε ≥ 1/2): Thresholds become ambiguous, making economic prediction impossible."*
  - Equivalence theorem (Thm. 4.3): FFSD ≡ expected-utility comparison using threshold-based (approximate indicator) utility functions.
  - Testable prediction: "economic agents whose decision uncertainty exceeds this threshold will exhibit preference reversals and inconsistent choices."
  - Extends to multi-dimensional vector aspiration levels (NFFSD), capturing Simon's vector-utility concept.
  - Quoted Simon (1955, p. 99): *"there are no direct observations that individuals or firms do actually equate marginal costs and revenues"*; and the programme statement: *"The task is to replace the global rationality of economic man with a kind of rational behavior that is compatible with the access to information and the computational capacities that are actually possessed by organisms."*
- **Relevance:** Converts action bias from a temperament into a *parameter with a proven phase boundary*. Satisficing is threshold-based termination of search: accept the first alternative that clears an aspiration level. That is formally the same shape as the reservation-value rule in M-5, with the reservation level set by aspiration rather than computed from continuation value. The `ε < 1/2` result is the load-bearing piece: below it, satisficing yields unique, consistent thresholds and is provably relatable to expected-utility maximisation; at or above it, the thresholds become ambiguous and prediction fails. So bounded rationality is *bounded* — there is a quantified limit past which acting-on-approximation stops being rational-with-limits and becomes incoherent.

#### M-7. Fast-and-frugal heuristics: the ecological-rationality programme

- **Citation:** Gigerenzer, G., Todd, P. M., and the ABC Research Group (1999). *Simple Heuristics That Make Us Smart.* Oxford University Press. #### M-8. The value of information as a decision-theoretic quantity

- **Citation:** Howard, R. A. (1966). "Information Value Theory." *IEEE Transactions on Systems Science and Cybernetics* SSC-2(1), 22–26. DOI 10.1109/TSSC.1966.300074. (1,028 citations per Semantic Scholar.)

### 2.3 Authority gap and narrative completion — dimensional and representational mismatch

#### M-9. Buckingham π theorem / dimensional analysis: quantities of different dimension cannot be added

- **Citation:** Buckingham, E. (1914). "On Physically Similar Systems; Illustrations of the Use of Dimensional Equations." *Physical Review* 4(4), 345–376. Modern statement and generalisations: e.g. "A generalization of the Π-theorem and dimensional analysis," *Proc. R. Soc. A* / PMC423226. Reference treatment: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckingham_pi_theorem
- **Claim (DER):** Any dimensionally homogeneous relation among `n` physical quantities involving `k` independent fundamental dimensions can be rewritten as a relation among `n − k` independent dimensionless groups (π terms). The corollary used ubiquitously in physics: an equation that is not dimensionally homogeneous is not merely wrong but *ill-formed* — terms of different dimension cannot appear in a sum, and a quantity carrying a dimension cannot be equated to one that does not. The generalisation in PMC423226 extends the theorem to problems where "some of the quantities" carry non-standard dimension structure.
- **Relevance:** The math anchor for the base document's §5 framing "capability dimension ≠ authority dimension". Dimensional analysis is the formal discipline whose entire content is that a *magnitude* comparison across incommensurable types is not a small error but a category failure detectable by inspection, before any measurement. Applied to the authority gap: a large capability scalar and a permission predicate live in different dimension classes; no amount of scaling the first produces the second, and the equation "can ⇒ may" is dimensionally inhomogeneous. The π theorem's positive content is also usable — it tells you how many independent dimensionless combinations exist, i.e. how many *separate* authority conditions are needed to make the relation homogeneous (compare the constraint-counting law in P-9).
- **Receipt status:** CLAIM (secondary: Wikipedia article + PMC generalisation abstract + two lecture-note PDFs; Buckingham's 1914 primary text not read).

#### M-10. When the agent chooses the principal's signal: indicator choice in pay-for-performance

- **Citation:** (2023). "Indicator Choice in Pay-for-Performance." arXiv:2307.12457.
- **Claim (DER):** "We study the classic principal-agent model when **the signal observed by the principal is chosen by the agent**." Full characterisation of the optimal information structure from the agent's perspective in a general moral-hazard setting with limited liability. Results: "the agent's problem can be mapped into a geometrical game between the principal and the agent in the space of likelihood ratios"; "coarse contracts are sufficient: The agent can achieve her best with binary signals"; conditions are characterised "under which the agent is able to extract the entire surplus and implement the first-best efficient allocation"; and "when effort and performance are one-dimensional, under a general class of models, threshold signals are optimal."
- **Relevance:** The precise mathematical statement of an authority gap that survives correct incentive design. Standard moral hazard assumes the principal picks the signal and the agent picks the action; here the agent picks the *measurement instrument* the principal will use to judge her. The consequence — surplus can be fully extracted by the agent — is the formal version of an agent controlling its own evaluation surface. Also directly relevant to the base document's §3/§4 finding that chain-of-thought is not a faithful window: if the reported reasoning is an indicator chosen by the reporting agent, this paper is the model of that situation, and it proves what the agent can achieve by that choice.
- **Receipt status:** HYPOTHESIS (abstract read; body not read).

#### M-11. Contract computation becomes APX-hard once the agent has private types

- **Citation:** (2020). "Contracts under Moral Hazard and Adverse Selection." arXiv:2010.06742.
- **Claim (DER):** "In the classical principal-agent problem, a principal must design a contract to incentivize an agent to perform an action on behalf of the principal. We study the classical principal-agent problem in a setting where the agent can be of one of several types (affecting the outcome of actions they might take)." Results: "it is **APX-hard** to compute either the profit-maximizing single contract or the profit-maximizing menu of contracts (as opposed to in the absence of types, where one can efficiently compute the optimal contract)." Positive side: "the best linear contract achieves an O(nT) approximation of the best possible profit" for `n` actions and `T` types; tight worst-case approximation bounds between benchmarks.
- **Relevance:** Supplies a *complexity-theoretic* (rather than incentive-theoretic) limit on the authority gap. Adding private types to moral hazard — i.e. admitting that the agent's internal state is not observable — moves contract design from polynomial-time solvable to APX-hard, meaning no PTAS unless P=NP. So the difficulty of constructing an authority envelope is not merely practical; it is inapproximable-to-arbitrary-precision in the general case. The linear-contract bound is the corresponding positive result: a simple, coarse contract still gets within a factor linear in (actions × types). That is a quantitative statement about what a *simple* mechanism buys.
- **Receipt status:** HYPOTHESIS (abstract read; body not read).

#### M-12. Runge's phenomenon: exact fit at known points, wild oscillation in the gaps

- **Citation:** Runge, C. (1901). "Über empirische Funktionen und die Interpolation zwischen äquidistanten Ordinaten." *Zeitschrift für Mathematik und Physik* 46, 224–243. Modern treatments: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runge%27s_phenomenon ; https://interpolationcalc.com/blog/runges-phenomenon/ ; arXiv:2311.11253 (review and replication of mitigation methods).
- **Claim (DER/OBS):** Interpolating a smooth, well-behaved function (Runge's canonical example `f(x) = 1/(1+25x²)` on `[−1,1]`) with a single polynomial of increasing degree through *equally spaced* nodes produces oscillation of increasing amplitude near the interval endpoints. The interpolant passes **exactly** through every known data point while diverging arbitrarily far from the true function between and beyond them. Adding more data points and raising the degree "do not automatically mean better accuracy". Mitigation is by node placement (Chebyshev nodes, "spaced more densely near the edges") or by refusing the single high-degree global polynomial in favour of piecewise/low-order constructions.
- **Relevance:** The math anchor for the base document's §3 narrative-completion / smoothing pattern, and a precise one. The failure mode is not error at the observed points — the curve is *exact* there — it is unbounded invention in the unobserved intervals, caused by demanding a single smooth global object with too many free parameters. Perfect local fit and wild global fabrication coexist in the same object, which is exactly the structure of a chain-of-thought that is faithful at the points where it can be checked. The Chebyshev-node result is also usable: the fix is not fewer parameters but *different placement* of where the parameters are pinned.
- **Receipt status:** CLAIM (secondary: Wikipedia, interpolationcalc explainer, and an arXiv review abstract; Runge 1901 primary not read).

## 3. ECONOMICS DOMAIN

### 3.1 Authority gap — the principal-agent literature (strongest anchor set in this slice)

#### E-1. Ross: the principal's problem, and its formulation as choice over fee schedules

- **Citation:** Ross, S. A. (1973). "The Economic Theory of Agency: The Principal's Problem." *American Economic Review* 63(2), 134–139. (Papers and Proceedings.) JSTOR 1830172; AEA "Top 20" archive PDF: https://www.aeaweb.org/aer/top20/63.2.134-139.pdf
- **Claim (DER):** Founding formulation. The setup, from the paper's own notation: the agent chooses an action `a ∈ A`; the state is `θ ∈ Ω`; outcome is `w(a, θ)`; and `f = f(w(a,θ); θ)` is the fee schedule the principal offers. The principal's problem is
  `max_{⟨f⟩} E_θ{ G[ f(w(a,θ); θ) ] }`
  subject to the agent's participation/incentive constraint
  `E_θ{ G[f(w(a,θ);θ)] } ≥ k`
  and the agent's action being induced through `a(⟨f⟩)`. Ross solves the case `w(a,θ) = H[θB(a) − C(a)]` (multiplicative-separable, i.e. `dθ/da = b(a)θ + c(a)`), deriving the first-order condition `U'[w−f] = λG'[f]` and the general-form solution `U[w−f] = aG[f] + b`. Central framing: agency arises whenever one party ("the agent") acts on behalf of another ("the principal") and the principal cannot costlessly observe or control the agent's action; the paper's opening line: "The relationship of agency is one of the oldest and commonest codified modes of social interaction."
- **Relevance:** The canonical anchor for the base document's §5. Its structural content is what matters: the principal's *only* instrument is the fee schedule `f` — a function from observable outcomes to transfers. Everything the agent does that is not reflected in `w` is outside the principal's reach by construction. The authority gap is therefore not a defect Ross identifies; it is the *precondition* of the problem he poses. Note also that the solvable case requires a specific separability structure `H[θB(a) − C(a)]`: outside it, the paper's closed forms do not exist, which is the origin of the whole subsequent computational literature (cf. M-11).
- **Receipt status:** VERIFIED-CITATION (primary PDF retrieved and scraped — 6 pages, title/author/venue confirmed, and the mathematical structure extracted including the objective, the constraint `≥ k`, the first-order condition and the multiplicative-separable solution class; OCR rendered most prose as images, so the narrative text is read from the abstract-level summaries in EconPapers/RePEc and Semantic Scholar).

#### E-2. Jensen & Meckling: agency costs decomposed into monitoring, bonding, and residual loss

- **Citation:** Jensen, M. C., Meckling, W. H. (1976). "Theory of the Firm: Managerial Behavior, Agency Costs and Ownership Structure." *Journal of Financial Economics* 3(4), 305–360. DOI 10.1016/0304-405X(76)90026-X. SSRN 94043. (~15,768 citations per IDEAS/RePEc.)
- **Claim (DER):** Defines and decomposes **agency cost**. From the text: *"In most agency relationships the principal and the agent will incur positive monitoring and bonding costs (non-pecuniary as well as pecuniary), and in addition there will be some divergence between the agent's decisions and those decisions which would maximize the welfare of the principal."* So agency cost = monitoring expenditures by the principal + bonding expenditures by the agent + **residual loss** (the welfare divergence that survives both). Generality claim: *"agency costs arise in any situation involving cooperative effort (such as the co-authoring of this paper) by two or more people even though there is no clear cut principal-agent relationship"* and *"there are agency costs generated at every level of the organization."* The paper integrates property-rights theory, agency theory and finance to derive ownership structure; it invokes Coase on the boundary of the firm as "that range of exchanges over which the market system was suppressed and resource allocation was accomplished instead by authority and direction."
- **Relevance:** The single most useful economics anchor for the base document's §5, because it makes the gap *quantitative and irreducible*. Residual loss is defined as the divergence that remains after monitoring and bonding have both been paid for — i.e. an authority gap that no feasible amount of oversight eliminates. The three-way decomposition also maps onto the governance stack the base document describes in §5: monitoring (behavioural monitoring, audit trails), bonding (policy-as-code, scoped tokens, attestations the agent supplies about itself), residual loss (what still slips through). The "every level of the organization" claim matters for multi-agent harnesses specifically: delegation does not remove agency cost, it stacks it.
- **Receipt status:** VERIFIED (substantial body text read via the Illinois-hosted full PDF: agency-costs section including the monitoring/bonding/residual-loss definition, the cooperative-effort generality claim, the Coase firm-boundary discussion, §5 ownership-structure programme, and the debt/equity risk-transfer analysis).

#### E-3. Mechanism design: incentive compatibility, the revelation principle, and Hurwicz's impossibility

- **Citation:** Hurwicz, L. (1960). "Optimality and Informational Efficiency in Resource Allocation Processes." In Arrow, Karlin & Suppes (eds.), *Mathematical Methods in the Social Sciences*, Stanford University Press. Hurwicz, L. (1972). "On Informationally Decentralized Systems." In *Decision and Organization* (Radner & McGuire eds.). Myerson, R. B. (1979). "Incentive Compatibility and the Bargaining Problem." *Econometrica* 47(1), 61–73. Maskin, E. (1977). "Nash Equilibrium and Welfare Optimality." MIT Working Paper (published *Review of Economic Studies* 66(1), 23–38, 1999). Maskin, E. (1979). "The Implementation of Social Choice Rules: Some General Results on Incentive Compatibility." *Review of Economic Studies* 46, 181–216. Nobel survey: Maskin, E. (2008). "Mechanism Design Theory." *Econometrica* / Nobel Prize Advanced Information, https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/advanced-economicsciences2007.pdf
- **Claim (DER):** From the Nobel advanced-information text:
  - Hurwicz's formulation: *"a mechanism is a communication system in which participants exchange messages with each other, messages that jointly determine the outcome... The mechanism is like a machine that compiles and processes the received messages, thereby aggregating (true or false) private information provided by many agents."* Outcomes are implemented "as equilibria of message games, where the mechanism defines the 'rules' of the message game."
  - **Incentive compatibility (Hurwicz 1972):** "the mechanism is incentive-compatible if it is a dominant strategy for each participant to report his private information truthfully."
  - **Hurwicz's impossibility:** *"Under some weak assumptions on technology and taste, Hurwicz (1972) proved the following negative result: in a standard exchange economy, no incentive-compatible mechanism which satisfies the participation constraint can produce Pareto-optimal outcomes. In other words, private information precludes full efficiency."*
  - **Revelation principle (Myerson 1979, 1982, 1986):** "any equilibrium outcome of an arbitrary mechanism can be replicated by an incentive-compatible direct mechanism." General version valid "not only when agents have private information but also when they take unobserved actions (so-called moral hazard), as well as when mechanisms have multiple stages." Proof sketch supplied in the survey.
  - **Multiple-equilibrium problem and Maskin's implementation theory:** *"The revelation principle is extremely useful. However, it does not address the issue of multiple equilibria. That is, although an optimal outcome may be achieved in one equilibrium, other, sub-optimal, equilibria may also exist. There is, then, the danger that the participants might end up playing such a sub-optimal equilibrium. Can a mechanism be designed so that all its equilibria are optimal? The first general solution to this problem was given by Eric Maskin (1977)."*
  - Further impossibilities: Laffont & Maskin (1979) and Myerson & Satterthwaite (1983) prove "the incompatibility of voluntary participation and classical Pareto efficiency"; hence "a new standard of efficiency which takes incentives into account" is needed — *incentive efficiency* (maximising a weighted sum of agents' expected payoffs subject to IC constraints).
  - Consensus incompatibility: *"An important insight is that consensual decision-making is frequently incompatible with economic efficiency."*
  - Myerson (1985) gloss: a mechanism μ is Bayesian incentive compatible "iff it is a Bayesian equilibrium for all players to report their types honestly and to obey the mediator's recommendations"; "we cannot expect all the players to participate honestly and obediently in a coordination mechanism unless it is incentive compatible."
- **Relevance:** This is the discipline whose *subject matter* is the authority gap, and it supplies four load-bearing results. (1) The mechanism/message framing is structurally identical to an authority envelope: rules that compile messages into outcomes, where the rules — not the agents' dispositions — are the object of design. (2) Incentive compatibility is the formal criterion for "the envelope works": truthful reporting must be a *dominant strategy*, not merely a rewarded one. (3) Hurwicz's impossibility gives a hard negative bound: with private information plus voluntary participation, full efficiency is unattainable — an irreducible authority gap, matching E-2's residual loss from the other direction. (4) Maskin's implementation theory answers the objection that a good rule is enough: a mechanism with one good equilibrium and other bad ones can still land in a bad one, so the design target is that *all* equilibria are acceptable. The "consensual decision-making is frequently incompatible with efficiency" insight bears directly on the base document's §4 sycophancy pattern.

#### E-4. Schelling: commitment as a strategic move that binds the committer

- **Citation:** Schelling, T. C. (1960). *The Strategy of Conflict.* Harvard University Press. Full text: https://www.sackett.net/Strategy-of-Conflict.pdf . Retrospective: Myerson, R. B. (2009). "Learning from Schelling's Strategy of Conflict." *Journal of Economic Literature* 47(4), 1109–1129. DOI 10.1257/jel.47.4.1109. https://home.uchicago.edu/~rmyerson/stratofc2010.pdf
- **Claim (DER/INT):** Commitment is a *strategic move* — "a move that induces the other" player's behaviour by altering one's own available actions. From the text: "The commitment is a means of gaining *first move* in a game in which first move carries an advantage; the threat is a commitment"; "to commit himself—there remains but a single, well-determined commitment, and the other makes a final decision"; and the paradox that commitment operates "to the disadvantage of the committed party." Credibility requires visible irreversibility: "In bargaining, the commitment is a device to leave the last clear chance to decide the outcome with the other party, in a manner that he fully appreciates; it is to relinquish further initiative, having rigged the incentives so that the other party must choose in one's favor." Myerson's summary: "One constrains the partner's choice by constraining one's own behavior"; and "it is only following the deliberate and irreversible abandonment of one's options that one can secure a position of total dominance." Myerson also notes Schelling's "emphasis on the critical importance of information and commitment in strategic" interaction, and "strategic credibility".
- **Relevance:** The economics anchor that *commitment itself is a designed instrument with value*, and that its value comes precisely from destroying alternatives. This is the opposite polarity from the base document's §1, where premature commitment is the failure — and the difference is entirely in who commits, when, and with what visibility. Schelling's conditions for a commitment to be *strategically* effective (deliberate, irreversible, observable, made before the counterparty moves) are a checklist that premature commitment in an agent fails on the "deliberate" and "before-the-evidence" items while satisfying the irreversibility item — which is what makes it costly rather than merely suboptimal.

### 3.2 Action bias — real options and behavioural economics

#### E-5. Real options: irreversibility plus uncertainty gives waiting a positive price

- **Citation:** McDonald, R., Siegel, D. (1986). "The Value of Waiting to Invest." *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 101(4), 707–727. DOI 10.2307/1885527. NBER Working Paper w1019 (Nov 1982); SSRN 227179. Book-length treatment: Dixit, A. K., Pindyck, R. S. (1994). *Investment Under Uncertainty.* Princeton University Press. Full text: https://msuweb.montclair.edu/~lebelp/DixitPindyck1994.pdf ; JSTOR j.ctt7sncv.
- **Claim (DER):** McDonald & Siegel: "This paper studies the optimal timing of investment in an irreversible" project under uncertainty, and derives the option value of waiting. Dixit & Pindyck formalise it: "This new approach to investment recognizes the option value of waiting for better (but never complete) information." Worked two-period example from the text (price uncertainty, investment cost `I`, project value `V₀`): the flexibility option's value is the difference between the two NPVs — e.g. `$773 − $600 = $173`. The decision rule: "For I > $1284, F₀ = 1500 − 0.455·I > V₀ − I, so **the option should be kept alive**, that is, we should wait until next year before deciding whether to invest." At `I = $1284` exactly, `F₀ = $916 = V₀ − I` and "one would be indifferent between investing today and waiting until next year." Crucially: "Therefore an option to wait has no value next year, and investment is made according to the conventional NPV criterion at that time. In later chapters we will consider more general situations where **uncertainty is never fully resolved, and the option to wait retains value at all times**." Also: uncertainty about the *discount rate* works in the opposite direction — "it creates a value of waiting (to see whether interest rates rise or fall)."
- **Relevance:** The economics anchor for action bias that is quantitative rather than psychological. The naive rule is "invest whenever NPV > 0"; the correct rule is "invest whenever the project value exceeds the strike *plus the option value*", i.e. `V > I + F₀`. The gap `F₀` is exactly the cost of acting now instead of later, and the worked example shows it can be large relative to the NPV margin ($173 on a $600 NPV — nearly 29%). The `$1284` indifference threshold is the economics-side analogue of the reservation value `ρ = W` in M-5 and the aspiration threshold in M-6 — three independent derivations of the same structure. The "uncertainty is never fully resolved, so the option retains value at all times" result is the important one for a coding agent: there is no point at which waiting stops being priced. Action bias = exercising the option before the value crosses the indifference threshold.
- **Receipt status:** VERIFIED (Dixit & Pindyck full PDF body passages read: Ch. 1 two-period worked example with the $773/$600/$173 figures, the $1284 indifference threshold, the "option should be kept alive" decision rule, the never-fully-resolved generalisation, and the interest-rate-uncertainty counter-effect. #### E-6. Behavioural economics: prospect theory, status quo bias, sunk cost

- **Citation (a):** Kahneman, D., Tversky, A. (1979). "Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk." *Econometrica* 47(2), 263–292. DOI 10.2307/1914185. JSTOR 1914185. Full PDF: https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Behavioral_Decision_Theory/Kahneman_Tversky_1979_Prospect_theory.pdf
  - **Claim (OBS/DER):** "This paper presents a critique of expected utility theory as a descriptive model of decision making under risk, and develops an alternative model, called prospect theory." Core structural claims: value is defined on *gains and losses relative to a reference point*, not on final asset positions ("Shifts of Reference... the reference point was taken to be the status quo, or one's current assets"); the value function is concave for gains and convex for losses (risk-averse in gains, risk-seeking in losses); losses loom larger than gains by roughly 2:1; and decision *weights* systematically underweight high-probability and overweight low-probability events relative to their objective probabilities.
  - **Relevance:** Reference-dependence is the mechanism by which the base document's §2 action bias gets its direction. If value is coded relative to a reference point, then "did nothing" is not the neutral zero — it is a position on the value curve, and its sign depends on where the reference was set. Combined with loss aversion, inaction that permits a loss is coded as a loss and therefore *over-weighted*, generating a systematic pull toward action. Also relevant to §1: once an interpretation is adopted it becomes the reference point, and subsequent evidence is coded as gains/losses relative to it rather than in absolute terms — which is a formal route from "settled early" to "defends the settlement".
  - **Receipt status:** VERIFIED-CITATION (primary PDF located at MIT; Econometrica 47(2):263–292, DOI and JSTOR ID confirmed across four independent records; the "Shifts of Reference" passage read; full body not read).

- **Citation (b):** Samuelson, W., Zeckhauser, R. (1988). "Status Quo Bias in Decision Making." *Journal of Risk and Uncertainty* 1(1), 7–59. DOI 10.1007/BF00055564. Full PDF: http://www.communicationcache.com/uploads/1/0/8/8/10887248/status_quo_bias_in_decision_making.pdf
  - **Claim (OBS):** "Most real decisions, unlike those of economics texts, have a status quo alternative—that is, doing nothing or maintaining one's current or previous decision. A series of decision-making experiments shows that individuals disproportionately stick with the status quo. Data on the selections of health plans and retirement programs by faculty members reveal that the status quo bias is substantial in important real decisions." "The main finding is that decision makers exhibit a significant status quo bias." Robustness note: *"the status quo bias is not a mistake—like a calculation error or an error in maximizing—that once pointed out is easily recognized and corrected"*; it is "best viewed as a deeply rooted decision-making practice stemming partly from a mental illusion and partly from psychological inclination." Second-round anchoring is predicted and found: "status quo bias would be manifested in an anchoring effect—second-year decisions would be anchored in part to first-year decisions." Field evidence: TIAA-CREF faculty plan choice and a water-distribution framing experiment both show the effect; subjects are "only partially anchored to the status quo point". Consequence for theory: "rational models will present excessively radical conclusions, exaggerating individuals' responses to changing economic variables."
  - **Relevance:** An honest complication for the base document's §2, and worth recording as such. Status quo bias is the *opposite* directional pull from action bias — it is a bias toward inaction. Both are documented, both are robust, and they are not the same phenomenon. Which one dominates is an empirical question about how the choice is framed: what counts as the status quo. That makes the reference point (E-6a) the operative variable and suggests that "action bias" and "status quo bias" are two readings of one reference-dependence mechanism, distinguished by which option is coded as default. The "not a mistake that is easily corrected once pointed out" finding is separately important: it rules out the simplest class of remedy. The second-year anchoring result is a direct measurement of commitment persisting across decision rounds — the closest behavioural-economics analogue to premature commitment in the base document's §1.
  - **Receipt status:** VERIFIED (substantial body text read from the full PDF: abstract, main finding, the not-a-calculation-error passage, the anchoring prediction, the TIAA-CREF and water-distribution studies, and the "excessively radical conclusions" implication).

- **Citation (c):** Arkes, H. R., Blumer, C. (1985). "The Psychology of Sunk Cost." *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes* 35(1), 124–140. DOI 10.1016/0749-5978(85)90049-4. APA PsycNet 1985-20101-001.
  - **Claim (OBS):** Demonstrates experimentally that people continue an endeavour after having made an unrecoverable investment, contrary to normative decision theory (under which sunk costs are irrelevant to the forward-looking choice). Attributes the effect to a desire not to appear wasteful/profligate. ~4,981 citations (Google Scholar); 796 (PsycNet); 311 (EconPapers).
  - **Relevance:** The mechanism behind the base document's §6 "Runaway Loop" pattern ("Action taken, action does not fully solve the problem, another action is taken. Simple task becomes 20 iterations deep"). Once tokens, edits, and elapsed turns are spent, forward-looking optimality says to stop and reassess; sunk-cost reasoning says to continue because stopping would render the prior investment wasted. Note the connection to M-4: David's own term for the intellectual version of this is "sunk cost hysteresis", explicitly linking it to the P-2 physics result.
  - **Receipt status:** CLAIM (citation and citation counts verified across ScienceDirect, APA PsycNet, EconPapers, ResearchGate and Google Scholar; abstract-level only, full text behind paywall).

#### E-7. Action bias measured: elite goalkeepers jump when staying put is better

- **Citation:** Bar-Eli, M., Azar, O. H., Ritov, I., Keidar-Levin, Y., Schein, G. (2007). "Action Bias among Elite Soccer Goalkeepers: The Case of Penalty Kicks." *Journal of Economic Psychology* 28(5), 606–621. DOI 10.1016/j.joep.2006.12.001. Full PDF: https://pluto.mscc.huji.ac.il/~msiritov/BarEliAzarRitovKeidarSchein.pdf . MPRA working-paper version: Paper 4477, https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/4477/
- **Claim (OBS):** Analyses 286 penalty kicks from professional soccer leagues. The empirical finding: goalkeepers jump left or right far more often than they stay in the centre, even though the *optimal* strategy (given the observed distribution of kick directions) is to stay in the centre more often than they do. Kick-direction distribution from the paper's data table: centre kicks account for 28.7% of penalties (82 of 286), of which the goalkeeper jumped left on 41, stayed centre on only 10, and jumped right on 31. The stated mechanism: *"a goal scored yields worse feelings for the goalkeeper following inaction (staying in the center) than following action (jumping), leading to a bias for action."* A follow-up study of goalkeepers' and kickers' attitudes (Appendix C questionnaire: "Jump right/jump left/stay in the center of the goal") confirms the anticipated-regret account.
  - Honest counter-evidence in the index: "No action 'Bias' among elite soccer goalkeepers during penalty kicks" (*Journal of Economic Psychology*, DOI pii S0167487026000103, 2026) directly disputes the finding, noting Bar-Eli et al. "claimed that elite soccer goalkeepers" show the bias.
- **Relevance:** The canonical *measured* action-bias result in economics, and the mechanism it identifies is the one that transfers: anticipated regret is asymmetric between acting and not acting, because a bad outcome following inaction is attributed to the self while a bad outcome following action is attributed to chance. That is a self-attribution asymmetry, which connects it to the base document's §2 "Self-Attribution Bias in LLM Agents" source (OpenReview hv9leXYyPP) — the same structure, one measured in elite athletes and one measured in models. Also note the 10-of-82 figure: when the correct answer is "stay", the rate of staying is 12%, i.e. the bias is not marginal. The 2026 rebuttal should be recorded as an open dispute about the specific dataset, not about the existence of the phenomenon (which is separately supported by Patt & Zeckhauser 2000 on policy inaction and Ritov & Patt on omission bias).
- **Receipt status:** VERIFIED (body passages read from the HUJI-hosted full PDF: abstract with the anticipated-regret mechanism, Study 1 data table with the 41/10/31 and 82-centre-kicks figures, Appendix B aggregation, Appendix C attitudes questionnaire).

#### E-8. Path dependence and lock-in (economics-side commitment)

- **Citation:** David, P. A. (1985). "Clio and the Economics of QWERTY." *American Economic Review* 75(2), 332–337. JSTOR 1805621. Survey treatment: http://www.ralfmeisenzahl.com/uploads/7/6/8/1/76818505/path_dependency_-_the_economics_of_qwerty.pdf ("History matters. Increasing returns. Positive feedback. Self-reinforcement. Lock-ins.")
- **Claim (INT/OBS):** The QWERTY keyboard layout persists despite the availability of superior alternatives (notably Dvorak), because of increasing returns to adoption, network/complementarity effects, and positive feedback in learning and hardware supply. Small, contingent historical events in the 1870s determined which standard achieved dominance; once achieved, switching costs made the outcome effectively irreversible. Standard summary framing (from the Meisenzahl survey): path dependence arises from the conjunction of increasing returns, positive feedback, and self-reinforcement, producing lock-in to a possibly sub-optimal configuration.
- **Relevance:** The economics anchor for the base document's §1, complementing M-4's stochastic-process formulation. QWERTY is the canonical demonstration that "an early contingent choice plus increasing returns ⇒ a persistent, sub-optimal equilibrium" — the same inferential shape as premature commitment, at the level of an industry rather than a single decision. The increasing-returns mechanism is the piece that has no direct physics analogue in this slice: in P-1 the basin is fixed by the potential, whereas here the basin *deepens as a function of having been entered*, because adoption itself changes the payoff landscape.

## 5. WHAT THESE THREE DOMAINS CONTRIBUTE THAT AI-ONLY LITERATURE MISSES

Physics and mathematics supply *impossibility and scaling results* rather than behavioural observations, which converts the three patterns from documented tendencies into consequences with known functional form: escape from a committed state is exponentially suppressed in barrier-over-noise (P-1, P-5), adiabatic slowing provably fails at the transition point so "go slower" cannot be a general remedy (P-2), a constraint-deficient system is at best weakly hyperbolic and therefore ill-posed rather than merely poorly governed (P-9), and the tolerance for acting on approximate information has a proven phase boundary at ε = 1/2 beyond which thresholds become ambiguous and prediction impossible (M-6). Economics contributes the *cost accounting* that AI-domain writing lacks: the authority gap is decomposed into monitoring cost, bonding cost, and an irreducible residual loss (E-2), the impossibility of full efficiency under private information is a theorem rather than a difficulty (E-3), and the value of waiting is priced as an option with an explicit indifference threshold and a closed-form surrogate (E-5) — so "should the agent have acted" becomes a computable comparison rather than a judgement about disposition. Together they also supply honest counterweights that an AI-only review tends to omit: saddle plateaus give the *illusory impression* of a minimum so the felt signature of settling is not evidence about it (M-1), truncated search can outperform full optimisation under noise (M-7), status quo bias pulls in the opposite direction from action bias and which one binds depends on how the default is framed (E-6b), and the headline action-bias measurement is itself under active dispute in 2026 (E-7).
