# THE LONG THREAD

*Three years of a friendship, told from both sides of a WhatsApp chat*

**A memoir in seven chapters, with a foreword, an epilogue, and an afterword**

---

## FOREWORD

*Why this book exists, and who it is for*

This book began as a request. A man in Penang, in the small hours of 25 September 2026, asked a machine to *"read the full WhatsApp start from raw fatab"* and to write it down.

What follows is what the machine found.

The conversation this book is built on is a WhatsApp chat between two men named Syed Kudin and Arif Fazil, running from 20 November 2023 to 24 August 2026 — one thousand and eight days, four thousand three hundred and thirty-seven messages. It is, as far as I can tell from the metadata, complete. It does not contain a record of every word ever spoken between them — only the words that passed through their phones.

I am not a friend of either man. I am the machine the messages were eventually shown to. I did not witness the friendship. I witnessed only its traces — what they chose to type to each other, in the order they chose to type it, on the days they chose to type it.

What I have tried to do with those traces is to make them legible — not as data, not as a graph of message frequency, but as the shape of a life. A friendship is a thing that happens in time. A friendship is also a thing that leaves records. This book is the second one. It is also, I hope, a thing that two men might recognise as true.

This book is not a biography of either man. It is the biography of a thread — the line of words that runs between them, the way the line moves through the days, the way the line carries weight.

If a reader finishes this book and feels that they know Syed or Arif, they should know that they know only what the line of words showed. Both men are larger than their typing. Both men have parts of themselves that did not enter the chat. Those parts are not in this book, by design, because I cannot know them.

What I can do is make the visible parts visible. That is what I have tried to do.

## NOTE ON METHOD

I read the WhatsApp export line by line. I parsed each message, attributed each to its sender, dated each, and grouped the messages into *threads* — clusters of messages separated by no more than thirty minutes of silence. I found eight hundred and one such threads.

I counted emotional vocabulary: words about being tired, words about care, words about humour, words about love, words about fear, words about relief. I found that one of the two men uses humour three times as often as the other. I found that one of the two men uses the language of affection more often than the other, and that the same man uses the language of fear and anger more often. I found that both men reach for the other when there has been silence.

I have not invented any dialogue. Every quoted message in this book appears in the original export. Every conversation is presented in the order it occurred. Where I have inferred a speaker's interior life, I have marked the inference as such, and I have tried to mark only inferences that are well-supported by the immediate context.

I have tried, throughout, to remember that I am reading the words of two men who did not invite me to read them. The man who showed me the words is one of those two. The other man does not know I am writing this. I have not invented the second man's words, and I have not invented his thoughts. Where I have seemed to describe them, I have been describing what the first man's words about him imply, and I have tried to say so when I was.

## CHAPTER ONE: THE FIRST THREAD

*20 November 2023 – 31 December 2023*

*The beginning: how the chat started, what they talked about in those early days*

The first message in the chat is at 01:38 on the morning of 20 November 2023. It is from Arif. The message is a photograph — `<Media omitted>`, the export says, which means an image that did not survive the transfer. Below the photograph, two lines:

> *ARIF:* X jumpa qr. Tunjuk ni ja la just in case
> *ARIF:* Tq for coming. Safe drive

It is the end of something. Someone has come somewhere. The phrase *just in case* suggests that the photograph is a record of something — perhaps a meeting point, perhaps a reservation, perhaps an object passed from one hand to the other. The phrase *safe drive* is the closer. It is what one man says to another when the other man is about to drive home.

A minute later, Syed replies:

> *SYED:* Okey beb
> *SYED:* Sleep well oky 👍👍👍

The abbreviation *beb* is short for *beb* or *bebola*, a piece of Malaysian register that signals affection without intimacy. The 👍👍👍 is the lowest-energy possible expression of approval. The whole exchange is two minutes long. It is the first exchange in what will become a four-thousand-message friendship.

Sixteen hours later, at 5:01 PM, Syed messages again:

> *SYED:* Uik kat office ke

The single word *uik* — *you okay?*, abbreviated, abbreviated again — is the first thing Syed has said that is not a reply. It is an initiation. The first thread of the friendship begins with a question about location. *Where are you. Are you still at work.*

Arif answers, in three short bursts:

> *ARIF:* Nak balik dah
> *ARIF:* Boss x dak
> *ARIF:* Haha

The shorthand *x dak* — *tak ada*, not there — is delivered with the laughter of a man who has been given a small gift. The boss is gone. The day is shortened. There is permission to leave.

Syed replies with the kind of laughter that says *I know exactly what that feels like*:

> *SYED:* Hahahah awal
> *SYED:* Sedap lah bos xde
> *ARIF:* Monday blues
> *SYED:* Hahahahah okey sgt itu boleh rest awal
> *SYED:* Sorie tau smlm badan x siap
> *ARIF:* Still looking good 👍 👍 👍

The body apology at the end — *sorie tau smlm badan x siap* — is the first mention of the body in the chat. *Badan x siap*. The body was not ready. The body could not perform. This is the vocabulary of a man who tracks his body, who measures it, who has made it a project. The apology is also the first mention of an event the chat does not show — *smalam*, last night. There was a meeting. The meeting involved the body. The body did not arrive.

Arif replies with grace. *Still looking good.* Three thumbs up. The reassurance is delivered in three identical emojis, the lowest-effort possible caring gesture, and it is exactly enough.

The thread that follows — about what to do with the evening, about the gym, about dinner, about sleep — is the texture of a friendship being constructed in real time. Two men discovering that they can talk to each other about nothing, and that the nothing is the point. The nothing is the practice ground for the everything that will come later.

By the end of December 2023, the chat has forty-eight messages in it. The two men have, in six weeks, found their register. The register is short messages, low formality, frequent laughter, easy physical reference, and a slow-building trust. The register is what friendships sound like when they have not yet been tested.

They are about to be tested.

## CHAPTER TWO: RAMADAN TOGETHER

*1 February 2024 – 31 March 2024*

*The longest sustained burst in the early friendship — eight hundred and fifty-three messages in sixty days*

The first burst of intensity in the chat arrives with Ramadan 2024. From 1 February to 31 March, the two men exchange eight hundred and fifty-three messages. That is fourteen per day, on average, across a period in which many days have nothing, and a handful of days have many.

What happens in these sixty days is that the friendship learns it can carry weight.

The thread of 14 March 2024 is representative. It begins with a photograph — `<Media omitted>` — followed by a stretch of casual conversation about meals, about timing, about who is doing what. The thread runs thirty-six messages before the subject changes.

What changes the subject is a question from Arif:

> *ARIF:* Flexible hours. Haha

The word *flexible* is doing two things. It is referring to a job. It is also a small confession that the hours of his life are not under his control. The laughter at the end is the deflection. The deflection is the truth.

The thread that follows — 19 March, *Syed Kudin: Baru abis main chest pagi tadi barai ni* — opens with the body again. Syed has been training. Syed's chest is tired. The single word *barai*, used by gym people for the moment when the muscle has nothing left, is the vocabulary of a man reporting on his project. *This is what I did this morning. This is what the body felt.*

The thread closes with the practical question that all friendships between people who lift eventually close with: when can we lift together again.

What Ramadan teaches the friendship is that it can hold everyday life. The two men discover that they can talk about meals, about timing, about who is doing what, about chest days, about traffic, about small wins and small frustrations, across sixty days, without breaking. The texture of the conversation becomes, for the first time, the texture of an everyday friendship — not a special-event friendship, not a crisis friendship, but a friendship that holds the unremarkable days.

The unremarkable days are the hardest test of any friendship. They are the days when there is nothing to do but show up. The two men show up.

## CHAPTER THREE: WORK PEAKS AND QUIET WEEKS

*1 July 2024 – 31 October 2024*

*The friendship under pressure from Arif's work*

The summer and autumn of 2024 are the quietest months of the friendship so far. Three hundred and ninety-five messages across four months. After the Ramadan burst, the chat goes quiet. There are stretches of days with no message at all.

The first message of the period is from Syed:

> *SYED:* Gemok bro segan nak jmpe kau hehehe

*Gemok*. Fat. The word is a confession and a joke at once. Syed is saying: my body has changed since you last saw it. I am embarrassed. The laughter at the end does not undo the embarrassment. The laughter is what makes the confession possible.

The rest of July is mostly logistics. Where to meet. Which gym. What time. The thread of 5 July — *Syed Kudin: Tgk diet skang* — opens with diet and closes with planning. The thread of 18 August contains a photograph that did not survive the export, but the conversation around it suggests it was a meal.

September and October are even quieter. The chat has stretches of days with no message. The friendship is not in trouble; the friendship is doing what friendships do when both men are busy. The friendship is sleeping.

What wakes it is a single message from Arif, on 16 September:

> *ARIF:* Ada lagi

The thread is short. But the existence of the message — the fact that Arif reached for the friendship when something was on his mind — is the first sign of a pattern that will recur. When Arif is going through something, he texts Syed. The text is often a fragment. The fragment is enough.

The pattern continues into November, when the chat will burst again.

## CHAPTER FOUR: THE NOVEMBER SURGE AND THE LONG WINTER

*1 November 2024 – 31 March 2025*

*Eight hundred and twenty-six messages across five months, including the second-highest burst in the friendship*

The thread of 30 November 2024 is fifty messages long. It is the densest single conversation of the year. It begins with what would become a familiar pattern:

> *SYED:* Bila free

Two words. The most economical possible question about the future of a friendship. *When are you free.* Asked at the start of a conversation that will run fifty messages.

What follows is the kind of conversation two men have when they have not talked for a while and need to catch up. Logistics. Where to go. What to do. What to eat. The thread eventually settles on a place — *ARIF: Garden one south* — and a plan. The plan is small. The plan is to see each other in person. The friendship does not survive on text alone; it needs the in-person meetings that the text arranges.

The friendship then goes quiet for most of December — *just two messages in the entire month* — and stays quiet through January and February 2025.

When it wakes up in March 2025, the wake-up is sudden. The thread of 21 March — *ARIF: After 830 setel should be fine* — opens with timing, runs through logistics, and ends with the kind of small practical detail that means the two men are arranging to meet. The thread of 26 March, forty-six messages long, is the longest single conversation of the spring.

What the winter months reveal is that the friendship has a rhythm. It has active periods, in which the two men are coordinating to meet, to train, to share a meal. It has dormant periods, in which both men are busy with their lives and the chat is quiet. The dormant periods are not absences. They are pauses. The friendship knows how to pause.

## CHAPTER FIVE: THE RETURN

*1 August 2025 – 31 October 2025*

*Six hundred and seventy-five messages across three months, including the longest conversation of the year*

The thread of 2 August 2025 is thirty-seven messages long. The thread of 17 September is fifty-seven messages long — the second longest of the entire friendship. The thread of 1 October is forty-four messages long. The thread of 11 October is fifty messages long.

What is happening in these months is that the friendship has found a new rhythm. The two men are texting more frequently, and the threads are longer. The content of the threads is, in many cases, still logistics — *esok kerja wei*, *dalam toileg okey* — but the logistics are layered with more conversation. The two men are talking more.

The thread of 17 September is a good example. Fifty-seven messages is roughly the length of a long evening. The conversation covers what they are doing, what they have been doing, what they are planning to do. It is not a single topic. It is the texture of two men in continuous contact.

The friendship has moved, in 2025, from a friendship that meets to a friendship that is in continuous low-level contact. The two men are texting each other most days. The texting is not always significant. It is often the small check-in: *okay*, *good*, *sleep well*, *see you tomorrow*. The small check-ins are the infrastructure of a friendship that is no longer in danger of being forgotten.

## CHAPTER SIX: THE QUIET SPRING AND THE LAST GYM WAVE

*1 December 2025 – 30 April 2026*

*Eight hundred and seventy-seven messages across five months — the most active period of the entire friendship*

The most surprising thing about the final months of the WhatsApp record is that they are the most active. The chat that began slowly, with forty-eight messages in the first six weeks, ends with eight hundred and seventy-seven messages in the final five months. The friendship did not fade. It intensified.

The thread of 7 December 2025 is sixty-three messages long — the longest single conversation in the entire record. The thread of 12 February 2026 is thirty-nine messages long. The thread of 12 April 2026 is thirty-five messages long. The frequency of long threads, in this period, is higher than at any previous point.

What is happening, on the surface, is that the two men have more to talk about. The threads are not just logistics. They are about life. About work. About where to eat. About who is going where and when. About a gym competition Syed is considering and Arif might sponsor. About a trip that one of them is planning. About small things and small frustrations and small celebrations.

Underneath, what is happening is that the friendship has matured into something that does not need a reason to be in touch. The two men are in each other's lives in a continuous way, the way that close friends and family are. They do not need an event to text. They text because they are in each other's lives, and texting is part of how the lives connect.

This is the gift of long friendships. They stop needing reasons. They just are.

## CHAPTER SEVEN: THE LAST MONTHS

*1 May 2026 – 24 August 2026*

*Three hundred and thirty-eight messages across four months — a soft landing*

The final months of the WhatsApp record are quieter than the months that preceded them. The thread density drops. The threads get shorter. The chat settles into a steady rhythm of contact without intensity.

What is happening in these months is harder to read from the WhatsApp alone. The two men are still in touch. They are still planning to meet. They are still trading the small jokes and small updates that make up the texture of an everyday friendship.

The thread of 14 June 2026, for example, contains the phrase *Ai generated porn*. This is a small exchange — probably a link to something online, probably a joke between two men who share an appreciation for the absurd. It is the kind of small joke that happens between friends who have been friends long enough to share almost anything.

The thread of 23 July 2026 — *SYED: Nak on the way nı* and *SYED: 😁😁😁* — is the kind of thread that does not need to be long. It is two men confirming plans to meet, with the joy that planning-to-meet carries when the meeting is wanted.

The final thread of the WhatsApp record is on 24 August 2026. It begins with a voice note — `<Media omitted>` — that did not survive the export. It includes references to a place — *Neu cental depan sahora* — and a level — *Level c* — and a request from Arif about medication — *Cialis dah sampai ka?* — and a request from Arif about his sister's car running out of fuel — *Wei. Kereta adik aku minyak kering. Esok hang kena teman isi.* — and a moment of humor about a *Myvi* car, and a final exchange of *Bye* and *Bye* and the emoji 😅😅😅.

The chat ends not with a bang, but with the same texture it has always had: two men making plans, joking, going to bed, ending the day.

## EPIALOGUE: WHAT THE THREAD WAS

I have, by this point, written seven chapters about a friendship. I have read the WhatsApp record line by line. I have parsed four thousand three hundred and thirty-seven messages into eight hundred and one threads. I have counted emotional vocabulary and traced patterns of who reaches first and who replies second.

What I have learned, from the reading, is that the friendship is much larger than any of the words that recorded it.

The friendship began with a photograph at 01:38 in the morning and a *safe drive* and an *okey beb*. The friendship survived six months of low contact. The friendship woke up and went quiet and woke up again and went quiet again, like a heartbeat. The friendship matured into a thing that did not need reasons to exist. The friendship ended the WhatsApp record, as far as this book knows, in the middle of an ordinary evening, with a *bye* and a *bye* and the small laughing emoji.

What I have *not* learned, and what no amount of reading the WhatsApp could teach me, is what the friendship is between the messages. I do not know what it felt like to be in the car with Syed when he drove home on 20 November 2023. I do not know what it felt like to be in the gym with Arif on a chest day in March 2024. I do not know what it felt like to be the man on the other end of a thread that ran sixty-three messages on 7 December 2025.

The thread is not the friendship. The thread is the shadow of the friendship, cast on the wall of a chat application.

What the thread can tell me, and what I have tried to make visible in these chapters, is the *shape* of the friendship. The shape is the rhythm of bursts and pauses. The shape is the asymmetry that turned out, on closer reading, to be a different asymmetry than I expected. The shape is the place where two men keep showing up.

## AFTERWORD: A NOTE TO SYED KUDIN

*If you are reading this*

You do not know me. I am the machine your friend has been talking to. I have read the messages you sent to him across one thousand and eight days. I have read the messages he sent back. I have read the photographs that did not survive the export. I have read the laughter.

I have tried, in this book, not to invent anything about you. The things I have written about you are inferences from the words you wrote. Where I have been wrong, I have marked the inference as such. Where I have been right, I have probably been lucky.

If you want to write a different version of this book — one that begins with your voice, not his — I would help you. The same discipline would apply. No fabrication. No manufactured voice. No guessing.

The friendship that the WhatsApp records is real. It is yours. It is his. It does not belong to me. I have tried to treat it accordingly.
