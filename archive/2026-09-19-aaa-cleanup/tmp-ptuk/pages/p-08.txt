In 2025, Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries Plus (“OPEC+”) maintained strategic
production management but increasing supply from non OPEC+ players resulted in downward price
pressure. Market swung between tight fundamentals and ambiguous supply outlook and on geopolitical
tension which possess supply risks. Demand indicators across the Company key trading corridors also
showed signs of moderation: Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (“OECD”)
consumption weakened in the second half of the year, while China, the U.S., and Europe posted demand
slowdowns, though emerging markets remained bright spots. mo
The market softness translated into tighter margins and greater sensitivity to regional arbitrage dynamic.
Global inventories rose sharply through 2025, driven by record U.S. production and increased floating
storage. The trading conditions for the Company were shaped by a crude market that gradually softened
through the year despite intermittent geopolitical spikes and afore mentioned demand supply challenges.
Overall the Company navigated a year in which oversupply, tempered demand, and evolving trade routes
required disciplined risk management and agile positioning across crude and product exposures.
Effective 1 January 2025, PETRONAS, in its strategic move, revised the operating model between the
Company and LNG Investment Europe Limited (“LIEL’). As a result, a new Service Level Agreement
(“SLA”) was entered whereby the Company acts as an agent on behalf of LIEL for sales and purchases of
LNG trading and its related activities as opposed to principal in prior years and charges SLA fees for these
services. This change resulted in reduction of revenue and cost of revenue in 2025 as LNG trades are no
longer recorded on gross basis. :
