## 1. Core Claim

> **Two humans meeting in real time is a communication system with
> impedance. Translation cost is the per-exchange penalty paid when
> embodied, linguistic, and affection registers do not already
> overlap. Low translation cost is operationally valuable; it is not,
> by itself, diagnostic of sexual orientation.**

---

## 2. The Three Primary Registers

The heuristic decomposes relational impedance into **three registers**
that can be measured or estimated on a 0–1 "already-aligned" scale:

### 2.1 Embodied Register

The vocabulary of bodies in proximity: movement grammar, postural
code, sport-and-craft vocabulary, breath-and-rest pattern, eye-contact
customs, proxemics.

**Already-aligned indicators:**

- Shared training history in adjacent traditions (e.g. both lift, both
  dance, both practise a martial art).
- Adjacent cultural background with similar bodily norms (e.g.
 东南亚 + Nusantara vs. Northern European + North American).
- Similar nervous-system set-points (resting arousal, recovery
  tempo, sensory appetite).
- Shared neurotype profile (both ADHD, both autistic, etc.).

**Translation-cost indicators:**

- Different vocabulary for the same posture (e.g. one reads a stance
  as confident; the other reads it as aggressive).
- Different recovery tempo (one recharges alone; the other through
  touch).
- Different sensory thresholds (one wants quiet; the other wants
  music).

### 2.2 Linguistic Register

The vocabulary of speech and silence: mother tongue, idioms,
code-switching habits, in-group vocabulary, idiomatic humour, the
*taciturnity* vs. *verbosity* axis.

**Already-aligned indicators:**

- Shared mother tongue.
- Shared register choice (slang, formal, BMpasar, Manglish, BM baku).
- Shared humour vocabulary.
- Compatible silence — both comfortable or both uncomfortable with
  long silences.

**Translation-cost indicators:**

- Constant code-switching fatigue.
- Words-of-art with incompatible meanings.
- Asymmetric verbosity (one is exhaustive; the other is laconic).

### 2.3 Affection Register

The vocabulary of touch and proximity that the relationship permits.
Crucially, **this is not the same as sexual behaviour**. It is the
much wider question of how bodies may meet: hugs, hand-holding, leaning,
sleeping proximity, grooming gestures, casual touch in public, etc.

**Already-aligned indicators:**

- Similar comfort levels with non-sexual touch.
- Reciprocal initiation patterns.
- Compatible public/private affection norms.

**Translation-cost indicators:**

- One partner is touch-hungry; the other is touch-averse.
- Asymmetric reciprocity (one initiates, the other tolerates).
- Public-affection norms diverge (one is visibly affectionate; the
  other is reserved).

## 3. The Composite Translation Cost

For any pair `(P1, P2)`, define the three sub-scores:

- $E(P_1, P_2) \in [0, 1]$ — embodied alignment
- $L(P_1, P_2) \in [0, 1]$ — linguistic alignment
- $A(P_1, P_2) \in [0, 1]$ — affection alignment

A first-pass composite:

$$\text{TC}(P_1, P_2) = 3 - \big(E + L + A\big)$$

so $\text{TC} = 0$ when all three are fully aligned, and $\text{TC} = 3$
when all three are maximally divergent. **Lower TC = lower
translation cost = easier relational bandwidth.**

> **Heuristic only.** Equal weighting is a placeholder. Future
> revisions may weight differently — e.g. affection often dominates
> embodied in long partnerships.

## 4. The False-Positive Lesson (anti-shadow)

The crucial anti-shadow the heuristic is designed to install:

> **Low TC ≠ evidence of sexual orientation. High TC ≠ evidence
> against it.**

Two diagnostic false-positive cases the heuristic predicts and the
orthogonal-axes framework forbids conflating:

**Case α — High-TC, same-sex-oriented partnership.** Two humans with
identical sexual orientation can ship *terrible* bandwidth if their
embodied/linguistic/affection registers do not align. The relationship
might be tender, committed, even lifelong — but laborious, with
constant translation overhead. Orientation and quality are
independent.

**Case β — Low-TC, opposite-sex-oriented deep friendship.** Two humans
of different sexes can share enormous embodied register (gym, dance,
sport), high linguistic alignment (shared mother tongue, humour), and
high affection register (touch, embrace, sleeping proximity) — *with
zero erotic content*. The bandwidth is real. The orientation is not
what the bandwidth would naïvely suggest.

A diagnostic system that infers orientation from bandwidth alone will
fail both cases in opposite directions.

---

## 5. What The Heuristic IS Useful For

Used within the orthogonal-axes framework, the heuristic **does**
produce useful statements:

- **Predicting friction.** High TC pairs should expect higher
  translation overhead and more repair work.
- **Diagnosing failure modes.** When a relationship fails, the
  heuristic localises the failure to a register (embodied /
  linguistic / affection) rather than blaming an axis (gender /
  orientation / quality).
- **Identifying growth edges.** A pair that *wants* lower TC can
  invest in shared practices in any of the three registers.
- **Diagnosing asynchrony.** When one partner has high E + L but the
  other has high A only, the asymmetry itself is the data.

These are honest, falsifiable predictions. The heuristic does **not**
license orientation claims.

## 6. Worked Example (Abang Sado + straight admirer, two iterations)

**Iteration 1 — Gym-buddy stage.**

- E ≈ 0.9 (shared lifting vocabulary, posture, training norms).
- L ≈ 0.6 (different mother tongues, but shared Manglish + slang).
- A ≈ 0.4 (some casual gym touch — high-fives, posture correction;
  not sleep-proximate).
- TC ≈ 1.1 — moderate bandwidth, low friction. Orientation
  irrelevant to the bandwidth.

**Iteration 2 — Long partnership stage.**

- E ≈ 0.95 (decades of shared craft).
- L ≈ 0.8 (decades of code-switching).
- A ≈ 0.8 (decades of physical affection availability).
- TC ≈ 0.45 — very high bandwidth. *Still orientation-agnostic.*

The heuristic predicts bandwidth growth with shared time. It refuses
to deduce orientation either way.

## 7. Open Questions (for the next revision)

These are deliberately left open in v0.1:

1. **Weighting.** Equal weights across E/L/A are a placeholder. Should
   affection dominate in long partnerships? Should embodied dominate
   in early-stage?
2. **Asymmetry.** Should $E(P_1, P_2)$ be a vector (one direction
   each way) instead of a scalar? Asymmetric pairs behave differently
   from symmetric ones.
3. **Cultural drift.** The affection register is highly culture-bound
   (BM Nusantara norms differ from Anglo-American norms differ from
   Northern European norms). How should the heuristic normalise?
4. **Temporal integration.** Translation cost is not stationary. Two
   humans *build* shared registers over time. The TC at year 0 is
   not the TC at year 10. The heuristic needs a temporal derivative.
5. **Failure-mode taxonomy.** When TC spikes in a previously low-TC
   pair, is that an axis shift, a register loss, or a temporal
   de-training? The heuristic needs a failure classifier.

These are explicitly **not** solved in this draft. Solving them
prematurely would inflate the model beyond its first-pass purpose.

## 8. Closing

> **Translation cost is a measure of bandwidth, not of desire. The
> orthogonal-axes framework guarantees that bandwidth and desire
> remain independent axes. The heuristic operationalises the
> bandwidth axis. It does not, and must not, speak for the desire
> axis.**
