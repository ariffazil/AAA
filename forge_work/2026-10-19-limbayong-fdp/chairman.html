Whether to continue committing irrevocable capital to Limbayong (FID taken 28 August 2026) before three specific facts are established.

Recommendation: HOLD capital release for six months. Gate on 1 April 2027.

2. THE EQUITY QUESTION — ANSWERED FIRST, BECAUSE IT IS THE REAL QUESTION

You asked what equity assumption sits under this project. The answer is the finding:

The equity is 100% PETRONAS Carigali. There is no partner. There will not be one.

That is not a modelling convenience. It is a structural fact, and it is the most important fact in this paper.

Why 100% is not a choice

A 100% interest is normally a strategic decision. Here it is a residual — the position left over after everyone else declined.

Section 7 of the underlying study ran the numbers from a foreign contractor’s seat. On a contractor basis, Limbayong needs US$96/bbl Brent to return 10%. Not 15% — 10%. Brent has exceeded $96 for a minority of the last decade. That is why the field was discovered in 2002, had a subsea contract awarded in 2021, watched its FPSO tender collapse twice, was declared “commercially unviable as a standalone” in 2023, and only reached FID in 2026 — when a used hull was bought for US$35 million.

I stress-tested whether a farm-out could fix this. For a 49% partner to reach a 15% IRR at $70 Brent, the fiscal terms required are beyond anything Malaysia publishes. The project does not generate enough value at $70 to pay a partner a commercial return and leave PETRONAS whole. Any farm-out would be a transfer of value from the nation to a private party, dressed as risk transfer. If a farm-out proposal reaches your board, that is the question to ask it.

What this means for the equity assumption

Every model in this pack — mine and the eight technical sections — runs at 100% equity, no debt, 10% real discount rate. That is correct for PCSB. But it carries a consequence that a chairman should see clearly:

There is no risk-sharing layer between this project and the national balance sheet.

No partner to share the overrun. No lender to test the assumptions. No joint-venture committee to ask the awkward question. When a 100%-owned project is wrong, it is wrong in private, and nobody outside finds out until the write-down.

6. THE ARGUMENT FOR PROCEEDING ANYWAY — AND ITS HONEST PRICE

I would be a poor shadow chairman if I only gave you the numbers that support a HOLD. There is a real case for proceeding, and it is not the one in the press release.

The strategic case: PCSB is 100% WI, it is the operator, and it has never led a Malaysian deepwater development. A host at Limbayong is the only infrastructure that monetises Bestari and anything else found along the L-B-P trend. A host for one marginal field is a stranded asset. A host with a second customer is a business. Judged on that basis, Limbayong is not a field development — it is the entry ticket to Sabah deepwater as an operator.

The price of that argument: it is a portfolio and capability decision, and it should be approved as one. It should not be approved on the oil economics, because the oil economics do not support it at any price below $88.

A sovereign operator is entitled to make strategic investments. It is not entitled to describe them as commercial ones. If the board approves Limbayong, approve it as a capability investment with a stated strategic return, not as an NPV-positive project. Then the accountability is honest, and the next chairman inherits a decision that can be evaluated against what it claimed.

7. WHAT I COULD NOT SEE

Stated plainly, because a paper that hides its blindness is worse than no paper.

  • No PETRONAS reservoir model, well proposal, AFE, FDP, or gas contract.
  • No MMHE conversion estimate. The number I most needed, I could not find.
  • No class survey or condition report on Excalibur after six years laid up.
  • No PSC text. Deepwater R/C terms are bespoke and not published; I modelled a generic cost-recovery structure with a profitability sliding scale.
  • No well performance data from any Malaysian deepwater analogue.
  • Every subsurface number is analogue-derived. There is no public seismic, log, core or PVT for this field, and I have not invented one.

A full FDP would cost US$15–40M and take 12–18 months of institutional work. This paper cost a fraction of that and answers a different question: not “what is the answer” but “what would we need to know before the answer can be trusted.”

The asymmetry is the whole argument. Six months and US$60M, to avoid the possibility of committing US$4.05bn to a project whose central facility cost is unknown, whose gas has no buyer, and whose break-even sits US$17 below today’s oil price.
