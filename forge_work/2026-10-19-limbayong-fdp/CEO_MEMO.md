## 1. THE VERDICT — HOLD

**Do not commit irrevocable capex until three numbers are known. On every central case in this study, Limbayong fails a 15% real hurdle, and most cases fail 10%.**

| Source | Basis | NPV10 | IRR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Section 7 (contractor, with 60% investment allowance) | capex $2.9bn | **+$288M** | 12.3% |
| Section 7 (contractor, allowance stripped) | capex $2.9bn | −$65M | 9.5% |
| Section 7 (PETRONAS consolidated) | capex $2.9bn | +$1,592M | 19.8% |
| Section 8 (base) | capex $2.9bn | +$313M | 12.1% |
| **HERMES reconciliation (consolidated, no allowance credit)** | **capex $4.05bn** | **−$839M** | **4.8%** |
| Section 8 Monte Carlo (20,000 paths) | Class 4 inputs | mean −$727M | **P(NPV<0) = 80.5%** |

**The spread is not disagreement. It is three different questions:** contractor-with-incentive, contractor-without, and sovereign-consolidated. All three point the same way — the project is marginal at best, and on a contractor basis it needs **US$96/bbl Brent** to reach 10%.

## 2. THE NUMBER THAT DECIDES IT: CAPEX

The largest disagreement inside this study is capex. Section 6 builds **US$4.05bn P50 (range $2.83–6.07bn)** from a WBS. Sections 7 and 8 both used **US$2.9bn**.

**US$15/boe is the industry-typical deepwater number. US$10.5/boe is not.** Sections 7 and 8's $2.9bn sits below the global range; Section 6's $4.05bn sits inside it. Until PETRONAS's own estimate is seen, **the honest central case is $4.05bn**, and at that number the project returns 4.8%.

## 3. THE THREE FINDINGS THAT CHANGE THE DECISION

**Finding 1 — the hull is undersized, and the yard is already fixed.**
FPSO *Excalibur*: built 2007, Sevan-type cylindrical, 70×70×27 m, **300,000 bbl storage**, originally **~30,000 bopd** on Chestnut [M: DNeX Bursa filing 20 Aug 2026; Sevan press]. Limbayong design needs **40,000 bopd and 150 mmscfd**. That is a **31% oil shortfall, 50% storage shortfall, and no gas handling** — Chestnut was oil-only. Delivery is contractually fixed at **MMHE Pasir Gudang by 5 Nov 2026**. MISC bought the *form*, not the *capacity*. Conversion estimated **US$600M (range $475–890M)**.

**Finding 2 — this is a gas field with an oil rim, not an oil field with a gas cap.**
Section 1 derives gas GRV at **1.86× the oil-leg GRV**; most of the 790.9 bcf is non-associated. Section 7 quantifies the consequence: **oil-only NPV −$29M / 9.7% IRR; gas adds $317M — 110% of total NPV.** And there is **no public gas sales agreement and no defined export route**, with a ~140–180 km subsea tie to SSGP Kimanis the only credible path.

**Finding 3 — 2029 first oil is not credible.**
Section 6 gives **Q4 2030** as P50 (51 months from FID). Kikeh took 43 months, Gumusut-Kakap 44, Malikai 46 — all clean newbuilds. A used cylindrical hull converting in a yard with no cylindrical-conversion reference beating all three by 7–10 months is not a schedule.

## 4. TWO ERRORS FOUND INSIDE THIS STUDY

I audit my own subagents. Two defects:

**Error A — a 2011 newspaper article presented as current evidence.** Section 8 cited an **"MMHE RM952M MISC contract (19 Oct 2026)"** as partial conversion coverage. That article is from **2011**; 19 Oct 2026 is the page render date. Its two contracts are **MT Onozo → FPSO Cendor (Terengganu)** and **SS Tenaga Satu → FSU Lekas (Melaka)** — neither is Excalibur, neither is Limbayong. [Verified: article body references FY ended 31 March 2011.] **Claim withdrawn.**

**Error B — a capex used before it was built.** Sections 7 and 8 ran economics on $2.9bn before Section 6 delivered $4.05bn. That is a sequencing failure by me. Section 2 of this memo is the correction.

Neither error changes the direction of the verdict. Both change its confidence.

## 7. THE ACTION

**HOLD irrevocable capex. Open a six-month assurance window. Gate 1 on 1 April 2027.**

Three deliverables by that date, total ~US$60M:

1. **Independent cost assurance** against the $4.05bn WBS. If real capex > $3.5bn → NO-GO or re-scope.
2. **Conversion scope to lump-sum** at MMHE, including gas handling and the 40 kbopd topsides. An open-ended conversion on a used hull is a cost-overrun generator.
3. **Gas sales agreement**, or a written decision that the gas is stranded and a re-run of economics without it. This is US$317M, not a rounding error.

If all three land favourably, GO with a full FDP. If any fails, the project is a stranded-hull write-down, not a development.

## 8. THE STRATEGIC CASE, STATED HONESTLY

No economic model in this study justifies Limbayong as a standalone oil project. **But it may still be correct.**

PETRONAS Carigali is 100% WI. It is the operator. It has never led a Malaysian deepwater development. A host facility at Limbayong is the **only** way to monetise Bestari and any future discovery in the L-B-P trend. A host built for one marginal field is a stranded asset; a host with a second customer is infrastructure.

**That is a defensible reason to sanction. It is not the reason given publicly.** The public framing is oil economics and a US$35M bargain. The real case is portfolio and capability. Both can be true — but a sovereign operator should say which one it is buying, because they carry different hurdle rates and different failure modes.

## 9. WHAT THIS IS NOT

No PETRONAS reservoir model, well proposal, AFE, FDP, or gas contract was available. Every cost is benchmarked; every reservoir figure is analogue-derived; fiscal terms are generic (1993 deepwater PSC vintage assumed). Section 8 sets out the US$170–240M assurance programme a real FDP would require against the 12-month institutional norm. **Treat the verdict as a structure for a decision, and the numbers as bounds awaiting the operator's own data.**

## APPENDIX A — THE EXXONMOBIL CALIBRATION

The requested toolkit exists. It is an **equity-analyst toolkit for modelling XOM's corporate earnings**, not a field-development model: no DCF, no IRR, no PSC, no decline curve, no WBS. What it publishes, and what was used:

| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Brent earnings sensitivity | +$1/bbl = +$700M annual Upstream | Sensitivities 2Q26, p3 |
| Implied effective take | **55.4%** ($0.446/boe per $1/bbl) | derived from 4.3 Moebd |
| Constant price basis 2024 | **$65/bbl Brent** flat real | Sensitivities p4 |
| Constant price basis 2030 | $75.40/bbl (2.5% inflation) | Sensitivities p4 |
| Upstream unit earnings | ~$10/oeb at $65 flat | Modeling Basics p8 |

**It corrected us twice.** First: my model's implied government take was 42.1% against XOM's disclosed 55.4% — a 13-point gap that flattered the project. Second, and decisive: a 55% take is the **wrong basis** for a 100%-owned sovereign field, which is why contractor and consolidated economics diverge so violently here (5.0% vs 17.3% IRR on the same field).

**What this model does that the toolkit cannot:** project DCF, IRR, NPV, PSC cost recovery and profit split, explicit decline, capex WBS, fiscal regime. A corporate earnings bridge is not a project gate. Using its published sensitivity to calibrate the gate is exactly right.
