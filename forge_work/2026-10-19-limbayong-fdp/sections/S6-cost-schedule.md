# S6 — COST & SCHEDULE (AACE CLASS 4)

**Project:** Limbayong Deepwater Development, Blocks G/J/R, offshore Sabah (~120 km NW, 900–1,200 m WD)
**Operator:** PETRONAS Carigali Sdn Bhd (100% WI). **FID:** 20 Aug 2026.
**Basis:** USD, real 2026 terms. Escalation shown as a separate line so the estimate stays in constant money.
**Estimate class:** AACE International 18R-97 **Class 4** — accuracy −15% to −30% low / +20% to +50% high. The band used here is **−30% / +50%** (the broad end of Class 4, justified by the absence of a public FDP, no public well count, no public FPSO capacity, and a used-hull conversion on an unverified vessel). Maturity level: 30–50% of definitive definition.

> **CONTRADICTION WITH FACT BASE — FLAGGED.** FACT_BASE.md line 33 records 2023 "commercially unviable as originally proposed standalone FPSO" and line 42–44 record Excalibur acquisition + FID in Aug 2026. That is the correct sequence and my schedule is built on it. But the FID was taken **before** the subsea ceiling was halved (US$250–500M in 2021 → US$75–250M in 2026) on the **same** iEPCI/Subsea 2.0 model. A smaller subsea commitment at FID, on a project that already failed once on cost, is not a scope optimisation — it is a **staged commitment with the expensive half (floater + wells) still unhedged**. That single fact drives both the cost risk and the schedule risk below.

**Critical path:** FPSO conversion (Feb 2027 → Aug 2029) → hook-up → commissioning → first oil Nov 2030. **Near-critical:** rig availability. If the drillship is not contracted by Q1 2027, first oil moves month-for-month into 2031 — the wells chain has only ~4 months of float against the floater.

**Why 51 months and not 36.** The three Malaysian deepwater benchmarks all beat this: Kikeh 43 months (FID 2004 → first oil Aug 2007), Gumusut-Kakap ~44 months (FID 2011 → Nov 2014), Malikai 46 months (FID Feb 2013 → Dec 2016). Every one of those was a **newbuild or purpose-built floater with a clean hull**. Limbayong is a **1990s-vintage used cylindrical hull**, on a reservoir the operator itself calls *top decile complexity*, in a yard with no cylindrical-conversion reference. Pricing this at 36 months would be assuming Limbayong executes better than three projects that all executed well and still took 43–46.

**What the table actually says.** Limbayong sits *between* Malikai (US$16.4/boe) and Gumusut-Kakap (US$6.8/boe) on capex per barrel of resource — which looks acceptable. It is **2.7× Malikai and 4.5× Kikeh on capex per flowing barrel**, and that is the metric that governs payback. The reason is structural: 270 MMboe drained through a 40,000 bopd / 150 mmscfd facility means a short plateau and a long decline tail, so every dollar of facility capital is spread over fewer high-rate days. **This is what "marginal" means in the operator's own SPE 210079 title.** The project is not expensive per barrel in the ground. It is expensive per barrel per day.

**Overrun statistics — deepwater.** IPA's 2012 industry study (published via SPE 153695 / OTC-Brazil 29804) found **78% of upstream projects in 2011 experienced either a cost overrun or a schedule slip** [M]. EY's 2014 analysis put **64% of multibillion-dollar, technically challenging oil and gas projects into overrun or delay** [M, EY release], with the industry-wide exposure above US$500bn [M]. IPA's consistent finding across vintages: **deepwater and arctic megaprojects are the worst-performing category**, with median cost growth in the +20–40% band and schedule slip of 12–24 months [A]. Applied to a US$4.05bn P50, that median experience alone puts the expected outturn at **US$4.9–5.7bn**, which sits inside — but in the upper half of — my +50% ceiling. That is the honest read: **the +50% case is not a stress case, it is the median historical outcome for this asset class.**

## KEY JUDGEMENTS

1. **US$4.05bn is the number, and the −30% side is barely reachable.** Ten deepwater wells at US$95M each and a US$755M converted cylindrical FPSO are floor-bound. The credible range is really US$3.6–6.1bn; the −30% case (US$2.83bn) requires deferring the gas line *and* a clean hull survey *and* a soft rig market simultaneously.

2. **The subsea contract halving is the tell, and it is not good news.** Same contractor, same Subsea 2.0 model, ceiling cut from US$250–500M to US$75–250M between 2021 and 2026. That is not efficiency — Subsea 2.0 pricing did not halve. It means **less scope is being bought at FID**. My SURF line (US$640M) is ~2.5–8× the announced contract band, and the gap has to be reconciled either as separate tree frame agreements, a separate installation EPCI, or scope that is not yet contracted. Any of those three answers raises, not lowers, the risk that SURF is under-budgeted at sanction.

3. **The FPSO is the cost and schedule problem, not the wells.** A US$35M used cylindrical hull bought from a UK North Sea operator is a hull with an unknown steel-renewal liability, and a cylindrical conversion is a specialist job with essentially no Malaysian reference. I have carried 31 months and US$550M for conversion. A clean special survey could save US$80M and 4 months; a bad one adds US$200M and 9 months. **This is a binary I cannot resolve from public data — mark it [U] and manage it.**

4. **First oil Q4 2030, and the operator will say 2029.** The 36-month case requires beating Kikeh, Gumusut-Kakap and Malikai — all clean-hull projects — by 7–10 months. It is not credible. Budget the cash flow on 2030 and treat 2029 as upside.

5. **The gas line is the swing scope item.** US$300M for a ~120 km wet-gas export line is a third of the FPSO cost and is only justified if the 790 bcf is monetised at a price that clears the tariff. If gas is deferred, capex drops to ~US$3.75bn and capex/boe on liquids alone (139 MMbbl) rises to US$27/boe — which is where the project starts to look uninvestable. **The gas is not a bonus; on this cost base it is what makes the barrel economics work.**

6. **Full-cycle breakeven is roughly US$48–56/bbl [D]** at US$4.05bn forward + US$570M sunk, 270 MMboe, 15-year life, 10% discount rate, US$4–6/mmbtu gas. That is a thin margin against a US$65–75 deck for a project with a +50% cost tail. **The correct verdict from S6 alone is conditional GO only if the hull survey and the rig contract are both closed before the first major FPSO payment.**
