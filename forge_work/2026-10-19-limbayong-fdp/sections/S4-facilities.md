# S4 — FACILITIES & FPSO

**Subject:** Limbayong Deepwater Development (Block G, ~120 km offshore Sabah; centroid 5.888 N, 114.510 E; 900–1,200 m water depth; oil + non-associated gas; 10 deepwater wells tied back; FID 28 Aug 2026 per Upstream).

> **DATA CONFLICT TO SURFACE** (per FACT_BASE hard-rule 6): The DNeX Bursa filing of 20 Aug 2026 — the primary, audited source — describes Excalibur as a **70 m × 70 m × 27 m cylindrical FPSO** with **300,000 bbl design storage**. Offshore Magazine (4 Aug 2022, trade press) reports **60 m diameter / 270,000 bbl / 25,000 bopd**. I have used the Bursa values as primary [M] and reported the trade-press figures as an [A] lower bound because (a) the Bursa filing is signed and audited and (b) the 60 m / 270 k numbers cannot be reconciled with a 70 m × 70 m × 27 m box (circumference 220 m, beam 70 m is incompatible with 60 m diameter). Owner-supplied data trumps trade press in this section.

---

## HEADLINE

The Excalibur is a **proven, used Sevan-type cylindrical FPSO** — the *right geometric form* for a harsh-environment Malaysian deepwater host but **the wrong size**: nameplate oil-processing capacity (~27,500 bopd) is **~31 % short** of the 40,000 bopd the field needs, storage is **~50 % short** of the 600,000 bbl Limbayong spec, and the hull has **zero gas-handling / no water-injection upgrade** for a 150 mmscfd gas + 75,000 bwpd WI duty. FID has nevertheless been taken, MISC is buying the hull, and the tow is contracted to **MMHE Pasir Gudang** (named in the MOA as the delivery quayside), so the concept is locked. **Recommendation: PROCEED but rebid the processing topsides as a Step 2 fabrication to give a credible route to the 40 k / 150 mmscfd / 75 kbwd design duty**, and route non-associated gas to the **Sabah–Sarawak Gas Pipeline (SSGP) at Kimanis** (already 500 km, 750 mmscfd class) via a ~120–150 km subsea tie from the FPSO. Conversion CAPEX US$300–500 M, 24–30 months from yard-cut to first-oil, AACE Class 5 (-30 %/+50 %).

## CONVERSION SCOPE & COST

**Yard:** Malaysia Marine & Heavy Engineering (MMHE), Pasir Gudang, Johor. **Named in the MOA as the delivery quayside** (DNeX Bursa filing Appendix I). MMHE is the MISC in-house yard and has done every MISC FPSO conversion for two decades (FPSO Cendor, FPSO Ruby, FPSO Peris). No FID-grade alternative yard is on the table.

**Scope (brownfield, 19-year-old hull):**

1. **Hull integrity recertification** — full class re-entry survey, hull UT 100 % near critical nodes, turret bearing / mooring chain / anchor leg replacement or recertification, blasting + re-coating. The hull was warm-stacked since ~2022, which is *less* corrosive than cold-stack but the mooring system has had no load cycle in 4+ years.
2. **Topside removal, redesign, rebuild, reinstall** — Excalibur's Chestnut-era topsides were ~3,000 t and designed for 30 k bopd / 20 k bwpd / zero gas. Limbayong needs 40 k bopd + 75 k bwpd + 150 mmscfd gas + 600 kbbl storage. **All of the processing train, all of the gas train, and the bulk of the water-injection train is new**. Practical scope: 5,500–8,500 t of new topsides, integrated onto the cylindrical deck (3,500 m² usable).
3. **Storage upgrade** — 300 k bbl as-built vs 600 k bbl required. Cylindrical hulls have a fixed central-storage ring sized at build. *You cannot add 300,000 bbl to a 300,000 bbl hull.* This is the single most important engineering decision in the project and the Section recommendation must address it directly: either accept a **6-day cargo cycle** (vs the 14-day cycle the design spec implies) or design the additional storage as a **calm-barge shuttle alongside** (capex +US$150–250 M, schedule +8–12 mo).
4. **Power generation** — full gas-turbine train replacement, with waste-heat recovery if Tier-4 emissions. Hull was electric-drive when new (Sevan spec) but no published nameplate MW.
5. **Gas train** — *new build*: slug catcher, multiphase pumps, gas conditioning, glycol regeneration, vent/flare, gas metering skid. The Sabah non-associated gas is CO₂-bearing and the project is marginal, so the gas train should be sized for 150 mmscfd at peak day, not design day.
6. **Water injection** — new 75 kbwpd HP pumps, filters, deoxygenation, scaling control.
7. **Mooring** — Internal turret was designed for North Sea metocean; Sabah monsoon and deepwater cyclones impose different loads (typhoon swells). Re-engineer the chain size and anchor pattern; expect all 9 legs to be replaced.
8. **Marine systems** — helideck (likely upgrade to Sikorsky S-92 class), lifeboats to current SOLAS, dynamic positioning interface (if any), offloading system — either tandem off-take to a shuttle tanker (current Anaconda-style system was a Sevan feature) or new SOFEC CALM buoy (capex impact).
9. **Commissioning & sail-away** — incl. 2-tow trip: Nigg → Pasir Gudang (heavy-lift vessel, ~30 days, weather window) and Pasir Gudang → Limbayong field (~10 days, wet tow, monsoon-sensitive).

**Schedule (Class 5):**

- Tow Scotland → Pasir Gudang: Q1 2027 (30–45 days, weather-window bound)
- Yard-cut to sail-away at MMHE: **18–24 months** (FPSO conversion benchmark, PaxOcean/MISC norms; Sevan cylinder deck is *easier* than a tanker conversion because there is no flare tower relocation, *harder* because the integrated deck and turret must be preserved)
- Sail-away to first oil: 3–4 months (tow + hook-up + commissioning)
- **Total: 24–30 months from FID**, putting first oil between **Q4 2028 and Q2 2029** if FID-day is 28 Aug 2026.

**Brownfield integrity risk — the elephant in the room:**

- The hull is **19 years old at sail-away** and **38 years old at end-of-field-life** if Limbayong produces 10 years. Class societies typically grant 5-year recertification cycles; 38 years is **beyond the design life of the original fatigue analysis** even though a Sevan cylinder sees lower cyclic loading than a monohull.
- The vessel was warm-stacked (Nigg, Scotland) — better than cold-stack (no anchoring cycles, no mooring fatigue) but **the topsides were never designed for tropical duty**, and the gas train is brand-new. The classic Sevan integrity watch-outs are: (i) **bilge-keel / column welds** in way of the cylindrical hull-to-deck joint — re-inspect 100 %; (ii) **turret bearing wear** after 4 years static; (iii) **riser balcony fatigue** in the new configuration.
- The cylindrical hull's *only* integrity advantage over a tanker-conversion is **no monolithic flare tower / no midship strength-rail modifications** — the Sevan design already integrates everything inside the 70 m disk, so there is no heavy structural cutting.

**Why the cylinder is the right call** — the Sevan geometry concentrates topsides mass over the moonpool/turret, gives a low-motion hull (better uptime in Sabah's monsoon and typhoon swells), and is the only FPSO form MISC owns in this size class. The fact that the hull is **used** is not a defect — it is the *only* way a marginal field gets an FPSO at all. Industry norm: 40–50 % of new FPSO projects in SE Asia / NW Australia in 2024–2026 are used-hull conversions (PaxOcean, MMHE, Keppel order books).

**Why storage is a non-fatal gap** — Limbayong's 139 MMbbl oil at 40 k bopd is 9.5 years of plateau; a 6-day shuttle cycle = 110 shuttle calls over field life = ~US$1.1 B of shuttle-tanker capex+opex (at US$10 M/yr charter × 110/12 yr). A SOFEC buoy alongside would push storage to 600 k bbl but adds US$150–250 M and 8–12 months. **Recommend: shuttle-tanker offtake at 6-day cycle for first 5 years; review SOFEC at 50 % field-life if oil price supports it.**

## GAS HANDLING & EVACUATION ROUTE

**Spec:** 150 mmscfd of non-associated gas at peak, ~54.7 bcf/yr at design (per FACT_BASE §VOLUMES). 790.9 bcf remaining gas reserves (FACT_BASE, GEM 2023) at 54.7 bcf/yr = **14.5 yr gas plateau**, longer than the oil plateau.

**Route recommendation: dedicated subsea gas export pipeline from the FPSO to the Sabah–Sarawak Gas Pipeline (SSGP) at the Kimanis landfall**, ~140–180 km of 16″–20″ pipeline, with a pipeline-end-manifold (PLEM) tie-in. The SSGP has been operating since 2014, is 500 km Kimanis → Bintulu (MLNG), 750 mmscfd class, and currently has spare capacity given the Kebabangan / Kamunsu East decline curves.

**Why not (a) re-inject all gas**: reservoir pressure support is required (the wells are deepwater toe-thrust, compartmentalised, water-injection-supported per FACT_BASE), but a 150 mmscfd gas-handling train is already spec'd. Flaring would breach PETRONAS zero-routine-flare; reinjecting 100 % requires extra compression and reduces sales-gas NPV.

**Why not (b) send to Bintulu LNG via SSGP directly**: 500 km transit with multiple offtake points; capacity is shared with Petronas upstream gas (Kebabangan, Bintulu Clastic, others). The Limbayong volume (54.7 bcf/yr) is meaningful — ~7 % of SSGP throughput — so a **dedicated tie-in + booking** would be needed and a pipeline capacity agreement with PETRONAS Energy Malaysia.

**Why not (c) re-inject and re-inject, no export**: leaks 790 bcf of revenue; gas-to-power for Sabah domestic has been the policy since 2014 (Borneo Post 2013).

**Decision:** Build the export pipeline. Indicative capex for a 16″–18″, 150 km subsea line is US$250–400 M (subsea 7″ equiv benchmark ~US$1.5–2.0 M/km × 1.5 for 16–18″ × 150 km + PLEM + SSGP tie-in skid). This is **not in the S4 conversion capex above**; it belongs in SURF / midstream. The FPSO topsides need only the slug catcher, gas conditioning, and metering skid (already in the US$40–80 M gas-train line).

**Evacuation route (offshore personnel):** Crew change by helicopter to a Sabah onshore base (Kota Kinabalu or Labuan, ~120 km), S-92 or H175 class on helideck sized to current CAP 437. Emergency evacuation: lifeboats to a stand-by vessel, then to shore. The cylindrical hull has **360° access around the deck** which materially improves evacuation geometry vs. a monohull FPSO with a bow turret.

## KEY JUDGEMENTS

1. **The cylindrical form is correct; the size is short.** Sevan geometry is the only credible answer for a marginal Sabah deepwater field; the project lives or dies on a credible **Step-2 topsides rebid** that closes the 40 k bopd / 150 mmscfd / 75 k bwpd gap.
2. **Storage gap is solvable with shuttle-tanker cadence, not a SOFEC buoy.** 6-day cycle is operationally fine for 9.5 years; the SOFEC decision can be deferred to 50 % field-life.
3. **The hull age is the *manageable* risk, not the project-killer.** 19 years at sail-away is within the range of class society recertification practice; the *real* risk is mooring-system redesign for Sabah metocean, not the cylinder itself.
4. **Gas export to SSGP-Kimanis is the gas-NPV unlock.** Without the export pipeline, 790 bcf of reserves is stranded gas; with it, the gas NPV roughly equals the oil NPV at current Malaysian regulated prices.
5. **FID has been taken; the alternative analysis is for the *next* FDP, not this one.** All three rejects above are correctly archived for the Bestari and L-B-P Phase 2 development plan.

## WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND

- If a credible bidder offers a **newbuild 70 m Sevan-type cylinder at ≤ US$900 M all-in with ≤ 30 months to first oil**, switch from conversion to newbuild (storage, gas, WI all spec-fit, no integrity compromise).
- If SSGP capacity is *fully booked* when PETRONAS Energy Malaysia confirms (expected Q1 2027), the gas export NPV collapses and the project reverts to **gas-reinjection + reduced oil plateau**; the 40 k bopd design rate then requires a 150 mmscfd gas-injection compression train (capex +US$120–180 M) and the project slips below commercial threshold.
- If the DNeX-attributed **60 m / 270 k bbl / 25 k bopd** numbers turn out to be the *post-2022 Avalon-deck-removal* as-built state (rather than a trade-press error), the capacity gap widens to ~38 % on oil and the Step-2 rebid is no longer optional — it is the project.
- If the independent ABS class survey at MMHE finds **hull thickness below class-acceptable** at the column-to-deck weld (the classic Sevan fatigue hot-spot), the hull is scrap and MISC must source a substitute cylinder (effectively ending the project as currently scoped).
- If **Malikai TLP topsides are decommissioned before 2030** (Shell exit announcements in 2025–2026 are tracked), then a tie-back to Malikai becomes technically feasible for Limbayong *if* the TLP can be re-topsides for 40 k bopd + 150 mmscfd — at ~US$600–900 M all-in, this becomes the **lowest-cost alternative** to the conversion and the recommendation must flip.
