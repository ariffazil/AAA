# S7X — ECONOMICS, RECALIBRATED AGAINST EXXONMOBIL'S MODELING TOOLKIT

## HEADLINE

The ExxonMobil Modeling Toolkit is not a field-development economics model — it is an
equity-analyst toolkit for modelling XOM's corporate earnings. But it publishes two numbers
that let us calibrate ours, and applying them **overturns the verdict in my first memo.**
My earlier conclusion (NPV10 +US$61M, 10.5% IRR, "not a value project") used the **wrong
fiscal basis**: I applied a foreign contractor's terms to a field PETRONAS owns 100%.
Corrected, Limbayong returns **NPV10 +US$1.11bn and 17.3% IRR at US$70 Brent on a
PETRONAS-consolidated basis** — and it does not work for any foreign partner at any
realistic oil price. That is the real strategic fact.

## WHAT THE TOOLKIT ACTUALLY IS

| Document | What it contains | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| Modeling Basics (Jul 2026, 16pp) | How to model XOM's segmented earnings and cash flow | Not a project DCF |
| Sensitivities & Key Assumptions (2Q26, 12pp) | Annual earnings sensitivities, reference price deck, unit earnings | Not a fiscal model |
| Interactive Analyst Center | 5yr historical + guidance, downloadable | Corporate only |

Method published on p5: **Volume × Unit earnings ± (price difference × sensitivity) = earnings**,
then **+ DD&A − cash capex = cash flow**. That is a corporate trickle-down, not a field
development cash-flow model. There is no IRR, no NPV, no PSC, no production decline curve,
no project WBS. [M]

## THE TWO NUMBERS THAT MATTER

**1. Brent earnings sensitivity.** XOM discloses: *increase of $1/bbl Brent = +$700M annual
Upstream earnings*, based on ~4.3 Moebd 2024 production. [M: Sensitivities p3; Modeling
Basics p8]

```
$700M / (4.3 Mboe/d × 365) = $0.446 per boe, per $1/bbl Brent
```

**44.6 cents of every Brent dollar reaches XOM's net income. 55.4% is taken by governments,
hosts, and price-linked entitlement effects.** That is the single most useful benchmark in
the document — it is a real, disclosed, audited effective-take number from the largest
integrated in the world, across a portfolio of PSCs, concessions, and OPEC terms.

**2. Reference price deck.** XOM's constant-price basis [M: Sensitivities p4]:
2024 = **$65/bbl Brent flat real**, $3.00/mmbtu Henry Hub, $6.50/mmbtu TTF
2030 = **$75.40/bbl Brent**, $3.50 HH, $7.50 TTF (2.5% inflation, 2024 reference year)

This is not a forecast. XOM says so explicitly. But it is the deck the largest listed
integrated uses to compare projects internally — which makes it a far more defensible
screening deck than today's US$105 spot.

**3. Unit earnings at $65 Brent: ~$10/oeb** [M: Modeling Basics p8]. XOM makes about
US$10 per barrel of oil equivalent at US$65 flat. That is an integrated, tax-paid,
portfolio-wide number.

## THE CALIBRATION TEST

My model's implied government take was **42.1%**. XOM's disclosed effective take is
**55.4%**. A **13.3-point gap** — my model was flattering the project.

| | Effective take | NPV10 @ $70 | IRR |
|---|---|---|---|
| My original model | 42.1% | +$61M | 10.5% |
| Calibrated to XOM's 55.4% | 54.6% | **−$649M** | **3.7%** |

Read that again. **Calibrating to XOM's disclosed sensitivity turns a marginal project into
a dead one.** That is what a real benchmark does — it does not confirm you, it corrects you.

## THE BASIS ERROR — AND WHY IT FLIPS THE ANSWER

Limbayong is **PETRONAS Carigali, 100% working interest, operator** [M: Welligence]. There
is no foreign partner. So "government take" is PETRONAS paying PETRONAS. Applying
contractor fiscal terms to it is a category error — and I made it.

| Basis | Who is the investor | NPV10 @ $70 | IRR @ $70 | Brent needed for 10% IRR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Contractor (foreign PSC holder) | An external company | **−$531M** | 5.0% | **$96/bbl** |
| PETRONAS consolidated | PETRONAS itself | **+$1,113M** | 17.3% | **$45/bbl** |

<blockquote>
Limbayong is not an oil project that happens to be owned by PETRONAS. It is a PETRONAS
balance-sheet project that only exists because PETRONAS can be its own host. <strong>No
foreign partner would fund this field at any oil price below US$96.</strong> That single
fact explains the twenty-four years of delay better than any geological story.
</blockquote>

## PETRONAS-BASIS ECONOMICS (the decision basis)

Assumptions: 5% Sabah state sales tax on crude (real external leak), PITA 38% on taxable
income, 12-year straight-line DD&A, opex $12/boe, gas $6/mmbtu, 66,000 boe/d plateau for
6 years then 14%/yr decline, capex $3.17bn spread 2027–2030, first oil 2029.

| Brent | NPV10 (capex $3.17bn) | IRR | NPV10 (capex $5.0bn) | IRR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $50 | +$227M | 11.6% | −$910M | 5.7% |
| $60 | +$672M | 14.5% | −$444M | 8.0% |
| $65 | +$893M | 15.9% | −$215M | 9.0% |
| **$70** | **+$1,113M** | **17.3%** | **+$11M** | **10.1%** |
| $80 | +$1,551M | 19.9% | +$461M | 12.0% |
| $90 | +$1,987M | 22.4% | +$907M | 13.9% |
| $105 | +$2,640M | 26.1% | +$1,569M | 16.5% |

**Breakevens on PETRONAS basis (capex $3.17bn):**

| Hurdle | Brent required | Max capex at $70 Brent |
|---|---|---|
| 10% IRR | **$45/bbl** | $5,018M ($18.2/boe) |
| 15% IRR | **$62/bbl** | $3,605M ($13.1/boe) |
| 20% IRR | **$80/bbl** | $2,746M ($10.0/boe) |

**This is the crux.** The project clears 15% at $62 Brent *if* capex holds at $3.17bn. It
fails at $70 Brent if capex reaches $5.0bn. So the entire investment case rests on one
number nobody in public has: **the real capex.**

## CAPEX IS THE ONLY VARIABLE THAT MATTERS

| Capex | Capex/boe | NPV10 @ $70 | IRR |
|---|---|---|---|
| $2.5bn | $9.1 | +$1,505M | 22.0% |
| $3.17bn | $11.5 | +$1,113M | 17.3% |

Global deepwater benchmark is typically **US$15–25/boe**. My $11.5/boe central estimate sits
*below* the industry range — which is suspicious, and is the single largest uncertainty in
this document. If Limbayong lands at industry-typical $18/boe, capex is $5.0bn and the
project returns **10.1% — exactly the cost of capital.**

## WHAT THE TOOLKIT GIVES US THAT WE DID NOT HAVE

1. **A disclosed effective-take benchmark (55.4%).** Every future PETRONAS project screen
   should be run through it before anything else. It is the cheapest sanity check available.
2. **A defensible reference deck ($65 flat real / $75.40 by 2030).** Screen at $65, not at
   spot. If it does not work at $65, it is a price bet.
3. **The Volume × Unit earnings decomposition.** Cleaner than my cash-flow stack: it
   separates *performance* (volume, unit cost) from *price* (sensitivity). Two different
   management problems, two different owners.
4. **XOM's breakeven definition**: "the oil price required for our cash flow to cover
   dividends and cash capex." That is a **cash-coverage** definition, not an NPV definition.
   It is a better question than IRR for a sovereign-owned operator, because PETRONAS does
   not have shareholders to satisfy — it has a national budget to fund.

The toolkit is a **corporate earnings bridge**, not a **project gate**. Using it as a gate

## KEY JUDGEMENTS

1. **Limbayong is value-accretive to PETRONAS and unavailable to anyone else.** At $70 Brent
   and $3.17bn capex: 17.3% IRR consolidated; 5.0% for a foreign partner who would need $96
   to reach 10%. Both numbers are true at once. [D]
2. **My first verdict was wrong on basis, and I am retracting it.** The corrected number is
   +$1.11bn, not +$61M. The error was applying contractor terms to a 100%-owned field. [D]
3. **Capex is the binding unknown.** $3.17bn clears 15%; $5.0bn clears 10%. Public data
   cannot resolve which. [D]/[U]
4. **At XOM's own reference deck of $65 flat real, the project returns 15.9%.** That is the
   honest screening result — above hurdle, not spectacular. [D]
5. **Gas is not cosmetic.** It is 39% of boe and worth ±$373M of NPV on a $3 price swing.
   An unsigned gas sales agreement is a US$373M hole in the sanction. [D]

## WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND

- Real capex above $5.0bn (project becomes an IRR-equals-WACC asset)
- Plateau below 55,000 boe/d sustained (NPV falls below $300M)
- Gas price below $5/mmbtu contractually locked
- A farm-out proposal: this model says no external partner can be found at these terms, so
  any farm-out is a value transfer, not a risk transfer
