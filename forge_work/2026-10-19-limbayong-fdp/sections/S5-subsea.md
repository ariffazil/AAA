## HEADLINE

**The full 10-well Limbayong SURF is a ~US$0.68 bn scope (Class 4: US$0.48–1.02 bn), not a US$75–250 M scope.** The 2026 TechnipFMC "significant" band is a *booking classification*, not a cost estimate, and cannot describe the full scope: it is 2.7× below even the best-case build-up at the top of the band. The 2021 "US$250–500 M" band, escalated to 2026 at 4.5%/yr, lands at US$623 M — within 9% of my bottom-up number. That convergence is the strongest signal in this section: **the 2021 award was approximately correctly priced for the scope, and the 2026 award is either a partial call-off, a phased booking, or excludes trees.** Any FDP economics built on "SURF ≈ US$250 M" understates subsea capex by roughly US$430 M.

Second headline: **the architecture that fits this field is not the cheapest one.** Three drill centres at 6.5–18 km, 900–1,200 m, waxy Sabah crude, and an operator commitment to tie back prospects at 18–30 km all point to multiplexed electro-hydraulic control, insulated flexible flowlines, lazy-wave flexible risers, and a single dynamic umbilical to a subsea distribution unit with spare capacity. Direct hydraulic is technically excluded at these offsets, not merely uneconomic.

## FIELD LAYOUT & ARCHITECTURE

### Layout concept [A]

No public field map, well trajectory, or discovery timeline exists for Limbayong. The layout below is an explicit assumption set, built to be internally consistent and to stress the cost model at a realistic step-out. It is not a claim about the actual field.

- **FPSO** at field centroid [A]; spread-moored or turret configuration unresolved [U] — the FPSO is being sourced as the used cylindrical hull *Excalibur* (US$35 M, DNeX→MISC, Aug 2026), and riser interface design depends on whether that hull carries a disconnectable turret. This is the single largest unhedged interface risk in S5.
- **DC-1 (Limbayong Main):** 4 wells, 4-slot manifold, 6.5 km.
- **DC-2 (Central):** 3 wells, 4-slot manifold, 12.0 km.
- **DC-3 (North/Satellite):** 3 wells, 4-slot manifold, 18.0 km.
- One 2-slot WI manifold at DC-1 serving both injectors.
- 2 spare manifold slots retained deliberately: PETRONAS stated the project "will pave the way for the development of the surrounding prospects within 18 to 30 km." Spare slots and umbilical spare tubes are strategic optionality for that stated follow-on, not gold-plating.

### Controls: multiplexed electro-hydraulic, not direct hydraulic [A]

Direct hydraulic is excluded on three independent grounds:
1. **Offset.** Direct hydraulic is practically limited to roughly 5 km and ~20 functions before response time and hydraulic lag become unacceptable. At 18 km with 10 wells it fails outright.
2. **Function count.** Each tree needs SCSSV, production master, wing, choke, annulus vent, crossover, plus sand/pressure/temperature monitoring. Ten wells × ~8 functions plus manifold valves exceeds any direct-hydraulic hose bundle that could be installed economically.
3. **Diagnostics.** No feedback path, no condition monitoring, no remote choke adjustment — unacceptable for a field whose operator has publicly flagged "top decile reservoir complexity" and marginal economics. Flexibility to re-choke individual wells without a vessel is worth more than the control-system saving.

Base case: dual redundant subsea electronics modules per manifold, 2 × 50% hydraulic supply, fibre-optic in the umbilical for distributed temperature/acoustic sensing on the 18 km production line (leak and slug detection). **All-electric actuation is technically available on Subsea 2.0 and would cut umbilical diameter and cost by roughly a third, but has no PETRONAS track record in Malaysia; treat as a post-FID value-engineering option, not the base case.** [A]

### Flexible vs rigid flowlines

**Base case: flexible, insulated, reel-layable** for all production, WI and GI lines. Reasons:
- 900–1,200 m and 18 km sits inside the comfortable flexible envelope; 8" ID at 10 ksi is standard product, not a development item.
- Compliant risers follow directly from flexible flowlines — no flex-joint, no riser base manifold, no SLWR buoyancy modules.
- Reel-lay installation is roughly 2–3× faster than J-lay at these lengths, and the Sabah weather window is the binding constraint.
- Insulation (5-layer PP, 50–70 mm) integrates into the pipe wall; pipe-in-pipe would be needed to match U-values with rigid steel.

**Rigid steel would only win** if (a) the gas export line to shore proceeds at 120 km — a 12–16" rigid SLWR plus pipeline is a different procurement and vessel class — or (b) reservoir pressure/temperature exceeds flexible limits (unknown: no public BHT/BHP for Limbayong). Flag as a falsifier, not a base case.

### Manifolds and jumpers

Three 4-slot production manifolds, 10 ksi, 1,200 m rated, with pigging loop and integrated sand detection. Fifteen jumpers: 10 tree-to-manifold, 3 manifold-to-flowline, 2 interconnect. All ROV-installable — **no saturation diving anywhere in this development**, which removes an entire cost and safety category and is a genuine advantage of the 900–1,200 m range.

### Risers: lazy-wave flexible, not free-standing

- 3 × 8" production, 3 × 6" WI/GI, all lazy-wave flexible, plus 1 dynamic umbilical.
- **Free-standing riser rejected.** A free-standing hybrid riser needs a buoyancy can, a foundation, and a flexible jumper to the FPSO; at 1,200 m it costs roughly US$80–150 M per tower. Justified only where riser count is high, pressure is extreme, or the host has severe motion. Here it would add US$100 M+ to solve a problem flexible lazy-wave already solves.
- **Steel lazy-wave riser (SLWR)** retained only for the deferred 120 km gas export route.

### Umbilical

Single 1.4 km dynamic section from FPSO to a subsea distribution unit, then static trunk and infield legs — 24.9 km total. This is cheaper than three parallel dynamic umbilicals (one per drill centre) and concentrates the fatigue-critical section in one place. Spare electrical and hydraulic cores and two spare 1" tubes are included for the 18–30 km prospect tie-backs.

**Cost shape.** Trees 15.4%, manifolds 10.8%, controls 7.0%, flowlines 17.6%, umbilical 4.7%, risers 5.7%, installation 13.2%, engineering+contingency 19.5%. This is a *hardware-heavy, install-light* profile — the opposite of a long single step-out, where Crondall Energy benchmarks put flowlines at 45%, umbilicals 30%, SPS 25% of SURF. The difference is structural: three clusters and ten trees load the SPS end. Anyone benchmarking Limbayong against a single 50 km tie-back will get the split wrong.

**Scope boundaries (deliberately excluded).** Drilling and completions (separate); FPSO hull, conversion, moorings and topsides (separate); 120 km gas export pipeline and shore terminal (deferred, Phase 2 — would add an estimated US$250–450 M); operating spares and 2-year IPS; flow-assurance chemicals; decommissioning. **If the 120 km export line is inside the sanctioned scope, S5 is understated by 35–65%.**

The 2021 number was reported as a *value band attached to a real scope*, and it survives escalation almost exactly. The 2026 number is a *booking class* — TechnipFMC's own defined tier — and a 10-well, 61.5 km, 900–1,200 m SURF cannot be delivered for US$250 M on any unit-rate set I can defend. Most probable readings, with my weights:

1. **Partial call-off / phased booking (p ≈ 0.50).** The iEPCI is structured as a frame with initial call-off for the first drill centre (4 wells, SPS + short flowlines ≈ US$180–240 M); later phases booked as they are released. Consistent with "fast-track" language in the award.
2. **Trees supplied outside this contract (p ≈ 0.20).** PETRONAS increasingly procures subsea trees under a separate long-term frame agreement. Removing trees and tree installation (−US$125 M) gives US$557 M — still above the band, so this only works combined with (1).
3. **The estimate is inflated (p ≈ 0.15).** Would require a single-cluster field with ≤8 km step-outs and ~US$25 M/well SURF. Possible only if the real field is far more compact than the 18–30 km prospect statement implies.
4. **Amendment / reservation fee, main value booked elsewhere (p ≈ 0.15).** The 2021 contract may never have been formally terminated; the 2026 award could be a re-scope delta.

**The number to carry into the FDP is US$682 M, not US$250 M.** Even the most favourable reading (2) only reaches US$557 M.

The schedule is driven by **flexible pipe lead time, not by installation**. Flexible pipe capacity is the tightest link in the global SURF chain entering 2026 — new capacity is not expected to relieve the market until 2029, and Petrobras alone has tendered roughly 500 km. Limbayong's 61.5 km of flowline plus 6 risers will be competing for the same extrusion slots as far larger Brazilian and Guyanese programmes. **Ordering flexible pipe more than 6 months after FID puts first oil into 2030.** [M for market condition, A for the schedule consequence]

### Vessel market and availability risk

- **Day rates are elevated but softening at the low end.** 2025 fixtures: 150 t AHC crane vessels often >US$60,000/day; 250 t AHC >US$80,000/day — at or beyond the previous peak. Short-term and spot fixtures varied almost four-fold top-to-bottom in 2025, so a 2029 campaign cannot be priced off today's spot. [M]
- **Supply is arriving.** ~30 firm newbuild 150/250 t AHC vessels are on order, >20% of the active fleet — a ratio comparable to the 2008–2015 build wave, mostly Chinese-built, mostly speculative and uncommitted. This should loosen 2028–29 availability but widen the rate spread by vessel age and specification. [M]
- **Top-tier concentration.** The Subsea 7–Saipem merger (Saipem7), expected to close H2 2026, leaves three Tier-1 SURF contractors and a combined market leader. For a single 211-day campaign in a remote basin, that is a thin bench: two credible bidders, not four. [M for merger, A for consequence]
- **Malaysian OSV market** is seen as stable-to-positive over 2026–28, with a leaner offshore cycle per PETRONAS Activity Outlook 2026–2028 — supportive for support vessels, but the flex-lay/reel-lay class is not an OSV and is priced globally. [M/A]
- **Weather.** Sabah offshore is less monsoon-exposed than Sarawak, but 1,200 m operations are swell-sensitive. Assume ~200 operable days/year and a hard March–October campaign window; every 30 days of slippage costs roughly US$10 M in standby and pushes RFSU past the next window. [A]
- **The real vessel risk is not rate — it is sequence.** A single-season campaign installs 61.5 km of flowline, 24.9 km of umbilical, 6 risers, 4 manifolds and 15 jumpers with one flex-lay vessel. There is no second-vessel redundancy. A 20-day mechanical failure in June 2029 costs a full year to first oil.

## KEY JUDGEMENTS

1. **SURF is US$682 M (Class 4: US$478 M–1,024 M), or ~US$68 M/well.** The disclosed 2026 band understates the scope by at least 2.7×. Any economic model using US$250 M is not conservative — it is wrong by roughly US$430 M, which on a 270 MMboe field is ~US$1.6/bbl of undeclared capex.
2. **The 2021 and 2026 awards are not two estimates of the same thing.** 2021 was a scope-valued award; 2026 is a booking class. Treating them as a declining cost trend — "TechnipFMC cut the price in half" — is a category error and the most likely analytical trap in this FDP.
3. **Multiplexed E/H control is mandatory, not optional.** Direct hydraulic cannot reach 18 km with 80+ functions. The cost of MUX (US$48 M, 7% of SURF) buys remote choke management on a field the operator itself calls top-decile complex — that is the highest-value 7% in the budget.
4. **Flexible lazy-wave everywhere; free-standing riser is not a candidate.** FSHR would add US$100 M+ to solve a motion problem that compliant flexible risers already solve at 1,200 m with six risers.
5. **Three drill centres with two spare slots is the right architecture, and it is also the tie-back option premium.** PETRONAS has publicly committed to the 18–30 km prospect radius. Sparing slots and umbilical cores now costs perhaps US$15–25 M; retrofitting a manifold position later costs a vessel campaign.
6. **The binding constraint is flexible pipe, not the vessel market.** 2029 vessel availability looks better than 2025; 2028–29 extrusion slots do not. This inverts the usual SURF risk ranking and should drive the procurement plan.
7. **No diving anywhere.** 900–1,200 m means all-ROV installation and intervention. This is a real cost and safety advantage over shallow-water Malaysian analogues and should be protected in the design (ROV-friendly torque tools, no diver-assisted connectors).
8. **The FPSO interface is the largest unhedged item in S5.** Whether *Excalibur* carries a disconnectable turret determines riser count, dynamic umbilical length, and whether the field can be storm-disconnected. Until that is settled, the riser and umbilical numbers carry an additional ±15% of their own value.

| Vessel day rates fall below US$250 k/day for flex-lay | Installation line drops ~US$12 M — not material to the verdict. |
