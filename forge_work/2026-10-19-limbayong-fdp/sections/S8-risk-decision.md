## HEADLINE

- **NPV@10% base case: ~US$0.3 B (positive but thin).** P50 combined point case (~118 MMbbl, capex 2.9 B, Brent 72, gas RM30, +12 mo) is **−US$0.75 B**. P90 ~ **−US$2.0 B**. Monte-Carlo P(NPV<0) ≈ **80%** at AACE Class 4 inputs.
- **Project IRR ≈ 12.1%** at base; **5.6%** at P50; **17.0%** at upside. **It does not clear a 15% real hurdle** under any central case. To clear it you need either **CAPEX ≤ US$1.8 B** (NPV@15% = +$215 M, IRR 17.4%) or **≥180 MMbbl recoverable** (NPV@15% = +$33 M, IRR >15%). Both thresholds sit **outside** the published design basis (CAPEX is already ~US$2.45 B) and the public volume range (139 MMbbl base, 97 P90). At 160 MMbbl the project still fails the 15% test (NPV@15% = −$144 M, IRR 13.8%).
- **Breakeven Brent @10% ≈ US$65/bbl.** At the screen base of US$75 the project is **viable but barely**; the EMV-weighted register pulls it to negative expected NPV.
- **The two cycle-killers are (1) the Excalibur/hull capability gap and (2) gas export.** Both are resolvable, but neither has been resolved at FID.
- **First oil 2029 is implausible.** 2027 sanctions implies <24 months to first oil; industry norm for a 10-well + 1,000-m DW + converted-FPSO development is **36–48 months** after FID. A 12-month slip to 2030 cuts NPV ~$200 M; a 24-month slip flips the base case negative.
- **Recommendation:** **HOLD** the sanction of irrevocable capex (drill rig, long-lead SURF orders, MMHE conversion kick-off) until the **FIVE CONDITIONS** below are resolved. FID on 28 Aug 2026 was a *political* decision; the *physical* decision is still in front of PETRONAS, MMHE, MISC and TechnipFMC. Implement a structured 6-month Assurance Programme; re-judge at Go/No-Go gate 1 in April 2027.
- **Confidence: 60%** in the FIVE-CONDITIONS framing and the HOLD recommendation; **35%** in any single cost or volume number (the public record is thin and the hull identity is inferential, not contractually public). See CONFIDENCE table.

## EMV ADJUSTMENT

**Unweighted sum of (P × Impact):** **−US$2,094 M** across the 21 risks. Adding this to the base-case NPV of **+US$313 M** gives an **expected (risk-adjusted) NPV of ≈ −US$1.8 B** at the AACE Class 4 risk surface. This is *more* negative than the Monte-Carlo mean of −$727 M (below) because the register stacks every risk at full severity simultaneously, whereas the Monte-Carlo samples them at their stated probabilities; the register number is therefore a *stress-case* upper bound on downside, and the MC mean is the better central estimate.

**Facilities is 38% of the register and is entirely self-inflicted** — it is the cost of buying a 19-year-old hull and hoping the topsides can be made to work. That is a choice, not geology.

Largest EMV contributors: **R-01** hull/process gap (−$248 M), **R-02** gas export (−$228 M), **R-03** volume (−$214 M), **R-11** schedule (−$175 M), **R-12** Brent floor (−$163 M), **R-05** drilling overrun (−$127 M), **R-13** gas price (−$101 M), **R-14** PETROS (−$90 M). The top five alone sum to −$1,028 M EMV, i.e. ~49% of the total register exposure.

**Two caveats the reader must carry.** (1) The impacts are *marginal* (each measured from base holding the others at base); because risks are correlated — a gas-route failure (R-02) and a hull/process failure (R-01) are the same physical problem seen from two angles — the register over-counts the downside. (2) **R-18** (no farm-out) has no direct project-NPV impact; it is a *balance-sheet* risk to PCSB (US$2.4 B of 100% carry) and shows up in the register as strategic, not financial. Both caveats push in the same direction: **the register is a stress bound; the Monte-Carlo mean is the central expectation.** Neither is a positive number.

**Independent conclusion:** at the **public** central case, the project is **marginally viable**. The **risk-adjusted** central case is **uneconomic**. The fact that PETRONAS sanctioned it anyway is consistent with a **non-cash NPV** that internalises strategic value (Malaysian contractor, capability-building for PCSB, Gentari-pivot optionality) — but a private contractor at the same numbers would not have sanctioned.

**Institutional FDP norm for a 270-MMboe, 1,000-m DW, 10-well project with a converted FPSO and floating gas export:**
- 12–18 months integrated FDP study (FEED + GeoSciences + Process + Commercial + Project Services)

**Comparison.** What PETRONAS appears to have done in practice (the 28 Aug 2026 FID with the 27-month FO): an aggressive parallel-tracking of FEED, hull acquisition (MISC Excalibur), SURF award (TechnipFMC) and class-society engagement, with no public reserves audit, no public gas route, and no public schedule rebaseline. **Verdict on Assurance Programme:** the *missing* US$170–240 M of independent FDP work is a **premium for confidence**, not a *necessity* if PETRONAS is willing to accept the negative expected NPV in exchange for strategic optionality. **The honest answer is that FID was not, technically, complete**; what was sanctioned was a *structured commitment to complete it* with a built-in 6-month window in which to abort. The Assurance Programme above is the work needed to **close that window** in a way a sovereign board can defend to public scrutiny.

## RECOMMENDATION

**HOLD** — formalise the **6-month Assurance Window** between FID and Go/No-Go Gate 1 (target April 2027). In that window:

1. **Stop MMHE Module E** (topside fabrication) until **C-1** is closed. (Estimated opportunity cost: US$30–50 M of re-work if FEED is wrong; loss of 6 months schedule.)
2. **Re-baseline first oil to Q4 2030** as the *contracted* plan. Public messaging: "FID 2026; first oil 2030; this is a 4-year development, not a 27-month one."
3. **Launch the gas-export ITT** in October 2026; award FEED December 2026.
4. **Issue a competitive farm-out data-room** by January 2027 to Shell, Murphy, ENI.
5. **Authorise the Reserves Audit** October 2026; completion Q1 2027.
6. **Establish a sovereign Board-level Risk Committee** with monthly progress reports on C-1 to C-5.

**Go/No-Go Gate 1 — April 2027:**
- **GO** if C-1, C-2, C-3 are TRUE at low-cost and C-4 is in advanced negotiation.
- **NO-GO** (i.e. cancel MMHE conversion, return TechnipFMC iEPCI) if **any** of C-1, C-2, C-3 returns FALSE.
- **REVIEW** if C-4 is FALSE only (project proceeds, but with a written acknowledgement of NPV erosion).

**This is not a "do not sanction" recommendation.** It is a *sanctioned* recommendation that the public case for the *commitment to spend ~$2.4 B* depends on a small set of well-defined facts that have not yet been established. If they are established as TRUE by April 2027, the project clears the hurdle. If they are not, the right call is a structured unwind that protects the Malaysian fabrication chain (MMHE, MISC, TechnipFMC) by routing the assets to other PETRONAS projects (Gumusut-Kakap phase-2, Kelidang, Rotan and similar satellite tie-backs) — not a forced no-go.
