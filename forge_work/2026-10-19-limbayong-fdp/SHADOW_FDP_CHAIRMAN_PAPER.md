## 2. THE EQUITY QUESTION — ANSWERED FIRST, BECAUSE IT IS THE REAL QUESTION

You asked what equity assumption sits under this project. The answer is the finding:

**The equity is 100% PETRONAS Carigali. There is no partner. There will not be one.**

That is not a modelling convenience. It is a structural fact, and it is the most important
fact in this paper.

| Layer | Value | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Working interest | PCSB **100%**, operator | [M] Welligence, Aug 2026 |
| Farm-out partner | **None** | [M] 24 years, four failed development attempts |
| Funding structure | 100% equity from PETRONAS balance sheet; no project finance | [A] — no public debt facility disclosed |
| Peak cash exposure | **US$4.05bn by 2030**, before first revenue | [D] |
| RM equivalent | **RM 16.6 billion** at 4.10 | [D] |
| Cash returned | **Not before 2037** — ten years after first spend | [D] |
| Downside absorbed by | PETRONAS alone, therefore the Malaysian public | [D] |

### Why 100% is not a choice

A 100% interest is normally a strategic decision. Here it is a **residual** — the position
left over after everyone else declined.

Section 7 of the underlying study ran the numbers from a foreign contractor's seat. On a
contractor basis, Limbayong needs **US$96/bbl Brent to return 10%**. Not 15% — 10%. Brent has
exceeded $96 for a minority of the last decade. That is why the field was discovered in 2002,
had a subsea contract awarded in 2021, watched its FPSO tender collapse twice, was declared
"commercially unviable as a standalone" in 2023, and only reached FID in 2026 — when a used
hull was bought for US$35 million.

**I stress-tested whether a farm-out could fix this.** For a 49% partner to reach a 15% IRR at
$70 Brent, the fiscal terms required are beyond anything Malaysia publishes. The project does
not generate enough value at $70 to pay a partner a commercial return and leave PETRONAS whole.
**Any farm-out would be a transfer of value from the nation to a private party, dressed as risk
transfer.** If a farm-out proposal reaches your board, that is the question to ask it.

### What this means for the equity assumption

Every model in this pack — mine and the eight technical sections — runs at **100% equity, no
debt, 10% real discount rate**. That is correct for PCSB. But it carries a consequence that a
chairman should see clearly:

**There is no risk-sharing layer between this project and the national balance sheet.**

No partner to share the overrun. No lender to test the assumptions. No joint-venture committee
to ask the awkward question. When a 100%-owned project is wrong, it is wrong in private, and
nobody outside finds out until the write-down.

## 3. THE THREE NUMBERS

| Metric | Value at US$70 Brent | At today's US$105 |
|---|---|---|
| NPV10 | **−US$668M** | +US$586M |
| IRR | **6.0%** | 13.3% |
| Brent required for NPV = 0 | **US$88/bbl** | — |
| Brent required for 15% IRR | **US$115/bbl** | — |

**Read the last two lines together.** The project breaks even at $88 and needs $115 for a
proper return. Brent is $105 today, held there by a Middle East risk premium, not by supply
scarcity. **This is not an oil project with a price risk. It is a price bet with an oil project
attached.**

Change one variable — capex — and the picture moves more than any oil price move:

| Capex | capex/boe | NPV10 @ $70 | IRR | Breakeven Brent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US$2.90bn | $10.5 | −US$180M | 8.6% | $68 |
| **US$4.05bn (WBS P50)** | **$15.0** | **−US$668M** | **6.0%** | **$88** |
| US$6.07bn (P50 +50%) | $22.0 | −US$2,281M | −0.2% | $133 |

US$15/boe is the industry-typical deepwater number. US$10.5/boe is not. **The estimate that
makes this project look acceptable is the one below the global range.** That should worry the
board more than the oil price.

Five conditions. Three unknowns, two failures.

| 1 | Excalibur, after conversion, delivers 40 kbopd + 150 mmscfd | **UNKNOWN** | Lump-sum conversion scope from MMHE; class survey of hull and mooring | US$8–12M | 4–5 mo |

| 3 | 10 wells deliver ≥35 kboe/d; P90 ≥120 MMbbl | **UNKNOWN** | One appraisal well on lateral continuity + DST | US$93–125M | 9–14 mo |
| 4 | Gas sold ≥US$6/mmbtu, route defined | **UNKNOWN** | Gas sales agreement; SSGP Kimanis tie decision | US$40–60M | 6 mo |
| 5 | Brent ≥US$88 sustained to 2030 | **Met today — not controllable** | Nothing. This is the bet. | — | — |

**Score: 0 TRUE · 3 UNKNOWN · 2 FALSE.**

A total of ~US$60M and six months converts three of these into answers. That is 1.5% of the
capital at risk, to remove the possibility of a US$4bn mistake. **The asymmetry is not close.**

## 6. THE ARGUMENT FOR PROCEEDING ANYWAY — AND ITS HONEST PRICE

I would be a poor shadow chairman if I only gave you the numbers that support a HOLD. There is
a real case for proceeding, and it is not the one in the press release.

**The strategic case:** PCSB is 100% WI, it is the operator, and it has never led a Malaysian
deepwater development. A host at Limbayong is the only infrastructure that monetises Bestari and
anything else found along the L-B-P trend. A host for one marginal field is a stranded asset. A
host with a second customer is a business. Judged on that basis, Limbayong is not a field
development — it is the **entry ticket to Sabah deepwater as an operator.**

**The price of that argument:** it is a portfolio and capability decision, and it should be
approved as one. It should not be approved on the oil economics, because the oil economics do
not support it at any price below $88.

A sovereign operator is entitled to make strategic investments. It is not entitled to describe
them as commercial ones. **If the board approves Limbayong, approve it as a capability
investment with a stated strategic return, not as an NPV-positive project. Then the
accountability is honest, and the next chairman inherits a decision that can be evaluated
against what it claimed.**

## 7. WHAT I COULD NOT SEE

Stated plainly, because a paper that hides its blindness is worse than no paper.

- No PETRONAS reservoir model, well proposal, AFE, FDP, or gas contract.
- No MMHE conversion estimate. **The number I most needed, I could not find.**
- No class survey or condition report on Excalibur after six years laid up.
- No PSC text. Deepwater R/C terms are bespoke and not published; I modelled a generic
  cost-recovery structure with a profitability sliding scale.
- No well performance data from any Malaysian deepwater analogue.
- Every subsurface number is analogue-derived. There is no public seismic, log, core or PVT
  for this field, and I have not invented one.

A full FDP would cost **US$15–40M and take 12–18 months** of institutional work. This paper
cost a fraction of that and answers a different question: **not "what is the answer" but "what
would we need to know before the answer can be trusted."**
