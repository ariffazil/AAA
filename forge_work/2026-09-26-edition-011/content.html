War-risk cover now prices a Hormuz passage at about USD 10 million for a supertanker MOVED

Insurance, not navies, is what keeps the strait shut. Tanker war-risk cover is being quoted at roughly 40 times the pre-crisis rate - around USD 10 million for one VLCC round the passage - with six protection and indemnity clubs withdrawn from the trade. It is the slowest variable in the whole chain: even after a political deal is signed, cover has to be re-priced and re-underwritten before hulls move. That lag is why a headline deal and a restored flow are not the same date.

Nobody told you the way car loans are priced changed in June NEW

From 1 June 2026 the Hire-Purchase (Amendment) Act abolished both the flat rate and the Rule of 78, and requires loans to be quoted on the effective interest rate and a reducing balance. The practical effect is that early settlement stops being penalised - the old method front-loaded interest so that paying a loan off early cost you more than the maths allowed. If there is a vehicle loan in the household taken before June, the settlement figure quoted under the old formula is now the wrong number. Ask for the EIR and the reducing-balance statement.
Source: Hire Purchase (Term Charges) Regulations 2026 [P.U.(A) 171/2026] in force 1 June 2026; Hire-Purchase (Amendment) Act 2026; reporting via paultan.org 2025-11-14, iMoney 2026, Baker McKenzie alert, RinggitPlus

Eureka

A form of boron that shouldn't exist: it bends, and it conducts NEW

Chemists made a boron allotrope, Imma-B60, that is both electrically conductive and plastically deformable, a combination that had lived on paper for over a decade. The route: form a Na4B60 precursor under high pressure, then drive the sodium out. The reporting disagrees on how much better it conducts - Nature news says a million times more than chemists expected, phys.org says ten million - so treat the exact factor as unsettled and the direction as solid. Boron is famously stiff and insulating; this one is neither.
Source: Nature Chemistry 2026 (10.1038/s41557-026-02267-7, 'An open-framework boron allotrope exhibiting high conductivity and plasticity'); Nature news 2026-09-23/24 (a million times more conductive); phys.org 2026-09 (ten million); figures disagree between origins - recorded, not reconciled
