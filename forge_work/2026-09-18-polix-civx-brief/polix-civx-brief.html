2POLIX — power topology of the Malaysian fiscal regime

Scope: federal petroleum revenue, taxation, subsidies, GLC governance and federal–state fiscal relations. Six actors, four rent flows, three rule asymmetries.

Capture score: MODERATE. Not uniform. State governments carry the highest capture risk (opacity 0.7); Bank Negara Malaysia the lowest (0.3) — operational independence holds even though the Governor is politically appointed. The concentration that matters most is not in a regulator but at the centre: the Prime Minister also holds the Finance portfolio.

2.4 Second seed case — PETRONAS and the GLC layer

Three actors control the country's largest pools of institutional capital. The structural tension is the same in each: a commercial mandate and a national mandate held by the same board, with the appointment power sitting outside the balance sheet.

Khazanah carries RM100B+ and PNB RM300B+ — between them, a large share of listed Malaysian equity. When appointment is political and the mandate is dual, the performance drag is real but diffuse. That diffuseness is exactly why it survives scrutiny.

3.3 The three paths

MY-FISCAL-BEST · 14y

Managed transition — high oil + fiscal reform + demographic adaptation

Assumptions: $95/bbl (high) · Growing 1%/yr (deepwater) · Managed transition (TFR 1.7, policy success) · Full reform (GST + property tax) · Full targeted cash transfers
Key risks
  • Oil price reversal after investment lock-in
  • GST political backlash (2014 precedent)
  • Demographic transition slower than assumed
Irreversible if taken
  • Deepwater infrastructure once committed (30yr lifecycle)
  • GST political capital spent
Option space: Full fiscal flexibility. Investment capacity for energy transition. Social safety net funded.  |  Best case requires political will that has historically been absent. Not impossible but not baseline.
MY-FISCAL-BASE · 14y

Muddle through — base oil + partial reform + demographic drift

Assumptions: $75/bbl (base) · Flat (new discoveries offset) · Medium aging (TFR 1.5, gradual) · Partial reform (GST-lite) · Gradual rationalization
Key risks
  • Fiscal deficit widens as petroleum revenue declines
  • MYR depreciation pressure from twin deficits
  • Aging population strains healthcare + pensions
  • Energy transition investment delayed
Irreversible if taken
  • Lost decade of energy transition investment
  • Brain drain accelerates (skilled workforce outflow)
  • Fossil fuel infrastructure lock-in
Option space: Limited fiscal flexibility. Incremental reform possible but structural change deferred.  |  Most likely path based on historical pattern. Not optimal but survivable.
MY-FISCAL-WORST · 14y

Structural decline — low oil + no reform + rapid aging

Assumptions: $55/bbl (low) · Declining 3%/yr (depletion) · High aging (TFR 1.3, rapid aging) · No reform (SST stays) · Full subsidies maintained
Key risks
  • Sovereign credit downgrade
  • MYR crisis (capital flight, FX reserve depletion)
  • Brain drain accelerates (skilled workforce exodus)
  • Social unrest from subsidy removal pressure
  • Energy security crisis (net importer by 2035)
  • Pension/healthcare funding gap for aging population
Irreversible if taken
  • Institutional trust deficit (hard to rebuild)
  • Human capital loss (10+ years to replace)
  • Ecological damage from deferred maintenance
  • Intergenerational debt burden
Option space: Very limited. IMF-style conditionality possible. Sovereignty compromised.  |  Low probability but high consequence. Requires combination of adverse factors. Worth studying, not worth panicking.
The gap that matters: the distance between BEST (0.8 fiscal) and WORST (0.2 fiscal) is not oil. Both extreme paths assume the same geology; they differ on political choices — tax base, subsidy design, and how early the transition is funded. Oil sets the weather. Reform decides the outcome.

4.1 How to read this document

  • POLIX is structural, not personal. It maps roles, rules and flows — it does not allege motive in any individual. Capture risk is a property of a position, not a verdict on a person.
  • CIVX is a study, not a prediction. Three discrete paths, no Monte Carlo, no external shocks modelled. It is a reasoning scaffold.
  • Epistemic tags are load-bearing. Every rent flow and rule asymmetry carries its basis — OBSERVED, DERIVED or INTERPRETED. The distinction is the value.
  • Nothing here is sourced from inside. Public sources only. No insider knowledge.
