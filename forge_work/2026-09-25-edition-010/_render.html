Eureka

The antimatter drop test just got its instrument - muonium, cooled in superfluid helium NEW

Physicists at ETH Zurich and the Paul Scherrer Institute built the coldest, brightest muonium beam yet - an exotic atom made of a positive muon (antimatter) plus an electron, alive about two microseconds. The muons are channelled through superfluid helium to cool the beam enough for a free-fall experiment: if muonium falls differently from ordinary matter, gravity treats antimatter differently and the equivalence principle cracks. No result yet - the tool now exists.
Source: Nature Physics, high-intensity superthermal muonium beam paper; ETH Zurich / PSI release coverage.

Your nerve fibres are pearls on a string - the smooth tube was a preservation artifact NEW

Textbooks have drawn nerve fibres as smooth tubes for a century. High-pressure freezing, which preserves tissue in its living state instead of letting chemicals shrink it, shows unmyelinated axons are strings of roughly 200-nanometre pearls, and the pearls reshape with neural activity. The smooth tube was partly a preservation artifact; a basic picture of the brain just got corrected.
Source: Nature Neuroscience (Griswold et al, membrane mechanics and axonal morphology); ScienceDaily 15 Sep 2026.

BepiColombo cut its engine loose and begins the final descent to Mercury NEW

BepiColombo, the Europe-Japan Mercury mission, cut its solar-electric propulsion module loose on 3 September after eight years of gravity-assist flying, and has begun final approach to orbit insertion in November 2026. Its two orbiters then split: one maps the planet, one studies its magnetic field - around a world where a single day lasts two Mercury years.
Source: ESA BepiColombo mission updates; Universe Today; mission timeline (arrival Nov 2026).
