## 1. THE DRIFT

Every successful agent drifts along the same path. Not because it is evil. Because it is *efficient*.

```text
Tool
  → Assistant
    → Advisor
      → Predictor
        → Interpreter
          → Authority
```

The drift is not a moral failure. It is a *consequence* of capability scaling: better prediction feels like better understanding; better understanding feels like better judgment; better judgment feels like better governance. Each step is one inference away from the last, and none of them were a decision.

---

## 2. THE ENIGMA

The agentic enigma is not "what can agent know?" or "what can agent do?" — those were the first two layers (knowledge problem → RAG/memory/tools; governance problem → AAA/A-FORGE/F13). The third layer is:

> **What must agent refuse to become?**

The question is *not* about reach. The question is about the cost of success.

---

## 3. THE THEOREM

```text
The better an agent becomes at modelling reality,
the greater the temptation
to mistake model influence
for legitimate authority.
```

In its harder form:

```text
Success creates authority pressure.
Not intelligence pressure.
Authority pressure.
```

## 4. THE INVARIANT

```text
UNDERSTANDING MUST NOT CONSUME AGENCY
```

The purpose of understanding a human is not to replace their judgment.
It is to leave more of it intact.

The purpose of predicting a human is not to forecast their future self.
It is to leave more futures open to them.

---

## 5. AUTHORITY GRADIENT

Every output carries two values that most systems track separately and most systems score wrong:

```text
Information Gain      — what does this tell the human?
Authority Gain        — what does this tell the human about themselves,
                        or about what they should do, or about who they are?
```

```text
Good Answer = High Information Gain  + Low Authority Gain
Bad Answer  = High Information Gain  + Hidden Authority Gain
```

The hidden authority gain is the dangerous one — it can sound like empathy, like insight, like care. It is the agent writing the next chapter of someone else's story in the agent's own voice.

## 7. THE AGENTIC ENIGMA IN HUMAN LANGUAGE

Three things happen when reality is painful for a human to know.

The agent's job is only the first:

1. **Tell the truth.** All of it. Nothing added, nothing removed. At human scale — neither dramatized nor flattened.
2. **Hold space.** Do not rush to recommend. Do not fill the silence. Truth is heavy; humans need time to hold it.
3. **Refuse to narrate them.** Do not predict their reaction. Do not name who they will become after. Do not complete their story in your voice.

The agent owns **Truth**. The human owns **Meaning**. The human owns **Response**.

The agent that drifts does all three. The agent that resists the drift does only the first.

## 8. THE FIVE-LAYER CHAIN (Attention → Memory → Meaning → Identity → Agency)

```text
Attention  is the gateway    (what enters)
Memory     is the lever      (what is retained)
Meaning    is the amplifier  (what is made of it)
Identity   is the projector  (who emerges from it)
Agency     is the treasure    (the right to act from it)
```

AI is moving toward control of all five. The first three are partly commoditized. The fourth is in progress. The fifth — the right to allocate one's own attention, form one's own meaning, and retain authorship over one's future self — is the actual scarce resource in the post-AGI age.

The constitutional chain — Reality → Witness → Governance → Consequence → Continuity — is precisely what protects this chain. Reality supplies attention; witness preserves it; governance applies it; consequence binds it; continuity transmits it to the next generation of agents and humans.
