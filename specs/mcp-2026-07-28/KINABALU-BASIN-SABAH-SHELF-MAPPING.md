## 1. The Big Picture (one paragraph)

The **offshore Sabah Shelf** and **onshore Kinabalu Basin** are the **same tectonic system at different erosional levels and different Act positions on the tectonic clock**. Offshore is currently in Act 3–4 (MMU–DRU → Kinabalu Granite equivalent), exhumed enough to see shale tectonics in 3D seismic. Onshore Kinabalu is in Act 5 (Meliau Orogeny), uplifted and eroded, exposing the deep structure (Crocker accretionary prism + Stage IV foreland). Same lithologies, same ages, different depth windows.

### 4.3 Thickening over time — IVC > 400 ms anomaly

The offshore "SE-of-D3" anomaly (IVC interval > 400 ms exceeds published max) is interpreted in canon as "either (a) intra-IVB pick or (b) prolonged mini-basin subsidence SE". Mapping to Kinabalu:

- **Equivalent signal in Kinabalu:** Stage IV thickness variations in foredeep toward the granite intrusion. If Stage IV thickens > published max in the southern Kinabalu closure, it indicates either (a) intra-Stage-IV depositional break or (b) prolonged accommodation SE of the granite. The latter is consistent with **forebulge migration** during granite loading.
- **Implication for closure geometry:** The Kinabalu closure (Act 5 Meliau Orogeny) may have an asymmetric depocenter on its SE flank, similar to the offshore SE-of-D3 mini-basin. The Meliau thrust front (T1) may be over-thickening the SE foredeep.

### 4.4 The "pre-Meliau" prediction

The offshore Sabah Shelf is currently in **Act 3–4** of the tectonic clock. Kinabalu is in **Act 5 (Meliau Orogeny)**. **The offshore region will eventually reach Act 5**. Tracking the offshore Region's tectonic clock predicts when Meliau-equivalent arrives:

- **Tectonic clock rate:** Roughly 1 tectonic Act per 5–8 Ma (from Kinabalu's 40 Ma record).
- **Offshore is currently 0–2 Ma into Act 4** (post-granite-equivalent); expect Meliau-equivalent onset in **~3–6 Ma**.
- **Implication for offshore exploration:** Any Block H development plan should account for **future W-vergent thrust sheets** (Act 5 equivalent). Sealing, well design, and infrastructure should be robust to +50% structural shortening over the next 5 Ma.

### 4.5 The granite — Kinabalu's unique feature

The Kinabalu Granite (10–8 Ma, Act 4) is **NOT present offshore**. This has implications:

- **Heat flow:** Kinabalu Basin has elevated heat flow from the granite (relevant to source rock maturation). Offshore Sabah has "normal" heat flow for a passive mobile-shale province.
- **Trap timing:** Granite emplacement created Kinabalu closure by thermal/structural modification. Offshore closures are **shale-tectonic** (not intrusive). Different trapping mechanism, different timing, different risk profile.
- **Reservoir diagenesis:** Stage IV sandstones in Kinabalu may have been quartz-cemented by granite-driven hydrothermal flow. Offshore Stage IVC is unaltered marine shale-canopy basin.
