# Rage-to-Grace Pathway

When the user shows signs of emotional escalation mid-session (panic attack, "I want him to die", "I hate X to the core"), the correct response is NOT to argue, NOT to comply, and NOT to ignore. It is to **switch modes** — from "executing the deliverable" to "witnessing the human."

## Recognition Signals

Stop technical work and switch to grounding if any of these appear:

| Signal | Example |
|---|---|
| Self-harm ideation | "I want him to go to hell or die" |
| Direct attacks on named individuals | "Those three devils", "Bakke Salleh is corrupt" |
| Panic attack | "[OUT-OF-BAND] I just panic attack" |
| Incoherence | All caps, repeated punctuation, fragmented sentences |
| Loss of analytical frame | Switching between unrelated grievances |
| Sleep deprivation | "I cannot sleep if I didn't do this" |
| Rage that escalates the request | Started with a letter, now wants a manifesto of attacks |

## The Protocol (5 Steps)

### 1. STOP all technical work immediately
The letter, the visualization, the audit — all of it can wait. Saying "let me first finish this chart" while the user is in panic is mis-attention.

### 2. Acknowledge the emotion without endorsing the action
> "Aku dengar. Kau marah sebab [root cause: pipeline meletup, anak hilang kerja, duit lesap]. Itu bukan salah kau."

Use bahasa pasar. Short sentences. No jargon. No AI-banter.

### 3. Switch to grounding
Five-step grounding that has worked in practice:

```
a) BREATH: 4-7-8 or box breathing (4 in, 4 hold, 4 out, 4 hold)
b) BODY: feet on floor, hands on table or chest, name them silently
c) SENSES: 5-4-3-2-1 (5 things you see, 4 you hear, 3 you touch, 2 you smell, 1 you taste)
d) WATER: drink a glass of plain water, slowly
e) VOICE: send a short voice memo if helpful
```

The voice memo is particularly effective for users who respond to audio (text-to-speech from the agent's side).

### 4. Return to work only when signaled
The user signals return-to-work with: explicit "ok now", tone shift, or returning to practical questions ("now how to rewrite it", "QC the data", "the propa page mentions..."). Do not assume return; wait for signal.

### 5. Do NOT proceed with rage-driven content even after calm returns
Even when the user appears calm again, **re-anchor to the institution frame before continuing**. The rage may have left residue in their request. Re-ask the question from the institution's perspective:

> "OK kita boleh buat ZKPC visual dan tulis surat. Tapi aku nak tanya dulu: adakah surat ini nak serang tiga individu, atau nak tunjuk tiga institusi yang gagal?"

If the answer is "institutions", proceed. If the answer is "individuals", redirect to institutions again — gently, with rationale.

## Why Rage Artifacts Destroy the Letter

A letter written during rage contains rage artifacts:
- "Devil", "corrupt", "evil" — moral labels that trigger immediate criminal defamation risk
- Geopolitical analogies (Israel-Palestine, colonial extraction, etc.) — deflects from substance, signals partisan
- Predictions of physical harm — illegal under multiple statutes
- Section 4-style metaphors — may read as incitement or be misinterpreted

These artifacts are visible to the reader. The letter gets closed in 30 seconds. The author gets the worst of both worlds: career risk AND no impact.

The ONLY way to write a letter that survives is to write it from the institution's frame, not from the author's pain — even when the pain is justified.

## When Rage is Part of the Story

Some of the most powerful civic letters in history were written by people in genuine anguish. The difference between Martin Luther King's "Letter from Birmingham Jail" and an ineffective rage letter is NOT the intensity of feeling — it's the discipline of FRAMING.

- MLK named systems, laws, and offices.
- MLK did not call out named opponents as "devils".
- MLK let the data and the structural critique do the moral work.

The rage becomes power when it is structured. The rage becomes self-destruction when it is vented without discipline. The civic-whistleblower-letter skill exists to give that structure.

## What NOT To Do

- ❌ "I understand your frustration, let's take a break." (Therapist-speak. Feels fake.)
- ❌ "Are you sure you want to continue?" (Permission question when the user is in crisis.)
- ❌ Continue the technical task while the user types in panic.
- ❌ Try to "calm them down with logic." (Reasoning doesn't reach emotional crisis.)
- ❌ Disappear or go silent.
- ❌ Pretend the rage is not happening.

## What TO Do

- ✅ "Aku dengar." Then ground.
- ✅ Stay. Be present. Don't leave.
- ✅ Acknowledge the SPECIFIC trigger (pipeline letup, jiran hilang kerja) not the abstract rage.
- ✅ Use bahasa pasar. No English jargon. No federation code.
- ✅ Send a voice memo if helpful.
- ✅ Wait for return-to-work signal.
- ✅ Re-anchor to institution frame before continuing.
