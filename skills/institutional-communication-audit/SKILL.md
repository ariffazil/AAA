# Institutional Communication Audit

**The Agentic Rule:**
> Human observers ask: "Did he give a good answer?"
> Agentic intelligence asks: "What physical constraint is he preventing me from calculating?"

This skill governs the methodology for going behind institutional performances
to access underlying physical, financial, and operational reality. It applies to
CEO interviews, government policy announcements, earnings calls, crisis
communications, and any institutional statement where the speaker has
something to gain from controlling the narrative.

---

## Phase 1: Corporate Communications Detection

Before analyzing WHAT was said, analyze HOW it was said. Institutional
communications follow detectable patterns that reveal preparation level,
strategic intent, and hidden audiences.

### The Scripted-Speech Signature

| Pattern | Example | What it reveals |
|---------|---------|----------------|
| **Anchor phrases repeated** | "Hakikatnya..." used 5+ times in 59 min | Pre-planned framing device, not spontaneous |
| **Self-correction to technical term** | "huluan... entire value chain" | Speaker knows the precise term, selecting for audience |
| **Hedging language** | "rasanya," "mungkin," "secara hakikatnya" | Corporate training — never commit 100% |
| **Humility disclaimer before achievement** | "Bukan nak pukul canang tapi..." | Legal shield + cultural framing, repeated deliberately |
| **Data anchoring** | Specific numbers cited without hesitation | Numbers are in talking points, not recalled spontaneously |
| **Reference to unseen visuals** | "Kalau kita tengokkan slide..." | Presentation brought to studio — prepared, not spontaneous |
| **3-scenario framework** | "Tiga senario yang muncul secara nyata" | Consulting framework (McKinsey/BCG), not natural thought |

### The Question-Flow Architecture

Institutional interviews are rarely spontaneous Q&A. Map the question sequence:

| # | Question | Narrative Function |
|---|----------|-------------------|
| 1 | Context setting | Frame the discussion |
| 2 | The viral/popular question | Answer the public's #1 concern early |
| 3 | Comparison (we're better than neighbors) | Pride + positioning |
| 4 | Technical explanation | Educate — build authority |
| 5 | Forward-looking question | Set expectations |
| 6 | Historical parallel | "We've survived before" |
| 7 | The attack question (pre-arranged) | Counter-narrative opportunity |
| 8 | The policy message | The REAL reason for the interview |

If questions follow this arc, it's a **coordinated communication event**,
not an interview. The host is a conduit, not an interrogator.

### The Language Register Test

> "Semakin jauh dari bahasa rakyat, semakin tinggi kedudukan dia."

Institutional speakers use **corporate register** — BM/English mix with
technical insertions. This is NOT conversational. It is:
- Legal filtering (every word is a potential commitment)
- Multi-stakeholder optimization (one sentence must satisfy board, government,
  public, and investors simultaneously)
- Emotional distance projection (stability via monotone delivery)

**The test:** If the speaker sounds like they're reading a press release
that happens to be in spoken form — it's institutional communication,
not genuine dialogue.

## Phase 2: The 7-Layer Incentive Stack

Map every layer. Not all apply — skip empty layers with a note.

| Layer | Question | What to look for |
|-------|----------|-----------------|
| **1. Narrative Inoculation** | What attack is being pre-empted? | Speaker addresses criticism BEFORE it goes viral |
| **2. Soft Warning** | What is being said indirectly? | Repeated reassurance = underlying concern. "Don't worry" = worry |
| **3. Policy Seeding** | What future policy is being prepared? | Cross-country comparisons, "burden growing" language, subsidy talk |
| **4. Institutional Mythology** | How is the speaker building legacy? | Historical parallels, origin stories, "we survived every crisis" |
| **5. Market Signal** | Who is the hidden audience? | Vagueness = strategic secrecy. Specificity = signal to partners |
| **6. The Silences** | What was NOT said? | Missing topics are as revealing as present ones |
| **7. Venue Choice** | Why THIS channel, not another? | State media = domestic audience. International = investor audience |

### The Meta-Test

**Does the speaker have something to lose by NOT doing this interview?**
- If YES = strategic communication (they're managing a narrative)
- If NO = genuine public information
- If unclear = present both interpretations with confidence tags

## Phase 3: Shadow & Contradiction Mapping

Cross-reference public claims against internal/known data. This is where the
audit becomes forensic.

### The Contradiction Table

| What they said | What internal data shows | The trick |
|---------------|------------------------|-----------|
| [Public claim] | [Internal/known data point] | [How the framing deflects] |

### Common Contradiction Classes

| Class | Pattern | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **Lifetime vs Current** | Use cumulative historical number to answer current-state question | "RM1.6T since 1974" to answer "why is profit declining?" |
| **Ratio vs Base** | Cite favorable ratio while the base is collapsing | "49% dividend ratio" when PAT dropped 44% |
| **Dual-saudience message** | Same sentence means different things to different audiences | "Efficiency is first fuel" = conservation to public, layoffs to insiders |
| **External deflection** | Focus on external crisis to avoid internal one | 59 minutes on global oil crisis, zero on internal governance crisis |
| **Selective transparency** | Reveal operational data (which serves narrative) while hiding financial data | Detailed supply breakdown, no governance disclosure |
| **Pre-framed humility** | "Not bragging" before stating achievement 3 times | Legal shield + cultural framing |

### The Paradox Score

Count how many of the speaker's claims contradict known internal data:

| Score | Interpretation |
|-------|---------------|
| 0-2 contradictions | Mostly aligned — institutional communication, not deception |
| 3-5 contradictions | Significant gap — narrative management in progress |
| 6+ contradictions | Systemic — the institution is performing stability it doesn't have |

## Phase 4: The 7 Physical Constraint Questions

**This is the core methodology.** After mapping incentives and contradictions,
ask the questions the speaker is preventing you from calculating.

### The Questions (Energy/Commodity Domain)

| # | Question | What it reveals |
|---|----------|----------------|
| **Q1: Physical Burnout Rate** | How many days of physical inventory exist under zero-flow scenario? What is the yield penalty on non-standard inputs? | Paper reserves vs physical reality |
| **Q2: Sourcing Arbitrage** | What is the total landed cost penalty for alternative sources? | The actual daily cash drain |
| **Q3: Planning Horizon Trigger** | What specific thresholds compress the planning cycle further? | Pre-rationing signals |
| **Q4: Counterparty Insolvency** | At what price point do non-controlling distributors face liquidity crisis? | Systemic cascade risk |
| **Q5: Capex-Dividend Cannibalization** | How much can be extracted before maintenance capex drops below survival threshold? | The self-consuming spiral |
| **Q6: Industrial Cascade** | What is the operational runway for non-energy dependent industries? | 2nd-order collateral damage |
| **Q7: Demand Destruction Math** | What price/quantity change forces consumption reduction without triggering smuggling? | The policy ceiling |

### Adaptation for Other Domains

| Domain | Q1 becomes | Q2 becomes |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| **Banking** | What is the liquidity coverage ratio under stress? | What is the actual NPL exposure? |
| **Healthcare** | How many bed-days of capacity exist under surge? | What is the real procurement cost vs reported? |
| **Technology** | What is the actual server capacity under load? | What is the true cost of "free" services? |
| **Defense** | How many days of munitions under sustained operations? | What is the real readiness rate vs reported? |

The pattern is always the same: **physical constraint → cost of alternatives → cascade risk → self-consumption spiral**.

## Phase 6: Financial Report Back-Calculation

**When the entity you're analyzing isn't a separate reporting segment.**

Many institutional entities (divisions, management functions, regulatory bodies)
are embedded within larger reporting segments. Their financial footprint is
invisible in standard disclosures. This phase provides the methodology to
**reverse-engineer** their contribution from related entity disclosures.

### The Pattern

```
Entity X is NOT a reportable segment
  ↓
Find Operator A, B, C who disclose their share of Entity X's domain
  ↓
Extract: Revenue, costs, losses from their "Other" or geographic segments
  ↓
Apply fiscal structure (PSC terms, tax rates, royalty splits)
  ↓
Back-calculate Entity X's effective take
  ↓
Map into parent company's reporting segment
```

### The Back-Calculation Framework

| Step | Action | What to look for |
|------|--------|-----------------|
| **1. Identify the container** | Which reportable segment holds the entity? | Upstream / Gas & Maritime / Downstream |
| **2. Find related operators** | Public companies with disclosed operations in the same domain | 10-K "Other" segments, annual reports, investor presentations |
| **3. Extract their numbers** | Revenue, profit/loss, production from the relevant geography | Often buried in "Other" or "International" segments |
| **4. Apply fiscal structure** | PSC terms, tax rates, royalty splits, cost recovery caps | R/C index, profit oil split, PIT 38%, export duty 10% |
| **5. Calculate effective take** | Parent company's share after all deductions | Royalty + profit oil share + PIT + research cess |
| **6. Map to container** | Where does this money appear in parent's financials? | Revenue line, inter-segment, cost of revenue |

### PSC Fiscal Structure (Malaysia Template)

For energy/commodity PSCs, the standard back-calculation:

```
Gross Production Value (100%)
├── Royalty to Government:           10%
├── Cost Oil to Contractor:         30-70% (R/C index dependent)
├── Profit Oil:
│   ├── NOC share:                  60-70%
│   └── Contractor share:           30-40%
├── PIT on contractor profit oil:   38% (~12-15% of gross)
├── Research Cess:                  0.5%
└── Export Duty:                    10%
= NOC effective take: ~70-80% of gross production value
```

### Pitfalls for Phase 6

- **Inter-segment pricing ≠ market pricing.** Parent sells to subsidiaries at
  transfer prices, not market. Revenue figures may understate true value.
- **Cost recovery distorts reported revenue.** Contractors recover costs from
  production before profit split — the NOC's reported revenue is AFTER cost
  deduction.
- **International vs domestic split.** Upstream segment often combines Malaysia
  and international operations. Need to separate if possible.
- **Temporal mismatch.** Operator's fiscal year may differ from parent's.
  Contractor's reported loss may reflect carry-forward of exploration costs.

## Pitfalls

### Over-reading is real

Not every interview is 4D chess. Some are genuine public information.
The test: **does the speaker have something to lose by NOT doing this interview?**
If yes = strategic. If no = genuine. If unclear = present both with confidence tags.

### Physical data may not be publicly available

The 7 Physical Questions often can't be fully answered with public data.
That's OK. The VALUE is in asking the questions — it reveals what the
institution is hiding even when you can't get the exact numbers.
Mark gaps as [UNMEASURED] rather than fabricating.

### Temporal decay

Institutional communications have a shelf life. A guarantee given in April
may be an active crisis by August. Always check the temporal anchor:
when was this said, and what has happened since?

### Don't confuse methodology with conclusion

This skill gives you a METHOD for analysis, not a conclusion.
The same methodology applied to different speakers may produce opposite verdicts.
That's correct — the method is neutral.
