# Social Exposure Read — the ladder, not the plan

## When to Use

- He reports that a colleague, friend, or family member may have seen his screen, heard something from his phone, read something over his shoulder, or walked off right after.
- He asks "what are the reality possibilities" / "apa kemungkinan" about another person's reaction to him.
- He is anxious about a moment that has already passed and cannot be undone.

Doctrine owners: `governed-uncertainty` (state beneath words, premature closure) and `human-meaning-membrane` (observation → interpretation → unknowns). This skill is the DELIVERABLE shape for this one recurring ask; it does not restate the invariants.

## The deliverable

A ranked possibility ladder, in plain language, in this order:

1. **The ordinary explanation first** — his own life (toilet, meeting, a boss, a call, a cigarette). Most departures are not about the observer.
2. **He noticed nothing** — a voice, a screen, a sound is ambient in a shared office; nothing encoded, nothing remembered.
3. **He noticed and felt mildly awkward, then dropped it.** No verdict, no story carried forward.
4. **He noticed and could read the register** — this is the only branch that depends on WHAT was exposed, not on who was nearby. Say so, because it is the one variable he can actually reason about.
5. **He noticed and is the type to collect it.** Lowest base rate; name it last and name the condition (pattern of gathering material on people), not as a fear.

Then the one variable that moves the risk, then the behavioural instruction — what NOT to change (how he sits, how he answers, whether he raises it). Then stop.

## The two prohibitions

- **No contingency dialogue.** "If he asks, say one line, tone bored" presumes a conversation the baseline may not support. He closes it in one clause — "we don't talk" — which costs him the turn and shows the agent is modelling a relationship it never checked. Ask the baseline, or leave the script out.
- **No menu.** Not "Option A: ignore, Option B: address, Option C: leave early." A menu converts his anxiety into homework and reads as the agent declining to have a view.

## The instruction that survives any baseline

Behavioural, not verbal: don't become different tomorrow, don't invent a conversation, don't raise the topic, don't ask whether he heard. If a repair is ever needed it will be prompted by the other person, and one flat line answers it ("voice note member aku"). The agent's job is to make sure he does not manufacture the event by acting like one happened.

## Pitfalls

- **Reassurance is not the ask.** He asked for possibilities; a comforting single reading ("he definitely didn't notice") is an answer he cannot use and will not believe. He wants the ranked set so he can see which branches he can rule out from what he knows.
- **After he corrects the baseline, do not re-explain.** One line acknowledging it, then the revised read. Re-deriving the reasoning after being corrected is the second mechanism, and it costs him another turn.
- **Do not scale the event into a doctrine moment.** No talk of trust, exposure, or what this says about him. It is 5 minutes in an office and the response should be the size of the event.
- **His panicking brain wants a cause for the timing.** "He left right after" is the weakest possible evidence and almost always coincidence — say that plainly rather than building a causal chain on it.
