# Shape-Map Generalization

Turning a sample-of-one into a structure that survives contact with reality —
without smuggling in numbers that were never measured.

## 1. The contract: SHAPE, not COUNT

When Arif says his own life is the instrument ("use my reality as verification
and falsification only"), the deliverable is a shape. Percentages are refused —
not out of caution, but because the population does not exist as a surveyable
class. State that refusal structurally in one line: what is visible on a
platform is a distribution of willingness, not of people.

Say it once, plainly, then move on. Repeating the refusal reads as hedging and
is itself a defect.

## 2. Deliverable order (do not reorder)

1. **Invariants** — what does not change across people, places, decades. Each
   one written as a MECHANISM he can check against memory, never as a category
   label. "Hasrat pergi ke badan, bukan melalui badan" is an invariant; "the
   subject is object-oriented" is a label.
2. **Bifurcation** — where the phenomenon splits into two opposite vectors
   that nonetheless answer the same question. Name the shared question, not
   just the split; that is what makes the map explanatory rather than
   taxonomic.
3. **Paradoxes** — self-cancelling pairs in plain sentences. Each should be
   readable as a single line he could repeat out loud.
4. **Falsifiers** — one per invariant, phrased as a concrete observable case
   ("satu malam dia minta sesuatu yang memerlukan dia menyeberang — invarian
   pertama tumbang"). A falsifier that needs a survey is not a falsifier.
5. **What his own life can and cannot test** — explicitly separate items his
   experience verifies (he lived them from inside) from items where he is an
   instance OF the claim and therefore cannot be evidence FOR it. This
   separation is the most valued part of the output; do not skip it.

## 3. Pitfalls

- **Do not promote his data to a population claim.** His sample is valid,
  self-selected, and often better than the literature — which has no studies
  on some sides of these questions at all. Say that, and still keep it [OBS].
- **Do not let a borrowed model outrun its own evidence.** When an invariant
  is imported from literature but only fits the lived case by forcing it,
  the model is wrong, not the case.
- **Price and share-of-population are the two numbers that quietly carry a
  whole thesis.** When either is absent, mark it UNMEASURED and say what
  happens to the model if it lands low. That counterfactual is the finding.
- **A mechanism that sounds biological is not evidence.** Effects must be
  direction-checked against the literature: if the pharmacology is documented
  to REDUCE the drive the argument needs it to increase, the argument fails
  and the honest move is to name the reversal, then offer the smaller claim
  that survives (it releases and amplifies what already exists; it does not
  create).
- **Resist the tidy ending.** If the map closes without a single place where
  it could break, it has been over-fitted to the source material.

## 4. Register for the final pass

When he asks for the map "in full human language", the audit trail disappears:
prose paragraphs, no tier labels, no tables, no bracketed codes, no bold
field-name scaffolding. Every concept gets a kitchen-BM name at first use and
keeps it. Short declarative sentences. Concrete images over abstractions. If a
sentence needs a glossary it fails.

## 5. When it ships as a PDF

Long maps go out as a single PDF, one copy, no signature, no distribution
line attributed to him. Render through typst; typst pitfalls and the verify
ladder live in `pdf-federation` (external canon — do not duplicate here).
Non-negotiables for this class of artifact: title page whose tagline is the
map's own claim; a method note stating what counts as evidence; one section
per structural element; a closing section naming the three things the map
cannot deliver. Verify round-trip text before delivery, and check that the
closing page does not read as an orphaned fragment.
