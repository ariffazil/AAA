# Error patterns caught by fact-check pass — v1 weekly brief (2026-08-07)

## The3 errors found

### 1. Date drift — Warsh "takes over September"
- **Error:** Reported as "Warsh takes over September"
- **Truth:** Kevin Warsh confirmed as Fed chair by Senate May 13, sworn in May 22, 2026. He already chairs the Jul 29 FOMC meeting where he held rates at3.50–3.75%.
- **Catch pattern:** Any temporal claim ("X takes over", "X starting", "happened last") → search for the actual event + date. Verify against Wikipedia or the relevant organization's press release.
- **Source:** federalreserve.gov May 22,2026 press release, Wikipedia "Kevin Warsh" article.

### 2. Invented magnitude — "JPM $4,500 target set when oil was $122"
- **Error:** Attached "when oil was $122" to explain JPMorgan's gold target cut
- **Truth:** JPMorgan cut Q42026 gold target to $4,500 in July 2026 (Yahoo Finance, GoldRepublic). Brent crude never hit $122 in 2026 — the highest was ~$100+ during Hormuz crisis peak.
- **Catch pattern:** Every "because X" or "when X was Y" causal claim → verify the magnitude of Y at the stated time. If the number doesn't appear in price history, drop the causal claim.
- **Source:** TradingEconomics Brent historical data, Yahoo Finance JPM target article Jul 2026.

### 3. Fabricated quote — "Morningstar: odds of rate cut in 2026 have vanished"
- **Error:** Attributed a specific quote to Morningstar
- **Truth:** No such Morningstar quote was located in multiple searches. The sentiment (hawkish Fed) was directionally right, but the specific attribution was fabricated.
- **Catch pattern:** Every quoted phrase must pass a search: exact phrase + source name. If not found, search 3-word substrings. If still not found, remove attribution or hedge as "analysts generally..."
- **Source:** Morningstar.com search (no match), MarketWatch Aug 4 article (different content).

## Key lesson
**Directionally-right is not factually-right.** All3 errors were directionally consistent with the underlying facts (Warsh is hawkish, JPM did cut gold target, Morningstar did write about August volatility) — but the specific details were wrong. The fact-check gate exists precisely to catch these "right direction, wrong coordinates" errors.
