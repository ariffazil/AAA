# Attribution integrity — audit the sourcing before the substance

Secondhand material fails in a fixed order: wrong author, wrong category, then
wrong number. Check in that order. Judging the substance of a claim that was
never made is wasted work that also flatters the source.

## 1. Author lock (cheapest, catches most)

Before evaluating any claim in pasted material, ask: **did the named person
actually say this?** Popular attribution is systematically wrong in a few
recurring ways:

- A later populariser gets folded into the original. Speakers slide between
  two names mid-paragraph so the audience hears one voice.
- A small slip turns a citation into authority (Jung/Young, wrong sexologist,
  one researcher's sample read as a population).
- Real primary evidence is tiny — one letter, one case, one paragraph — and
  gets narrated as a body of work ("he worked on this extensively").

Rule: **when a claim is load-bearing, name the primary artifact** (which
letter, which paper, which year) or mark it unattributed. A name plus a
paraphrase is not a citation.

## 2. Category lock (before cost, before function)

Markets and archetypes get merged because the same customer appears in both.
Split by **who holds the power and what changes hands**, not by who is in the
room:

- Renting access to a finished thing (gaze, display, posing) is not the same
  market as surrendering authorship of it (service, submission, direction).
- Opposite vectors can share a client base. Sharing a customer is not sharing
  a mechanism.
- A paid performance is not evidence of intrinsic desire, and an intrinsic
  desire is not evidence of a market. State which one the evidence supports.

## 3. Number lock

Precise numbers with no source are narration, not data. Two moves:

- Recompute or re-fetch the headline figure from the primary series before
  repeating it. Denominator and tense errors are common ("diagnosed" vs
  "experienced", past-year vs lifetime, national vs sub-sample).
- When a number is missing and the argument depends on it, label it
  UNMEASURED. Never substitute a plausible range for a missing datum — a
  fabricated midpoint propagates into every downstream conclusion.

## 4. Quoted-position lock (when the source audits YOU)

Pasted audits often quote a position the agent never held, then refute it.
Before conceding or defending:

1. Re-read the actual thread; mark each quoted sentence Yours / Not-yours.
2. Open with one line naming the misattribution.
3. Answer only the parts that are genuinely yours.

Conceding a position you never held is as damaging as defending one you did —
it rewrites the record the human is building on.

## 5. Delivery

- Verdict per claim, in order: right / half-right / wrong, each with the
  specific defect named. No narrative of the session.
- Separate "the source is wrong" from "the source is selling something".
  Both matter; they are different defects and need different corrections.
- Close with a short trust block: which claims rest on a primary source, which
  are inference, which are unmeasured.

## Pitfalls

- **A good vibe does not buy credibility.** Production quality, emotional
  register and confidence are uncorrelated with accuracy — a persuasive essay
  can be wrong exactly where it is persuasive.
- **Do not correct the attribution and then adopt the claim anyway.** If the
  author is wrong, the claim needs its own evidence before it can be used.
- **Do not treat a popular correction as established.** Popularisers build
  their own folklore; a famous misattribution is itself often misattributed.
  Verify the correction the same way you verified the original.
