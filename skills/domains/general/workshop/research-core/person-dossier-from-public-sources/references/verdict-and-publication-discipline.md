# Verdict & Publication Discipline

Two separate decisions that get collapsed into one. Keep them apart.

## 1. When a verdict is warranted

A verdict ("earned it" / "held on" / "could not hold") is a personality-scale judgment on a real person. Three gates before issuing one:

1. **Not sitting.** If the subject still holds the office, do not issue a verdict. State plainly that the file is open and why: a ruling on a serving officer is a judgment with consequences the subject cannot answer, and the question the requester actually cares about is not yet decidable. Record what the record shows so far and stop.
2. **Departed, with a simple wall-clock cutoff.** Prefer a single arithmetic cutoff (out of office >= 12 months) over a judgment-requiring one ("5 years, considered case by case"). A cutoff that needs a per-row judgment call reintroduces exactly the discretion it was meant to remove, and the disagreement will surface on the borderline case.
3. **Deceased = always in scope.** Full entry, full verdict permitted.

Interim and vacant periods are not people and do not get verdicts. They get a roster row plus a description of the governance defect the gap represents.

## 2. The rubric — score the seat, not the person

Six dimensions, filled from sourced facts only:

1. **Mandate** — what was the explicit ask when the seat was handed over?
2. **Delivery against that mandate** — measured, with the fiscal year named.
3. **Navigation of constraint** — the political, legal or external crisis actually faced.
4. **Value that outlived the tenure** — institutions, mechanisms, capability.
5. **Transition** — a successor arranged, or a vacuum?
6. **Scar** — what cost or pathology is attributable to the tenure.

Verdict mapping: clear mandate + delivery + clean transition = earned. Mixed, or an unachievable mandate = held.

**"Held" is often the honest verdict for a thin record, not a criticism.** Where the record shows no distinct decision, say so rather than inflate. State that reasoning inside the entry, so a reader does not mistake an evidentiary gap for a failure.

Separate **authored during tenure** from **inherited context** in every entry. A holder is responsible for what happened on their watch, not for what they walked into — collapse those two and the verdict becomes a smear.

## 3. Never publish a verdict stamp on a real person

A verdict badge on an identified living or recently-departed individual converts a research document into a public assertion of fact *about a person* — a different speech act from civic commentary about an *office*. Two independent reasons to strip them from anything publishable:

- **Legal exposure.** Defamation regimes treat a character verdict on a named person differently from fair comment on a public office, and additional statutory layers apply where the subject is a royal or holds a ruler's office.
- **The requester's own exposure — usually the real one.** When the subject set includes officers who hold authority over the requester's employment, the live risk is not litigation, it is the requester's job. Ask which exposure is actually on the table before reasoning about case law; the employment one is not fixed by careful phrasing.

Split the artifacts: the internal dossier behind the vault partition carries verdicts; the public artifact carries a documented chronology with none. A pseudonymous byline does not transfer the exposure — if the artifact sits on the requester's domain, it is the requester's.

## 4. Research gates are not publication gates

An internally-generated plan for a *published* artifact will generate HOLD gates (scope, cutoff, disclosure of the requester\'s affiliation). None of those gates apply to research the requester asked for personally. Do not carry an artifact-specific gate set into the research phase, and do not ask for sign-offs on them. The one decision that belongs to the requester is the terminal one — publish, or keep it in the drawer — asked once, when a draft exists.

## 5. The corrections section

Every dossier built on secondary coverage ends with a **claims-encountered-that-are-false** list. Secondary summaries propagate each other\'s errors, and a belief the requester already holds is the one most likely to be confirmed back to them. Each line: the circulating claim, the correction, the source for the correction. This section is frequently the most valuable page in the document.

## 6. Retraction discipline

If you state a derived figure to the requester before the artifact is built and later find it wrong, open the delivery with the retraction, in plain language, before the artifact. Do not bury it and do not wait to be caught. A number you narrated from memory carries your voice; withdrawing it costs one sentence and buys the credibility of the rest.
