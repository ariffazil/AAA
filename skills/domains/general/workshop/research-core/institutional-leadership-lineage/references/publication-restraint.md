# Publication Restraint — where a fitness verdict may go

A leadership archive contains judgments about real people. Those judgments are legitimate inside a
private research artifact and can become a liability the moment they are published, forwarded, or
attached to a named author. Decide the publication surface at the start, because it changes what you
may write, not just where you file it.

## 1. Incumbents get no verdict

A holder still in office receives the recorded facts and an explicit **VERDICT WITHHELD — IN
OFFICE**. Not a soft verdict, not a "leans positively", not a scale value with a hedge.

Two mechanisms, both mechanical:

- A fitness verdict on a sitting officer is an assessment with consequences the subject cannot
  answer, and it is falsified the moment they act again. The file is genuinely open.
- It dates instantly. An archive that scores a sitting leader becomes stale in a way an archive of
  closed tenures never does.

Write the entry as facts plus a plainly-labelled "what the record shows so far" paragraph, and state
that the file stays open until the tenure closes. This is not evasion — it is the accurate state of
the evidence.

## 2. Recency ladder for non-incumbents

| Holder status | What the artifact may carry |
|---|---|
| In office | Facts; no verdict; no assessment of fitness for the seat |
| Departed inside roughly the last year | Facts; restrained summary; verdict only if the record independently compels it |
| Departed several years and no longer holds related office | Full profile and verdict |
| Deceased | Full profile and verdict; the ordinary dignity rules still apply |

Use a short, wall-clock cutoff rather than a long one. A short rule is one sentence and needs no
per-row political judgement; a long rule invites a case-by-case argument about every borderline
holder, and the borderline cases are exactly where the dispute will be about the verdict, not the
cutoff.

## 3. Public office and corporate office are not the same exposure

A precedent built for assessing heads of government does **not** port onto corporate officers. The
speech-act differs:

- A head of government holds a public office; civic assessment of their performance has a legitimate
  public-interest basis and a body of constitutional precedent behind it.
- A company chair or chief executive is a **corporate officer**. Statements that they are unfit, not
  qualified, or improperly motivated are assertions of fact about a private person's competence and
  conduct, not fair comment on an office, and they carry materially stronger legal exposure.
- Never port a shared visual template or verdict vocabulary from a public-official project onto a
  corporate-officer project just because the two documents look alike. Same layout, different
  speech-act, different risk.

## 4. Whose risk is it

When the requester is an **employee of the institution being profiled**, the risk profile changes
shape, and the change is not the one people expect:

- The realistic pressure channel is **not** litigation by the institution. It is the employment
  relationship: placement decisions, performance review, mobility. There is no judge, no defence and
  no hearing in that channel, which is precisely why it deserves more care than a lawsuit does.
- The exposure follows the **name on the artifact**, not the domain it is hosted under. A pseudonym on
  a page under the requester's own name does not anonymise anyone.
- Therefore: keep the verdict in a private artifact. Do not let a fitness scale on the requester's own
  sitting officers reach a public page.

State this once, factually, when advising — the arithmetic is what matters to the decision, not the
warning tone.

## 5. Twin-artifact split

When a private dossier and a public page are both wanted, split them **at the level of the sentence**,
not by copying one into the other:

| Content | Private dossier | Public page |
|---|---|---|
| Appointment dates, tenures, office architecture | yes | yes |
| Published financials, outcomes, honours | yes | yes, with citation |
| Fitness verdict on a departed holder | yes | optional — see the recency ladder |
| Verdict on an incumbent | no | no |
| Motive attribution, personality diagnosis, private life | no | no |
| Observations available only from the requester's own position | yes, marked as such | no |

Rule of thumb for the public surface: if a fact is already in the institution's own reports, a
regulator filing, or serious press, it may appear with its citation visible. If it exists only
because the requester was inside the institution, it is private-archive material.

## 6. Never in either artifact

- **Motive attribution.** "They knew the figure was misleading and used it anyway" is an assertion
  about a mind, not a fact about a record. Cut it. Report what was said and what the record shows.
- **Personality typing.** A diagnostic label applied to a named living person is not analysis; it is
  a claim about their character with no evidentiary basis. It also reads as an attack, which loses
  the reader you were trying to convince.
- **Private life, family, health, wealth beyond the record.** Not evidence of fitness for a seat.
- **Inherited titles used as explanations.** A social or hereditary honorific does not explain
  behaviour; asserting that it confers protection or influence is an unsourced claim about a
  political system, not a finding about a person.
- **Uncorroborated single-source allegation**, however topical. One partisan source is not a finding.
  Record it as unverified and keep it out of the profile body, or leave it out entirely.

## 7. Test before publishing

For each judgment line, ask: *would this sentence survive being read by the person it is about, and
by the requester's employer?* If the answer is no, either source it to something published already,
or move it to the private artifact, or drop it. Writing it in a private file is a decision to keep it
private — honour it when the artifact is later reformatted for a public surface.
