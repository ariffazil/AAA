# Worked example — PETRONAS chairman & President/CEO lineage (1974 to present)

> Use as a model of the spine table and source-status discipline, and as the
> factual starting point for any PETRONAS leadership question. Every row is
> VERIFIED on at least two public sources or explicitly flagged; keep the flags.

| Office | Appointed by | Function |

## Claims that circulate about this lineage — all checked, all wrong

Each of these appears in otherwise reliable summaries, is carried into requests as established fact,
and is false. Test any brief that contains them before building on it, and publish the correction in
the archive's **claims corrected** section.

1. **PETRONAS did not acquire Talisman Energy.** A rival acquired it. PETRONAS' Talisman-linked
transaction that year was its subsidiary buying the Montney assets. Any "PETRONAS Talisman
writedown" attributed to a CEO of that era is unsupported.
2. **There was no VSS in 2015.** PETRONAS told Parliament it would not implement one, and the CEO
publicly denied pay cuts and retrenchment. Separation-scheme discussion begins the following year —
with the shareholder, not the company.
3. **A large special dividend is routinely mis-dated by one fiscal year.** PETRONAS reports dividends
*paid during* a year; summary tables attribute them to the *profit year*. State which basis you use,
and check the special component before quoting a record payout.
4. **The incumbent CEO's background is misreported.** The firm named in most summaries is not the one
he came from, and his professional body is the English institute, not the association usually cited.
5. **A chairman's tenure is commonly extended into his successor's years.** Check every chair row
against two sources; the handover is where these errors cluster.

**Structural point worth carrying:** a large impairment or write-off is booked in the year it lands,
which is routinely *after* the officer who authorised the bet has left. Anchor the decision to the
year it was made and the write-off to the year it was booked, and say which is which.

## What this example teaches

1. **The interregnum (Jul 2004 - Jun 2012, 7 years 11 months) is the headline
   finding, not a missing name.** After the sitting chairman died in office, the
   chair was carried in an *acting* capacity by two successive Presidents/CEOs,
   and a substantive non-executive appointment only resumed in July 2012 — the
   company's own appointment release then said the change would "restore clear
   demarcation of roles between the chairman and president and CEO", i.e. the
   institution stated publicly that the separation had been absent. Any search
   for a "chairman" inside that window returns nothing — the hole is the fact.
   **Compute the span from the two dates; do not round it to an impression.**
2. **Two rows are genuinely open and must stay marked, not smoothed.** The year
   the chair was first split from the chief-executive office is undocumented
   (first firm evidence 1986), and one mid-career chairman's start year could not
   be recovered although his occupancy is confirmed by contemporaneous trade
   press and his February 1988 retirement. Publish both as open questions.
3. **The appointed-ness of the chair is written into the statute.** The governing
   petroleum act subjects the company to the head of government's "control and
   direction", and a contemporary legal analysis of that act states the power to
   appoint the chairman is vested in the head of government and not in the board.
4. **The appointment mechanism changed twice.** Executive chair with
   cabinet-equivalent standing (1974-76), then a chair often drawn from the
   executive or the civil service, then from 2012 a non-executive appointment on
   a 3-year contractual cycle. Read the mechanism before judging the holder.
5. **Personal compensation is not symmetrically disclosed.** Individual pay is
   not published as a listed company must; board composition, directors' fees
   and meeting allowances appear in each Integrated Report's governance
   section. Write NOT FOUND rather than inferring a figure. Where a whole-board
   aggregate is published, it is still not the holder's pay — do not divide it.
6. **Fan-out as applied:** one subagent per holder or era-cluster (1974-76,
   the two chair-and-chief holders, the 1980s chairmen, the long CEO tenures,
   the three recent chairmen), one separate task for the money lane, each task
   carrying the mandatory citation clause. The spine above was fixed by hand
   first so every child worked against a stable office/tenure map.
