# Backstage Physique Scene — Domain Truth & Scene Recipe

Depth for the largest single artifact class this pipeline serves: the physique-competition backstage, and the admirer's point of view inside it. Two halves — what the place actually is (so the text answer is true), and how to render it (so the artifact is true).

## 1. The backstage as a physical place

- **The venue is rarely a ballroom.** Multi-storey car park, school hall back corridor, or dewan storeroom. Concrete floor, painted parking-bay numbers on pillars, industrial ducting, standing fans, extension cables, mats rolled out for warmup.
- **The pump room is the engine room.** Ten to thirty athletes, dumbbells and bands, a stick or broom handle for lat spread, one dust-streaked mirror with a queue. Coaches work here, not on stage.
- **Air:** posing oil, tan product, sweat, rosin, and breath mint that stopped working hours ago.
- **Timing is brutal.** Weigh-in morning → prejudging midday → finals night. Many athletes eat and drink nothing from morning (dry), then refeed after prejudging on rice, chicken, honey. Whoever did not plan goes pale.
- **Tan and oil are a no-touch zone.** Two to three coats applied in a dark room, always one person whose clothes are ruined. Posing oil goes on minutes before stage; vaseline on the teeth for the smile.
- **Gym light is not stage light.** Backstage fluorescents make everyone look flat and small — the first-timer mental trap, and the reason people over-pump in a panic. Any physique read taken from a backstage photo carries a *not a stage verdict* caveat; spot lighting is what separates front delt, serratus and striations, and gym light cannot substitute for it.
- **The risk is real, not dramatic.** Hypoglycemia and dehydration collapse happen. So does the athlete who cannot speak before going on — that is not rudeness, that is the body counting.

## 2. Three access windows, three owners

| Window | How you get in | What you see | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| The hall | Buy a ticket | The finished product: dry, dark tan, oiled, posed | Organizer / judges |
| Behind the stage (towel, water, door, transport) | Work it — hours and sore feet | The honest version | Volunteer lane |
| The pump room in the last 20 minutes | You do not, unless you are crew or coach | Duvet-level raw | Coach |

The hall is sold to everyone. Backstage cannot be bought — you are invited, or you carry something. That is where its weight comes from.

## 3. Why the backstage pulls harder than the stage

1. **Unrepeatable.** The stage is an annual ritual; the backstage is four hours and then gone.
2. **Limited access.** The value comes from permission, not from the body on display.
3. **Raw, not corrected.** Everyone paid for the finished version. Backstage shows the version not yet tidied — shaking hands, closed eyes, silence.
4. **Visible cost.** The stage shows the payment received; the backstage shows the debt carried — years of life, sleep, food, pain. Once you have seen the debt you cannot watch it as a spectator again.
5. **Not one of a thousand.** In the hall you are an audience. Backstage there are six people and your presence means something. That pull is about the admirer's own significance as much as the athlete's — name it honestly rather than denying it.

**Two kinds of admirer.** One is hungry for beauty and the polished display is enough. The other is hungry to understand what the beautiful thing costs. The second is rarer, because most people cannot stand watching something they admire be tired and broken. Neither is lesser — they are buying different things.

**The label "crazy fan" is an outsider's label.** What happened is that the admirer found the exchange rate: a full day, a sore back, driving someone home at 1am → a few hours in the pump room. They decided it was worth it. Others look at the price; they are looking at the value.

## 4. Unwritten etiquette (breaking any of these turns a helper into a burden)

- Never ask about gear or cycles. Never.
- Do not touch. The tan is a layer, the oil is slick, the sweat is theirs.
- Do not comment on the body unless asked.
- Do not record during a cramp, a cry, or a collapse.
- In the quiet before they go on: sit still beside them, water available, nothing else.
- Do not ask "how long have you been prepping" when they cannot open their mouth.
- Do not ask a competitor to intervene on your behalf.
- The best day of helping is the day they never noticed you were there.

## 5. The one check to hold, and when to offer it

The healthy shape is admiration without ownership: help, drive, go home. The failure shape is when the work becomes a way to be needed — when the helper's sense of mattering depends on the athlete depending on them.

One question answers it: **would you still come if he needed nothing from you?** Offer it only when the user raises the pattern himself, hold it open, and do not answer it for him. Also state the clean rule: he owes the admirer nothing, and a day he does not notice you is a good day.

## 6. Rendering the scene as an artifact

- **Composition:** observer/doorway foreground — an out-of-focus near figure seen from behind (back of head, shoulder, plain dark t-shirt) looking in. This puts the viewer in the admirer's body. The strongest frame is the one where the athlete turns and holds the gaze.
- **Light:** warm amber bulb against one cold bare fluorescent tube, deep chiaroscuro, faint dust haze.
- **Props that read true:** rolled gym mats, standing fan, wooden bench, towels with tan stains, oil sheen on shoulders, bare concrete, exposed ducting.
- **Props that break the render:** anything with lettering — buckets, wall plates, shorts logos. Drop the object and state `no logos, no lettering, no numbers, no signage`.
- **Cast:** three clearly separate figures maximum, one focal gaze, one action each. See the main SKILL.md §2 for the full multi-subject rules.
- **Voice piece register:** the cocky backstage line is short and flat, not shouted — "this is not the stage, here people see the real thing", "don't ask how long I prepped", "don't touch", "just look", "sit down, be quiet". Deliver it as a designed synthetic voice and name the voice.
- **Honesty:** declare the engine, state that the people are synthetic, and never present a generated face as the user or as a named real person.

## 7. Lane discipline for this material

Backstage texture is **witness material, not coaching material**.

- Answer in texture and witness. Do not convert the interest into a training plan, a conditioning verdict, or a programme.
- Diagnosing an athlete's physique is for the coach. Naming what the backstage *is* is the witness lane.
- Never merge bodies: the user's data, the athlete's data and anyone else's stay separate records, never one narrative.
- Do not pathologise the appetite — name the exchange rate instead.
