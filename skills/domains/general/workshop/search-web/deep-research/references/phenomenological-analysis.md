# Phenomenological / Embodied Analysis Methodology — Session 2026-08-30

## When to Use
User shares deeply personal, embodied, or identity-based content and asks for "deep analysis." Content covers sexuality, body politics, gender, desire, cultural identity, or lived experience. The standard Phase 1-5 web-research workflow is INSUFFICIENT — it treats everything as empirical claims to be searched and verified.

## The Core Distinction

Three types of claims require different validation:

| Type | Example | Validation | NOT Validated By |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Empirical** | "Nipple stimulation activates genital cortex" | Literature, fMRI, reproducible experiments | First-person experience alone |
| **Phenomenological** | "The chest is the living center" | First-person repeatability, consistency across time, resonance with indigenous frameworks | Western IRB surveys, Likert scales |
| **Archetypal** | "The powerful seek the witness" | Cross-cultural pattern (Sacred Band, bissu, hijra, shudo), recurrence in user's life | Single-session observation |

Most personal deep-research requests are MIXED. Handle each type with its appropriate validation.

## Procedure

### Phase 0: Identify Content Type
Before any search: Is this primarily empirical, phenomenological, or archetypal?

### Phase 1: Center Indigenous Vocabulary
CRITICAL: Do NOT default to English academic terminology when the user's experience has a richer term in their own language.

- Malay embodied experience → center "rasa" not "somatic intelligence"
- Rasa = sensation + emotion + intuition + body wisdom + spiritual knowing (covers MORE territory than any English equivalent)
- English academic terms are often LESS precise than indigenous originals
- Pattern: AI trained on English academic text translates user's experience into degraded English vocabulary

### Phase 2: Web Research (Modified Purpose)
- NOT to "prove" the user's experience is correct
- YES to find cross-cultural parallels (anthropology)
- YES to ground specific biological claims (neuroscience)
- YES to honestly assess what literature exists vs. claimed gaps

### Phase 3: Synthesis Structure
1. Name the pattern (archetype/dynamic identified)
2. Ground it (evidence that supports or complicates)
3. Center their vocabulary (user's own terms, not imported)
4. Honest assessment (what survives vs. overclaimed)
5. "So What" (meaning for user's life/framework)

### Phase 4: External AI Audit Protocol
External AI excels at: empirical falsification, literature grounding, overclaim detection, ethical risk.
External AI misses: phenomenological claims, archetypal patterns, indigenous epistemologies, poetry-as-valid-knowledge.

Correct audit: External AI validates empirical layer. Phenomenological layer validated by first-person repeatability. Archetypal layer by cross-cultural evidence. Don't let one validator overwrite the others.

## Pitfalls
1. Overclaiming universality from first-person data → repair to "In some individuals..."
2. Defaulting to English academic terms for non-Western concepts → check native vocabulary first
3. Treating phenomenology as failed science → body-philosophy is not biological claim
4. Skipping cross-cultural evidence → user's personal pattern may map to documented anthropology
5. External AI false equivalence → when Perplexity falsifies phenomenology, name the wrong epistemological standard
