# Business Advisory — Capital-Constrained Entrepreneurship

> When Arif asks "what business should X start with Y capital" — the workflow for
> researching a person's background and recommending business opportunities.

## Pipeline

### 1. Person Research (parallel)
- `web_search` for exact Instagram username (first result = public IG URL + follower count)
- `web_extract` on IG URL (minimal data if public; "private" if locked)
- `web_search` `site:linkedin.com "[name]"` — career history, employers, roles, tenure
- `web_search` for full name to catch cross-platform presence
- **Instagram blocks headless browsers** (chrome-error://chromewebdata/) — never try browser_exec on IG
- When IG is private, LinkedIn is the richest source for professional context

### 1b. Reality Map (5 Questions)
When the person is NOT someone Arif has deep context on, ask 5 questions before advising. These map the real constraints that determine whether any recommendation works:

1. **Employment status** — "Kerja sekarang, masih ke?" → determines if business is side or full-commit, changes risk tolerance entirely.
2. **Capital source** — "RM50k tu saving berapa bulan, atau semua duit yang ada?" → ALL savings = zero margin for error = pick safest option. Disposable = can take calculated risks.
3. **Family load** — "Dah kahwin? Ada anak?" → commitment burden determines burn rate tolerance and time availability.
4. **Hidden advantage** — "Apa benda yang orang selalu tanya hang pasal?" → the thing people instinctively ask THIS person for = their unrecognized unfair advantage.
5. **Risk floor** — "Kalau gagal bulan ke-3, hang buat apa?" → determines if safety net exists. Some people can afford to fail; some cannot. The answer changes the entire recommendation.

**Rule:** Do NOT skip these when advising a third party Arif brought to you. A wrong LinkedIn identity match can send the entire recommendation sideways — the5 questions catch it in 60 seconds.

### 2. Skill Mapping
Extract from LinkedIn/web:
- Current and past employers
- Job titles and tenure
- Industry domain (e-commerce, O&G, construction, F&B, etc.)
- Specific competencies (logistics, operations, sales, technical, creative)
- Professional network (industry contacts, platforms used)

Label epistemic source: OBS (direct from profile), DER (inferred from role), INT (interpretive)

### 3. Capital Constraint Analysis
Given the capital amount (e.g., RM50k):
- What businesses can START at this level? (not scale — START)
- Break-even timeline realistic for this capital
- Monthly burn rate vs revenue projection
- Risk of total loss

### 4. Advantage Matching
The key question: **What unfair advantage does this person already have?**
- Industry knowledge (e.g., Shopee backend ops → knows seller algorithm)
- Professional network (e.g., O&G contacts → supply chain access)
- Technical skills (e.g., DevOps → can build own tools)
- Location advantage (e.g., near factory area → supply access)

**Rule:** Recommend businesses that leverage EXISTING advantages, not generic suggestions. A person with 4 years in e-commerce operations starting a nasi lemak stall is ignoring their unfair advantage.

### 5. Recommendation (collapse to ONE)
- Lead with the top recommendation
- Name 2-3 alternatives briefly
- State what NOT to do and why
- End with the one question that determines direction

## Pitfalls

1. **Don't recommend F&B to everyone.** Malaysian business advice defaults to "open nasi lemak stall." Only recommend F&B if the person has food industry experience or the capital is too small for other options.

2. **Don't ignore the person's age.** A 37-year-old with 6+ years professional experience has different risk tolerance and network than a 22-year-old fresh grad. Match recommendation to life stage.

3. **Don't assume capital = investment.** RM50k might be ALL their savings, or it might be side money. The stakes change the recommendation. If unknown, note it.

4. **LinkedIn is more useful than Instagram for business advisory.** IG shows lifestyle; LinkedIn shows capability. When the question is "what business," LinkedIn wins.

5. **Don't recommend online-only businesses without product reality.** "Start a Shopee store" without a concrete product or supply chain insight is generic advice. Either name the product category or don't recommend it.

6. **Bridge protocol still applies.** Answer first, collapse the noise, direct BM Penang register. No tables, no bullet lists for the final recommendation — one clean paragraph.

7. **LinkedIn identity trap — NEVER assume.** Multiple people share common names ("Luqman Nurhaqim" had 6+ LinkedIn profiles). When you find a LinkedIn match, treat it as UNVERIFIED until the human confirms. Building an entire recommendation on an assumed identity is worse than no recommendation — it erodes trust. State the assumption explicitly: "Aku nampak profile LinkedIn ni, tapi aku tak confirm dia orang yang sama. Hang kenal ke?" If IG is private and no LinkedIn confirmation, fall back to the5 reality-map questions FIRST, then advise from their answers — not from guessed background.

8. **Ethical constraints shape business choice.** When a person signals strong religious values ("Allah akan tanya"), that eliminates entire business categories — anything with riba, gambling, dubious halal status, or exploitation. Never recommend a business that conflicts with their moral framework, even if the numbers work. A person who won't sleep at night over ethical doubts will quit the business regardless of profit.

9. **When you know nothing, ask first.** If the person has minimal online presence (private IG, no LinkedIn, no public data), do NOT fabricate a profile from assumptions. The5 reality-map questions (Step 1b) exist for exactly this case. Ask them, get answers, THEN advise. One good question beats ten minutes of research that returns the wrong person.
