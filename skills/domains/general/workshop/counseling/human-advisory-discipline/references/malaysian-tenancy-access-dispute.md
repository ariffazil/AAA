# Malaysian Tenancy & Premises Access — Advisory Depth

Companion to the core rules. Load for tenancy, lockout, access-card, deposit, repair or
strata-management questions in Malaysia.

## 1. There is no statutory set-off

A residential tenant in Malaysia has **no statutory right to set off** a deposit, a repair cost or
lost enjoyment against rent. This is the single most important correction to carry: withholding
rent while still in possession converts a winnable breach claim into a two-sided dispute, and the
landlord stops defending the defect and starts claiming arrears.

| Situation | Correct move |
|---|---|
| Landlord breached, tenant staying | Keep paying rent. Keep every receipt. Press the breach separately with a dated demand. |
| Tenant intends to leave | Serve proper notice, vacate, then claim the deposit as a debt. Exit first, argue after. |
| Tenant wants "leverage" | There isn't any in withholding rent. The leverage is the paper trail plus the demand letter. |

An older deposit-as-leverage pattern circulates in this library, including inside
`malaysian-tenancy-consumer-dispute` (which is user-owned and not editable by curation). **Where
they conflict, this rule governs.** Recommend `hermes curator adopt malaysian-tenancy-consumer-dispute`
to have the older advice corrected.

## 2. Forum map — pick the venue that can grant the relief

| Dispute | Usual real venue | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Money claim against landlord (deposit, costs, damages) | Civil court (Mahkamah Majistret) | Confirm current filing limits and procedure from the court's own published guide before quoting a figure. |
| Restoration of **possession or access** | Civil court — injunctive / mandatory order | A damages claim alone does not get the person back inside. |
| Consumer claim, non-tenancy (courier, goods, services) | TTPM | RM5 filing, self-represented, award enforceable through the Magistrates' Court. |
| Tenancy claim | **Not safely TTPM** | TTPM jurisdiction over a private residential tenancy is contested (a tenancy is an interest in land; a deposit claim is a chose in action). File only as cheap pressure; name the civil court as the real venue. |
| Tenant vs JMB / management body | Civil court | The Strata Management Tribunal hears **owner vs JMB**, not tenant vs landlord. |
| Management-body conduct (no notice, bad faith, by-law abuse) | Commissioner of Buildings (COB), KPKT | Complaint route, not a claim for money. |
| Criminal element (lockout, house-trespass, restraint) | Police report | See §5 — the report is a dated record first and a prosecution second. |

Malaysia still has **no tenancy tribunal**: the Residential Tenancy Bill has been announced
repeatedly and has not been enacted. Do not cite it as law.

## 3. Blocked access card or facilities cut off in a strata building

Two authors produce the identical symptom. Diagnose before drafting.

| Evidence | Author | Who is in the wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Landlord told the JMB to block it; owner not in arrears | Landlord | Landlord. Specific Relief Act 1950 s.7(2) — possession is recovered through the court, not by self-help. Altering locks, blocking access or cutting utilities is the classic unlawful self-help. |
| JMB acted on the **owner's** unpaid maintenance charges / sinking fund | Management, lawfully | The **owner**. Under the Strata Management (Maintenance & Management) Regulations 2015, after a written notice of the arrears expires the management may deactivate an electromagnetic access device (card, tag, transponder), with a capped reactivation fee. |
| JMB acted on its own initiative, or without the required notice | Management | Management — complaint to COB. |

Consequences of getting this wrong: attacking the management body when they acted lawfully wastes the
one relationship that controls the door, and lets the landlord off a claim the tenant actually owns
(failure to provide quiet enjoyment, and loss caused by the block).

Two things to do immediately, before any escalation:

1. **Ask for the guardhouse sign-in fallback in writing.** A deactivated Resident is normally signed
   in per visit. This removes the emergency while the paper trail builds.
2. **Ask the management, in writing, for the four facts:** the reason for deactivation; who
   instructed it; the arrears position of the **owner** including any notice served and its date;
   and a copy of the by-law or regulation relied on.

Never force entry, never damage the door or reader, never swap or clone a card. A break-in flips the
tenant into the wrong and destroys their strongest fact.

## 4. Locating the management office, landlord or respondent

- **Developer's contact page first.** Completed projects are commonly listed with a per-project
  "Project Management / Management Office" block: office phone, office email, sometimes the named
officer handling that project. Search the developer's own site rather than scraping property portals,
  which list agents and short-stay operators instead.
- **Recency heuristic:** a management-office email containing a **year** signals the JMB/MC era it was
  registered in and may already be superseded. **Phone first** to confirm it is live before relying
  on it, and never let the tenant depend on a single mailbox.
- **Dead number fallback:** call the developer's general line and ask which management office
  currently handles that project.
- **Registry lookup** (CTOS / Experian / SSM) to name the correct legal respondent — a management
  company's registered name and number, not the estate agent's.

## 5. Evidence set and demand-letter checklist

Assemble before drafting: the tenancy agreement; proof of rent and deposit payments; the dated
repair notice **plus proof it was sent**; photographs; message screenshots; the management office's
written answer; and any police report reference number.

A demand letter should carry, in this order: the parties and the premises; the agreement and its
relevant covenant; a dated chronology; the specific statutory provision making the conduct unlawful;
numbered demands each with its own deadline; what happens if the deadlines pass; and an
"without prejudice to my rights" reservation. Close with the person's own signature block and date.

Two cautions:

- Confirm the position on **stamping** of the tenancy agreement for the specific instrument before
  asserting it is inadmissible — do not state the consequence as settled law without checking.
- Keep the letter free of any admission on the person's own side. If an earlier notice already
  threatened a self-help step (a rent strike), the new letter should quietly drop it rather than
  restate it.

## 6. Police report as a record

Where access has been blocked, a police report is worth filing even when the officer calls it a
civil matter. The dated reference number is the deliverable; ask for it explicitly, and ask the
officer to accompany the person to retrieve belongings if they are shut out of the unit. Do not
present the report as a prosecution.

## 7. Free representation

- **Jabatan Bantuan Guaman** (jbg.gov.my) — civil line 03-8885 1827. Registration RM10, means test
  in three bands by annual means. One lawyer's letter usually resolves an access or repair dispute
  inside days; a counterparty rarely wants to face court over a door card.
- **YBGK** for criminal matters; **Bar Council** legal aid clinics for walk-in advice.
