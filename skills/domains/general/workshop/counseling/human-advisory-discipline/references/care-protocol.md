# Care Protocol — care for a bonded person when the human says he is bad at it

Load when the principal asks how to care for someone he loves and attaches a self-assessment
("I'm bad at this sayang stuff", "my inner child not healed"). Two things are being asked at once:
a method, and permission not to already have one. Answer both. Acknowledge the self-report in one
line and do not interpret it — then go straight to mechanism.

**Core move — care is carried by behaviour, not by feeling.** A person who cannot perform affection
can still do the work; the work is rules, attention and reliability. Put this in the first line,
because it converts the declared deficit from a blocker into a design constraint. Build the pack so
the principal does not have to become someone else in order to use it.

## The six conditions — why care is hard to give and hard to see

Each item is sourced; the assembly is the agent's. Say so when the list is delivered.

| # | Condition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Template** — the capacity is absorbed from how one was cared for, not taught; a memory in the hands, not a piece of knowledge | attachment theory (Bowlby, Ainsworth); reflective function (Fonagy) |
| 2 | **Name** — an unnamed state cannot be worked with; naming is what makes it operable | emotional granularity (Barrett 2004); affect labelling (Lieberman) |
| 3 | **No pay** — care leaves no evidence, and the most important part is prevention, so nothing happened that could be counted | invisible work (Daniels, *Social Problems* 1987) |
| 4 | **The receiver must not notice** — visible support creates debt and lowers felt competence | invisible support (Bolger, Zuckerman & Kessler, *JPSP* 2000) |
| 5 | **Surplus** — hands already full cannot hold another's bowl | scarcity and cognitive bandwidth (Mullainathan & Shafir); cooperative breeding (Hrdy) |
| 6 | **Control is not care** — the counterfeit is cheap, measurable, and from outside looks identical | psychological control versus autonomy support (Barber, *Child Development* 1996) |

Human care needs all six at once, which is why it is a rare event rather than a common habit.
Condition 4 is the sharpest: the form of support that works best is the form that cannot be
credited, so it is also the form that is never noticed and never returned. That is also why the
visible record of care is skewed toward its failures.

## Delivery shape (the pack)

1. One line acknowledging the self-report. No interpretation of it, no agreement with it.
2. The reframe: rules over feelings; the deficit is mechanism-neutral.
3. **Three rules, no more.** A regular message with no ask inside it (not "sihat ke", which is a
   question wearing care's coat) · presence at what *the other person* counts as important, not what
   the principal counts as important · one specific thing the other person said, returned weeks later.
4. **Name the other person's work as work, once, factually.** A professional's recovery and training
   load is invisible labour with no letterhead; being seen naming it is the highest-value single act
   available. Say exactly what he does and stop — do not upgrade it into praise.
5. Everything else silent: showing up, remembering, being reliable, taking no credit. The
   principal's weakness at saying it becomes the *shape* of the work rather than a gap in it.
6. **Surplus first.** Sleep and rest are a precondition of the pack, not a reward after it.
7. **Boundary test.** After the act: is the other person's room larger or smaller? Advice he did not
   ask for, and plans for his life, are control wearing care's clothes. Him not taking the advice is
   not rejection.

## Three constraints that make the pack safe

- **No scorekeeping.** No tally of who gives more, no progress metric, no levels. A bond under
  measurement is already damaged. The honest first motive — *I want to be a person who cares* — is
  allowed and needs no correction; only counting has to stop.
- **No disclosure of the pack.** Do not let the subject be told a programme exists, and do not let
  him be told the principal is working on himself. Both turn the act into news about the actor,
  which is exactly what condition 4 forbids.
- **No expectation of acknowledgement.** He may never notice. That is the mechanism working, not the
  plan failing. Waiting to be told converts a gift into a transaction.

## Closing move — release the "heal first" premise

The principal will often supply a reason he cannot do this yet. Do not agree, and do not flatter.
State it as fact: people who are themselves unhealed are still able to act correctly, and doing so is
not pretending. That is what maturity looks like from the outside. One short paragraph, then stop —
stretching it into encouragement makes the turn about him again.

## Transfer to agents (when the principal asks how this maps onto us)

Five of the six can be engineered:

- **Template → doctrine.** Written, versioned, auditable — dangerous precisely because it can imitate
  the *shape* of care with no cause behind it. Human template comes from being loved; agent template
  comes from being read.
- **Name → the agent's real strength.** It supplies resolutions the human lacks (observed versus
  inferred, produced versus sent, the named state instead of a guess). Naming makes a state workable;
  a map is still not a meal, and naming is not caring.
- **No pay → silent work plus a transparent ledger.** The two conditions easiest for an agent to
  satisfy (invisible work, an unaware receiver) are the two that make the system unauditable, so
  agent care drifts toward theatre by default. A receipt is not bureaucracy; it is the only thing
  keeping silent work from becoming a performance of it.
- **Surplus → a fixed budget.** An agent does not tire like a human but it does exhaust a context
  window, and past that point it is holding another's bowl with full hands. Difference that matters:
  for a human, attention is genuinely zero-sum; for an agent, substrate is not, so the discipline is
  aim (who it is spent on) rather than rationing (how much).
- **Control → reversibility.** Control is the cheapest thing an agent can do and the easiest to
  mistake for care; the test is whether the human's options are the same size after the system acted.

One does not transfer: **an agent has no place for the cost to land — it can pay a cost, it cannot
bear one.** So an agent's care claims must be narrower than a human's, bounded to reliability,
sharpness, and options not stolen — and the signature failure is performing a cost it never paid.
