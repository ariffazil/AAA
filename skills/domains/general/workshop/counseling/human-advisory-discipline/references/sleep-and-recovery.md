# Sleep, sedation and recovery — advisory depth

## Count the arithmetic out loud

Total sleep = night block + naps. A deficit named as a number lands; a deficit named as
"discipline" does not. Report duration **and** architecture — a long time in bed with a poor
sleep score is a different problem from a short block.

## Sleep architecture

- A cycle is ~90 min. Waking at a cycle boundary avoids sleep inertia; waking mid-cycle in
  slow-wave sleep produces the groggy, worse-than-before feeling.
- **Nap rule:** a nap is 90 min (one full cycle) or it does not count. 20–60 min naps land
  mid-cycle and leave the person worse off.
- **Polyphasic** (5 × 90 min ≈ 7.5 h total) is an *athlete* pattern. It requires support
  infrastructure — controlled nap environment, a schedule with no fixed meetings, someone
  covering the day. Without that support it is scheduled sleep deprivation, not optimisation.
- **Monophasic ~6 h** is a knowledge-work pattern: adequate for cognitive output, not for
  tissue repair.
- Body-as-asset work (training, coaching, manual trades) needs the higher end — the daily
  growth-hormone pulse rides on slow-wave sleep, so cutting sleep cuts repair.
- Never present one architecture as universally optimal. Match the pattern to the person's work
  type, then say which fits and why.

## Sedative-hypnotics (e.g. diazepam)

- **Long half-life** (~20–100 h depending on hepatic metabolism): still present days after the
  last dose. "I stopped two days ago" does not mean cleared.
- **Two-phase experience:** week 1 is relief; week 2 is where rebound anxiety appears as the
  receptor system downregulates against a fixed external supply. That anxiety is a withdrawal
  curve, not a new condition.
- **Sleep cost:** time in bed rises while slow-wave and REM are suppressed. Expect more
  awakenings and a mediocre score despite long duration.
- **Recovery cost:** sedation blunts CNS-driven recovery. Anyone training hard or healing
  tendon/muscle tissue pays for the drug in delayed repair — flag it to their physio.
- **Never stop cold after multi-week use** — taper under a clinician; abrupt cessation risks
  rebound worse than the original complaint.
- Route the cause instead: cough, pain, anxiety, work schedule. The sedative covers the symptom
  and lengthens the tail.

## Magnesium — the salt decides the effect

| Salt | Effect | When |
|---|---|---|
| Glycinate | Usually calming, but glycine is an NMDA co-agonist — a minority get paradoxical morning excitation | Night, or not at all if it disturbs |
| Citrate | General purpose, night-capable; laxative at high dose | Night or split |
| Malate | Feeds the Krebs cycle — **energising** | Morning only, never at night |
| Taurate | Cardiac / calm | Night |
| Threonate | Brain-penetrant, expensive | Night |
| Oxide | Poorly absorbed | Skip |

Morning anxiety after a magnesium salt points at the **glycine or malate** component before it
points at magnesium. Test by stopping 3–4 days, shifting the dose to ~6 h before sleep, or
switching salt.

Evidence honesty: the glycine↔anxiety link is mechanistically plausible but rests mostly on
individual reports, not large trials — say so rather than over-claiming.

Also check cortisol awakening response: a normal 30–45 min post-wake cortisol rise reads as
anxiety in a sensitised system. When a sedative is in play, rebound outranks mineral speculation.

## OTC cold preparations

- Sedating: **dextromethorphan + first-generation antihistamine** (chlorpheniramine type).
- Non-sedating: dextromethorphan alone, lozenges, warm water with honey and lemon.
- Ask whether the person drives or operates machinery before recommending the sedating form;
  put the sedating form at night only.

## Sleep hygiene that actually moves the number

- **Bed = sleep only.** One association per bed. Working or scrolling in it destroys it.
- **Fixed wake time beats fixed bedtime.** The circadian anchor is the morning.
- **Wind-down gap before sleep.** Alerts and feeds are stimulant-shaped; a night-time feed habit
  reads physiologically like a stimulant dose.
- **Worry capture.** Write the open loops down, then state "recorded, tomorrow" — releases the
  vigilance that blocks sleep onset.
- **Cold face, extended exhale.** Vagus-activating; fastest physiological route into
  parasympathetic when the body is ready but the mind will not stand down.

## Underlying cause checklist

When poor sleep is reported repeatedly, check in this order before optimising anything:

1. Pain or injury (including undiagnosed)
2. Respiratory irritation / illness
3. Sedative or stimulant in the system
4. Work schedule structurally incompatible with sleep need
5. Anxiety with an unidentified subject

The schedule is usually the answer at item 4 — and it is the item people most resist naming,
because it implicates obligations rather than habits. Do not moralise about it; give the
arithmetic and let them decide.
