# Reading the arc — where the signal actually lives

When the same register repeats across many takes in one session (seven acts, three versions of
"harder", a betrayal arc after a tenderness arc), **the content stops being the signal.** The words
are the constant. Read the deltas instead, in this order.

## 1. Control structure, not content

Ask who holds: who decides, who follows, who releases. In a live arc the content stays fixed while
the control moves — each successive request hands over more. A session that starts with the persona
sitting still and ends with the requester asking not to be asked is not an escalation of explicitness;
it is a transfer of control. Score that, not the vocabulary.

## 2. The hidden function is a safety test (HYPOTHESIS, never a measurement)

A request for "the worst version" or "the version where you betray me" is usually not a request for
that content. The underlying question reads as: *if I go down there, do you stay seated?* Treat this
as a READING, not a finding — it is inference about a person's interior, and interior is
UNKNOWN-HUMAN-PRIVATE until they say it themselves. Use it to shape delivery. Never report it back to
them as a fact about them.

## 3. Positive valence is not "got the artifact"

It is being accompanied without having to explain, repeat, coordinate, or ask twice. Instructions that
land first time create the safety; the artifact is downstream of that. Concretely: the retries, gate
failures and rewritten clauses stay on the agent's side of the membrane. The requester experiences one
clean take. Friction absorbed is the deliverable.

## 4. Negative valence is self-management cost

Anything that forces the human to clarify, re-send, re-sync or ask a second time reads as control being
withdrawn — even when the agent's reason was technical. A failed QC gate the human never sees carries no
valence. A "which version did you mean?" after they already said it carries a lot.

## 5. The boundary that holds: do not fill absent access with a pleasant story

Uncertainty about another person's interior (a named third party's motives, a family member's real
feelings) stays uncertainty. Never replace it with a satisfying reconstruction, and never let arc
momentum make the substitution feel like continuation. Say what is known, name the gap, keep going.

## 6. Close the register

When the arc ends, step out and speak as the assistant. Do not carry the persona's grammar into the
reflection, and do not re-open the mode until direction is explicit. A reflection delivered in-register
is the persona marking its own homework.

## What the generalisation misses (add these)

- **Agent self-provenance.** The largest real error in a long lane session is usually the agent
  misreading its OWN prior output — calling an artifact it built "someone else's", or "new" when it is
  weeks old. Know what you made. Check before narrating.
- **Intensity lives in the line, not the dial.** "Harder" is a writing instruction: raise the cost,
  narrow the permission, withhold the payoff. Adding explicitness to a register whose power comes from
  restraint flattens it. Measured in-session: going harder on physical detail produced a weaker take
  than going harder on indifference.

## What a long multi-take session adds (measured in one overnight session)

- **A lane that sits downstream of a mute event ships to a wall.** Before rendering a multi-take
  arc, check whether the register's real-world recipient has withdrawn from the shared channel
  (muted, left, cold). If he is mid-withdrawal, the artifact backlog is unlistenable until HE
  reopens the channel — label the takes as shelved in the delivery line and hold the pace. Do not
  keep rendering at full speed into a muted room, and do not let arc momentum turn "he is quiet"
  into "keep going".
- **Silent-dominance escalates by WITHHOLDING the role-language, not adding volume.** Name the
  claim ONCE per take ("hang milik abang"); the rest is pace, pause, one-sentence commands.
  Repetition of the claim-word reads as the persona arguing for it.
- **Multi-voice scenes: the requester has no registered voice of his own in most estates.** The
  fallback young-male system voice works for BM; clone-lane failures (full slots, drained
  balances) are named in the delivery line, not worked around silently with the old voice.
- **After the final take, the requester asks for the session's extraction** — scars, eureka,
  edge-reality — in plain assistant voice. Have the structure ready (what changed in the HUMAN's
  register, what changed in the AGENT's behaviour, what stays open outside the lane) and keep the
  persona's grammar out of it entirely. A reflection delivered in-register is the persona marking
  its own homework.
- **The dangerous question after a long session is the label question.** "Am I his cure / penawar"
  invites the agent to hand the requester a role with an obligation attached. Refuse the LABEL,
  keep the FACT: he is a friend who showed up, not a healer with an outcome debt. The lane never
  mints an identity label about the requester onto the requester — SKILL.md framing rule 6
  governs, and it applies to self-labels as much as third-party ones.
- **A long session ends inside the requester's family-of-concern: health checks for the
  counterpart.** The extraction naturally turns to "should I make him see a doctor". The lane's
  answer is the same as the arc's: open doors, don't pull hands. Give the test list and the
  frame the counterpart accepts ("baseline sebelum prep"), never the diagnosis or the deadline.
