# Pricing a Dispute — Time Value and Optionality

When the user asks about a dispute, a concession or a foreign operator's entry "NPV wise", the question is **not** what the situation costs us. It is what the counterparty earns on capital it never had to pay, and what posture it bought with patience. Answer the counterparty's economics.

## The core reframe

A dispute over **who** rather than **how much** is a dispute over **time**. When a party does not contest the debt, only the destination of the payment, the principal was never at risk — only its timing. What is contested is the float.

State this at the top. It reframes every headline number downstream.

## Procedure

1. **Establish the quantum and the delay window.** Take the window from the order or the filing (the period the payment covers, and the date payment was ordered), not from the announcement of the dispute. Announcements date the controversy; orders date the cash.
2. **Compute the float.** `float = quantum × counterparty_cost_of_capital × years`. Use the *counterparty's* cost of capital — a supermajor's WACC runs ~6–9% — never ours. The float is what the counterparty did not have to pay to borrow.
3. **Express it against the counterparty's earnings.** `uplift = float ÷ net profit over the same window`. An uplift equal to a quarter of annual profit is a finding; a bare ringgit figure is not. This step is what turns arithmetic into a conclusion.
4. **Read the award for what it does NOT carry.** "The outstanding amount" carries no interest, no carrying cost, no time value. A principal-only award **splits the win**: the claimant wins the case, the respondent wins the economics. Report both lines — a judgement can be won on liability and lost on economics.
5. **Price the delay when it is unpenalised.** `effective price = price × (1 + r)^-N`. Long unpenalised delay discounts the real price to a fraction of the nominal one, automatically, every year it runs. A respondent contesting only destination is buying that discount for free.

## Clock asymmetry

Every actor runs on a clock. Political and jurisdictional actors run on electoral, dividend and ministerial cycles — short, and forced to show movement. An operator runs on asset life; a plant or field can be a thirty-year asset.

Where the clocks differ, the patient party is structurally advantaged. A "neutral" posture is then not neutrality — it is a **purchased option whose strike is time**, acquired free by the party that can wait.

## Read the pleadings for what was NOT contested

The scope of a claim defines the risk. Two patterns recur:

- **Contest only the payee, not the debt.** The party removes itself from the dispute entirely — every outcome is acceptable. Interpleader is the mechanism, not the motive; do not read neutrality as neutrality.
- **Contest only the scope of a law, not a payment.** The asset is relocated outside the constitutional or regulatory question's reach **without moving the asset** — the physical thing stays, the governing regime moves.

Ask explicitly what the party did *not* dispute. That silence is the position.

## A foreign operator entering a contested jurisdiction

Two structural moves get described with the same word ("transformation") and are not the same:

| Move | Mechanism | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Portfolio restructuring (satellite vehicle)** | Mature assets into a separately financed company; project debt rather than parent equity; parent keeps equity upside; vehicle runs on a lower cost base than the parent can | Capability preserved; financing changed |
| **Headcount reduction** | Cost falls; portfolio unchanged; the capability transferred out is later repurchased as consultancy | Cost down, capability down, portfolio unchanged |

The first is asset restructuring. The second is capacity withdrawal. Naming which one is happening is the finding.

**Why a third party can enter where two claimants cannot:** when two sovereign claimants each hold a veto, neither can accept the other as holder, and no new work can start while the question is open. A third party with no veto right is the only acceptable counterparty. Terms also improve for that entrant **every year the dispute stays unresolved**, because the sovereign's short clock forces concessions. Entry is therefore not opportunistic seizure — it is a priced position on an unresolved conflict, and the price is falling.

## What not to claim

- **A plausible mechanism you constructed is not evidence.** A causal chain that explains the facts ("clean books, therefore free to exit") is an inference. Label it, or drop it. Structural plausibility is the most seductive form of fabrication, because it is usually right about the mechanism and unsupported about the event.
- **Retract your own earlier figure explicitly.** If a number was asserted in a prior turn that the record does not support, name the number, say it is unsupported, and give the figure the sources actually carry. Softening it is worse than the original error — a rounded retraction leaves the bad number in play.
- **Do not attribute intent without a document.** Value accrues to whoever holds the longer clock. That is a structural outcome, not a plan. A posture that produces advantage is not proof of a strategy to produce it.
- **The winner's verdict is not a balance sheet.** Winning a case and winning the economics are different outcomes; both belong in the report.

## Pitfalls

- **Answer the layer that was asked.** "NPV wise" about a dispute means the counterparty's return on unpaid capital, not our cost of carry. Delivering carrying cost when the other side's return was wanted gets corrected, and the correction costs a turn.
- **Check whether liability was ever contested.** A respondent that disputed only destination was never exposed to the principal, so "they owed it and paid" understates how little was ever at risk.
- **Do not build on an unsupported quantum.** Verify the principal against a named outlet or the filing before computing anything. A float calculation built on an inflated principal is confident, precise and worthless.
- **Separate the physical asset from its governing regime.** A plant cannot be moved; the law governing its cash flows can. When an operator forms a vehicle in a third jurisdiction and writes the contracts under a neutral commercial law, the constitutional question at home no longer reaches those contracts. That is the relocation — not the asset.
