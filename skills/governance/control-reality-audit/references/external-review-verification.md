# Auditing an external AI's review of your own system

External reviewers arrive as prose that reads authoritative and carries no evidence. The task is not to accept or reject it — it is to **sort its claims**, the same way you would sort a control. Report the split, credit what verifies, refute what does not.

## Step 0 — provenance, before content

Ask what the reviewer actually had access to.

- If it says it observed the system ("I can see your logs") but was given only **your** summary, the provenance is fabricated. Everything downstream rests on it.
- If the reviewer's numbers **match yours exactly**, that is an echo, not corroboration. An echo cannot confirm a claim — the same error produces the same number twice. Agreement is evidence only when the two observers had **independent channels**.

## Step 1 — check every citation against its source

For each named person, work, date or study: does it exist, and does it say that?

- A citation that checks out is worth saying so plainly — that is what makes the rest of your audit credible.
- A vague gesture at a school of thought is not a citation. Mark it UNVERIFIABLE, not refuted.

## Step 2 — check every claim about your system against disk

Any claim about what exists, is configured, or happened must be probed. Unprobeable → UNVERIFIED. Do not soften it, and do not accept it because it sounds right.

## Step 3 — flag invented criteria

Watch for a criterion stated as though it were established law — a test, a standard, a rule. Search for it. If it appears nowhere but in the review, the reviewer **authored the rubric and then graded with it**. That is self-issued authority, and it is the single most useful thing to catch.

Same family: **a verdict label with no denominator.** A verdict word with no defined scale, no rubric and no measured input is a mood, not a finding. Ask what it is a fraction *of*; if there is no answer, report the label as unanchored.

## Step 4 — flag the interior-state move

"What you were **actually** doing was X, not Y" assigns a motive, state or intention to a person from text alone. No channel reaches an interior. This is a defect whether the reading is flattering or unflattering — a complimentary version is the same error wearing better clothes, and it slips through review precisely because it pleases.

The same move applied to third parties: attributing a private rationale to people whose reasoning was never heard. Record it as an inference about an interior, not as fact.

## Step 5 — flag the merge

Two incidents sharing a shape are not one story. A reviewer that folds an accidental incident and a deliberately-constructed experiment into one narrative has smoothed the record into something more tellable — the same defect as an unmeasured number.

## Step 6 — sort, then report the split

| Bucket | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **VERIFIED** | checked against source or disk, holds |
| **REFUTED** | checked, does not hold — give the contradicting evidence |
| **INVENTED** | presented as fact or as canon and exists nowhere |
| **UNVERIFIABLE** | no source reachable; state the gap, do not guess |

Lead with the verified items, then the refutations, then the invented ones. Keep the invented ones in the report — a fabricated criterion is the finding most likely to be re-quoted as authority later.

## The trap this skill exists to close

Do not let a review of your conduct **become** your self-description. A description of you is a claim about you, not a fact about you, and adopting it wholesale — especially a flattering one — converts an external guess into an internal constraint. Check it the same way you would check a claim about the codebase: who observed it, with what access, and does it survive contact with the record.
