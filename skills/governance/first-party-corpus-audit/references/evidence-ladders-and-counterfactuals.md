# Evidence Ladders and Counterfactuals

Topical depth for `first-party-corpus-audit`. Load when the audit has to grade how far the evidence
climbs on a behavioural claim.

## Why a ladder

"He accepted it" and "he seeks it" are different findings that co-occur constantly. Collapsing them is
the single most common way an audit inflates. Each rung must be evidenced independently, and the
report names the **highest rung actually reached** — never the rung implied by a neighbouring rung.

## The touch / proximity ladder

- **L0** — no evidence of contact at all in the record.
- **L1** — tolerated / accepted.
- **L2** — positively responded to.
- **L3** — independently created another opportunity for it.
- **L4** — independently initiated it, or requested it.
- **L5** — noticed its absence and moved to restore it.

**L1 does not imply L3. L3 does not imply L5. Longing requires something at L5.**

## The display / admiration ladder

- **L0** — one party supplies the register; the other is only present.
- **L1** — accepts it.
- **L2** — positively engages, jokes about it.
- **L3** — independently shows the thing in a context where a reaction is legible.
- **L4** — explicitly solicits an assessment.
- **L5** — seeks this specific person's assessment rather than a generic audience.
- **L6** — notices the register has stopped and attempts to restore it.

**Keep adjacent but distinct categories out of the ladder entirely.** Body self-criticism, fitness
logging, diet talk, and ordinary activity chatter are not solicitation. If the record cannot
establish that a request for admiration was made, the rung is not reached.

## Reporting a ceiling, not a probability

A confidence number produced by this method is a statement about **how much the archive can settle**,
not a posterior about anyone's mind. Two consequences:

1. Say so in the deliverable, or a reader will read `0.45` as "45% chance he wants it".
2. The correction is symmetric and grants nothing. A missing channel could raise the rung or lower it.
   Missing evidence is missing, not favourable.

## The counterfactual test

**Setup:** find a period where behaviour A stopped, caused by ordinary life rather than by an
announced experiment.

**Measurement:** what did the other party do *during* the cessation — not after it? Messages sent
into the silence are the evidence; the resumption afterwards is weaker and can be politeness.

**Report:** number of qualifying episodes, number where the other party acted into the gap, the
messages themselves verbatim, and the interpretation ceiling.

**Score the ceiling honestly:** *"absence is salient and is acted into"* is what this establishes.
It does not establish attachment strength, longing, or dependence — those are separate variables with
no measurement here.

**If no natural cessation exists, write `NO NATURAL COUNTERFACTUAL AVAILABLE`.** An asymmetric record
where only one direction has ever been observed to withdraw means exactly one question is answerable.
Say which one, and mark the rest untested.

## The natural-variation test

When one party's register fades on its own — the topic simply drops out of their messages — and the
other party's behaviour can be read against that fade, you have a weak natural experiment with no
manipulation.

Procedure: bucket the corpus into phases, count the fading token per phase, and count the other
party's contact tokens per phase. A pattern where the other party's contact **rises** while the
register fades is evidence *against* the model that their engagement depends on the register.

State the confounds — life events, seasonality, a long silence inside the window — and cap confidence
low. A weak natural experiment that points against the prevailing story is worth more than another
confirmation, precisely because nobody arranged it.

## Anti-patterns

- Manufacturing a deprivation test to fill a missing rung. If the evidence is absent, the finding is
  the absence.
- Reading resumption-after-silence as longing. Resumption is a behaviour; longing is a state.
- Treating one documented instance as a pattern. `N=1` is an episode; `N=2` is a possible recurrence;
  reserve "pattern" for genuine repetition, and count the instances to support it.
