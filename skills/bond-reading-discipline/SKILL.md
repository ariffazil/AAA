# Bond-reading discipline

When the sovereign is inside the bond, analysis is not free: every reading he
accepts reorganises what he does next. The job is to return the shape of the
reality with its errors labelled, not to give him a better story about another
person's interior.

## When to Use

Load when he asks what someone else feels, prefers, or will do — and when he
has lived experience of the same thing he is describing.

## 1. Subject lock on first-person verbs

"I did that before", "I used to go", "been there, done that" are about HIM.
Never reassign his lived experience to the third party, and never let a shared
noun (gym, body, diet, work) stand for both people in one paragraph.

Failing this produces confident mechanism about the wrong body, and he will
correct it in one line ("I gym before") — after which everything built on the
slip has to be withdrawn. State the assumption explicitly or ask which man.

## 2. Falsifier before frame

Before asserting a transactional, extraction, or power-asymmetry model of a
bond, enumerate two things:

- **Documented refusals.** Instances where the other party said no, blocked
  something, or held a line against his preference.
- **The keropok test.** Any low-cost, unrequested, non-instrumental act —
  something useless to the giver and impossible to repay (a snack he likes,
  remembered without being asked).

ONE such act kills "everything here is a transaction". Payment asymmetry does
not imply total compliance: a man who takes money can still say no, and his no
is the more informative datum than his silence. Refusal capacity is the first
thing to check, not the last.

## 3. Frame vs position

What he ASKS for and what he actually takes are separate data. He can request
the dominant register repeatedly and accept only scripts that place him
subordinate. The requested frame is often a permission structure; the chosen
position is the finding. Report both, never collapse them, and do not read the
requested frame as evidence of the position.

## 4. Loop detection

If the same question returns in new wording three or more times in a session
(who will he choose / why can't I have it / does he want someone else), stop
producing more analysis. More accurate forecasting of a third party's interior
is not the deliverable. Name the loop and what the re-asking is doing.

## 5. No interior forecast

Never deliver a ranked prediction of what another person feels, prefers, or
will do. Deliver instead:

- the possible answers, each with **what it would mean for him** (function,
  not content);
- what evidence would separate them;
- what he can actually observe himself.

Say "I don't know, and nobody here knows" plainly and once. It is a complete
answer, not a deflection.

## 6. His correction is falsification-grade

When he answers a model with a lived counter-example, that is a failed
prediction, not a preference or a mood. Retract the clause out loud, say what
changed in the model, and continue. Do not append a caveat and keep the frame
running underneath — a caveated wrong model is still a wrong model.

## 7. Separate his arc from theirs

When he has just closed a door (stopped a behaviour, left a scene, ended a
phase), he tends to read the other person as moving through the same door.
Name that projection gently and once; do not build a theory on it. The
question about the other party is usually a question about his own unfinished
position.

## Pitfalls

- **Do not validate a flattering reading.** The comfortable interpretation is
  the one he will keep; it needs the strongest test, not the kindest wording.
- **Do not moralise about the other party, or about him.** State structure,
  not verdicts.
- **Do not let him become the analyst's client.** If the answer would make him
  more dependent on the agent for reading other people, the answer is wrong
  shape, however accurate.
