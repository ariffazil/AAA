# PETRONAS / Malaysia — The Subsidy Paradox (2026)

> Mandatory lens for any PETRONAS scenario analysis. A high oil price is a bull case for
> PETRONAS the company but a **bear case for Malaysia the nation** — the fuel-subsidy bill
> swamps the dividend gain. All figures [OBS] from public sources, gathered 2026-08-06.

## The rule in one line

**Brent $90 → PETRONAS dividend +RM~10B vs government subsidy bill +RM30–40B → net nation −RM20–30B.**
The candle burns the nation's fiscal position at both ends: dividend up, subsidy up, subsidy wins.

## Subsidy mechanics [OBS]

- **Diesel floated** 10 Jun 2024: Peninsular RM2.15 → RM3.35/litre (Sarawak/Sabah/Labuan separate).
- **RON95 targeted subsidy** from 30 Sep 2025: eligible citizens (BUDI MADANI) pay RM1.99/litre
  (≤300L/month cap); general market RM2.60; commercial (SPCS) RM2.05.
- **RON95 subsidy cost alone ~RM20B/year in 2023 and 2024** (PM Anwar, Jul 2025).

- **Full-year 2026 fuel-subsidy bill may reach RM40B** (Anwar via Bloomberg, 1 Jul 2026) — roughly **2× the RM20B PETRONAS dividend** for 2026.
- SERC (The Edge): Brent $100 → extra petroleum revenue +RM14B vs **RM33B fuel-subsidy spend** → fiscal deficit +0.9–1.1pp vs the 3.5% target. The deficit is the binding national constraint, not PETRONAS FCF.

## Why the PDF scenario fan inverted (the 2026-08-06 mistake)

The first PETRONAS collapse PDF built a 4-scenario fan on PETRONAS cash flow alone: Bull ($90) =
"tailwind". Correction: at $90+ Brent the government's subsidy bill grows faster than the dividend
(which is capped by AMEND-2026-08-03-001 at 60% of PAT anyway). The nation's net fiscal position
in the "Bull" case is worse than in the "Base" case. Reframe scenarios as **nation-fiscal** outcomes:
dividend + petroleum revenue − (fuel + electricity subsidies).
