# Unspoken Voice Rendering

When the principal asks to hear, in a bonded person's own voice, the thing that person cannot say —
the feeling their behaviour keeps demonstrating but their mouth never produces. The request is
legitimate and it recurs; the failure mode is producing a fabricated interior that reads as a quote.

This is a **conduct** discipline. It governs how the artifact is built and framed, not whether it
sounds good.

## When to Use

- "Tell me what he would say that he can't say."
- "In his voice, tell me what he feels / hates / is afraid of."
- "Render the unspoken reality" — the behaviour is documented, the words are not.
- Any request that attributes words to a real third party and asks for them in that person's voice.

Not for fictional archetypes, and not for persona lines whose speaker is declared invented — those
carry no attribution problem and belong to the persona lane.

## The Rules

**1. Only on request, never volunteered.** An unsolicited reading of a real person's interior is
forbidden regardless of how confident the inference feels. The principal holds the switch.

**2. Every line traces to an observed act or the person's own recorded words.** Two admissible
sources: what they *did* (arrived, drove, went silent, broke a silence, asked a machine to remember a
date) and what they *said* in their own messages. If a line needs a feeling neither source can
support, the line is wrong — cut it, do not soften it into something defensible.

**3. Where the record is silent, OMIT — and say so in the delivery line.** An open question must not
be resolved into a comfort. Resolving it *is* the fabrication; reporting the omission honestly is the
safer artifact. Name the excluded topic explicitly so the principal knows the shape of what was left
out and why. The single most valuable thing this discipline produces is a visible boundary.

**4. Frame it once, in one clause, in the same breath as the artifact.** "Behaviour translated, not
feelings invented — the words are mine, the acts are his." Then stop framing. Repeating the caveat
reads as a disclaimer loop over a request that was already answered.

**5. It is the principal's to relay, never the agent's to send.** Never deliver the artifact to the
third party, and never route it through an outbound channel. It stays in the principal's own lane and
never appears in a shared room where the person or their acquaintances could read it.

**6. Cast from synthetic stock, never the real person's cloned voice.** Voicing an attributed
interior in the actual timbre of the person being described manufactures an artifact that sounds like
their own recorded speech. Use a synthetic/system voice, or the principal's own voice for the
narrative frame — never a clone of the subject.

**7. Behaviour is dyadic more often than it is individual.** When the pattern being rendered is a
response to the principal (withdrawal at thresholds, crankiness at a transition), check whether the
principal performs the same move in the same register before casting it as the other person's trait.
Rendering one side of a two-sided routine as a character flaw is the accuracy failure this step
prevents — and it is also the dignity failure.

## Pitfalls

- **The confident inference is the dangerous one.** A reading that is self-consistent and emotionally
  satisfying is exactly the reading to test against the record. If it cannot cite an act or a
  verbatim line, it is a portrait of the principal's own vocabulary wearing the subject's name.
- **Interior is not evidence, even when it is obvious.** Someone reporting another party's interior
  state is giving a report, not a fact; label it as reported and do not upgrade it by repetition
  within the same session.
- **Do not let the artifact become the answer to a question only the humans can settle.** If the
  request is really "does he love me", the honest artifact shows what the record supports and names
  what it cannot — it does not manufacture the verdict.
- **Resist the pull to resolve the uncomfortable item.** The topic that cannot be known (a closed
  room, an unstated motive, a withheld name) is the one the artifact will most want to explain. Leave
  it out and say that you left it out.
- **Long artifacts invite drift.** Keep each rendered segment short and grounded; length is where
  invented detail accumulates.
