# Sourcing and grading peptide/hormone claims

The user tracks named creators and often remembers a compound's protocol by the creator's claim rather than the creator's name. Hunt the **claim**, not the topic.

## Finding a creator remembered only by a claim

1. Search the distinctive claim phrasing **verbatim in quotes** plus one domain term. A remembered sentence ("MOTS-c needs CoQ10 and magnesium glycinate") is a far better key than the topic, because every channel in this space covers the same topics.
2. If that misses, search the **co-occurrence of the specific compounds** — `"MOTS-c" "magnesium glycinate" "CoQ10"` — instead of the presenter.
3. Check whether the name is a founder of a peptide brand. Ownership is often what separates near-identical channels from each other, and it is a cue the user may mention ("he sells his own peptides").
4. Confirm with a hard identifier — channel handle, brand domain, podcast name — then present the candidate and say which cues he may be blending together, instead of asserting one answer.

## Dead or moved channels

A 404 on `youtube.com/@handle` is usually **removal, not a typo**. Diagnose in order:

1. Re-check the handle spelling before declaring the channel dead — one wrong letter looks identical to a ban.
2. Search `<name> channel removed deleted` and `<name> rumble` / substack / Instagram.
3. Channels that discuss non-approved compounds are removed with some regularity; the standard fallback is Rumble plus an Instagram/Substack presence, and creators announce the move in their last uploads.
4. If he still cannot play it, separate the causes before advising: platform availability, regional blocking (needs VPN), and app-store availability are three different problems. Ask which one he is hitting.

## Grading the claims

- Most of this material is preclinical. State trial status once, then give the protocol — do not let approval status become the answer.
- Prefer physician-led channels, podcast interviews with the researchers who discovered the compound, and primary literature (PubMed/PMC) for mechanism and dose ranges.
- A company that sells the compound is not neutral. Flag the commercial interest in one clause and move on; don't turn it into the argument.
- Mechanistic-sounding cofactor or stacking claims are frequently untested in humans. Separate what was measured from what was merely inferred.
