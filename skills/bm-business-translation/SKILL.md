# BM Business Translation

Translate corporate, vending-retail and healthcare-administration documents into Bahasa Malaysia without drifting into literal or dated phrasing.

## When to load

- User asks to translate a company profile, letter, proposal, or specification into BM
- User asks for a BM version of an English document for submission to a Malaysian institution
- You are writing a new BM business document from scratch and need the correct register

## Core rules

1. **Translate by industry convention, not literally.** A literal rendering reads foreign to a Malaysian reader even when it is grammatically correct.
2. **Never translate proper nouns**: company name, `Sdn Bhd`, registration numbers, phone numbers, addresses, personal names, product names, document reference numbers.
3. **Keep numerals as figures** (`1940 mm`, `RM10,000`, `220–240V AC`), never spelled out.
4. **Preserve structure exactly**: numbered paragraphs, alpha sub-items `a) b) c)`, capitalised addressee block, right-aligned date. Translation changes words, not layout.
5. **Formal business register stays formal.** `Dengan segala hormatnya`, `Sehubungan itu`, `Sekian, terima kasih`, `Yang benar` — do not modernise these.

## Traps

- `vending machine` → **mesin vending**. `Mesin layan diri` is understood but reads dated in corporate documents.
- `cashless` → **tanpa tunai**, not a calque of "cash-less".
- A company is **syarikat**, never `sesi`.
- `excalation`/`escalation` in a service context is **peningkatan tindakan**, not a literal "eskalasi".
- Do not translate the `(M)` in a company name — it is part of the registered name.
- Where an English term is standard in the industry (`tempered`, `QR`, `e-dompet`), keeping it is correct; forcing a pure-Malay coinage is worse.
