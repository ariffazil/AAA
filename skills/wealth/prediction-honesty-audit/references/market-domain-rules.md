# Market Domain Rules — Commodity vs Equity

Same tool, different domain. Getting this wrong produces advice that is right for one and wrong for the other.

| | Gold / commodity | Company equity |
|---|---|---|
| Floor | durable; cannot be manufactured, rusted, or diluted | accounting; can be fabricated |
| Cash flow | none — cannot compound | dividends, reinvestment, growth |
| Information | scheduled and symmetric: Fed, NFP, CPI, dollar. Everyone sees it at the same moment | hidden and asymmetric: insiders know before the tape |
| The chart *is* | the reality | the belief of others; producible on purpose |
| What kills the holder | leverage and size, not being wrong | fraud plus leverage |
| Correct use of technicals | exit trigger — momentum death | exit trigger, **never** a fraud diagnosis |

## The two claims that are both true

- A **short-horizon trader** needs direction and momentum, which price gives faster than any report. Fundamentals contribute nothing at that horizon.
- A **long-horizon holder** needs the floor, because they will live through a drawdown and the floor is the only thing that stops them selling at the bottom.

When two people argue fundamentals vs technicals, the disagreement is usually a **horizon** difference, not a truth difference. Name the horizon instead of adjudicating. Do not pick a winner between people; pick the correct tool for the stated holding period.

## Company-specific cautions

- "It has never been sick" is not evidence. Collapses happen from a healthy-looking state; the chart is blind to cause but not to effect, and causes arrive at a cliff, not a slope.
- A technical signal tells you *that* buyers are gone, never *why*. That is enough to exit and not enough to diagnose. Waiting for the chart to say "fraud" means waiting until zero.
- An issuer with no ticker has no chart at all: no price signal, no insider-selling trace, no early warning. Do not simulate one; say the instrument is unobservable and measure the observable flow instead.
- Do not claim a commodity has no fundamentals. A commodity quoted against a currency inherits that currency's fundamentals — scheduled, public, and symmetric — and that is precisely what makes them tradeable.
- A published post-mortem chart annotated "the signal was here" is written by someone who knows the ending. Use it as a teaching artefact, never as evidence of predictive power.
