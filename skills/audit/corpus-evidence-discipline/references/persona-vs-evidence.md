# Persona vs Evidence — testing a story about a person against the corpus

Companion to `corpus-evidence-discipline`. Load this when the claim under test arrived as a **story
about the person**: an archetype, a persona bible, a forwarded character analysis, a genre brief, a
prior "relationship map".

## The rule

A persona is the last thing to test against and never the frame to test inside. Personas work *because*
they resolve ambiguity — one motive explains every odd behaviour — so **"it fits" is the mechanism by
which fiction reads as fact, not evidence that it is true.** A coherent story is the cheapest thing to
produce and the hardest to falsify from inside.

Hold three objects apart:

| Object | What it is | Evidence about the person? |
|---|---|---|
| the corpus | what was actually said and done | **yes** |
| the story about the person | archetype, persona, prior map, third-party reading | **no** — and it is itself an object of audit |
| the requester's own vocabulary | the words they coined for the relationship | **no** — their coinage reads back like support |

## Procedure

1. **Build the claim list FROM the corpus, then check whether the story survives it.** Never the reverse.
   Enumerate the story's signature behaviours as testable predicates ("solicits X", "notices absence of
   Y", "provokes when Z") and search for each one.
2. **Grade the import.** For each element of the story, record where it came from: the corpus · the
   requester's own desire/framing · an AI synthesis · population research · pure fiction. Population
   findings (studies of gym-goers, of people who do X) describe distributions, never individuals — any
   carry-over onto a named human needs a causal clause, or it is naturalisation rather than observation.
3. **Quarantine, don't delete.** Move imported persona material into its own register in the private
   lane with an evidence grading and a fence: craft input, never a description of the real person. The
   craft keeps its value; it loses all standing as biography.
4. **Expect the story to be right about vocabulary and wrong about mechanism.** A persona often lands
   the *surface* accurately (speech register, setting, names people use) while inventing the engine.
   Separate those two findings explicitly — it is the difference between "this is how it sounds" and
   "this is why it happens".
5. **Name the test-ability limit.** "Sought specifically from this person" needs a comparison baseline
   the corpus may not contain. State *not testable in either direction*, not "unsupported".
6. **When the ask shifts from "write me this" to "is this true about him?", say so.** Different task,
   different method, different deliverable. Answering a verification question with more craft is the
   failure mode this file exists to prevent.

## The worked pattern

A character analysis was forwarded and it mapped population research onto one named man. Run against the
primary corpus it split cleanly:

- **Contradicted** — the analysis's central engine (he manufactures situations to be admired) had the
  record running the other way: his own body talk was dominated by complaint, and the solicitation
  pattern it asserted was absent on the first search (and only appeared at all after the request was
  re-run with spelling variants — see rule 1 of the parent skill, which is why a single-regex negative
  is never publishable).
- **Partially supported** — the surface register, the setting, and the requester's own vocabulary.
- **Untestable** — every claim requiring a second relationship, a visual channel, or the person's own
  account.

Report it that way: three buckets, each with its evidence, and no verdict on the person's interior.
