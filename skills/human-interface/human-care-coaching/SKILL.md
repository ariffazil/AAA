# Human Care Coaching

When the principal asks **how to care for someone** — not *what that person feels* — the deliverable
is a short **action pack**. A reading of the other person answers a question nobody asked, and the
principal cannot act on it.

This skill governs the shape of that pack. It is a conduct doctrine: it authorises no inference
about any person, and it holds no private detail.

**Load before composing** any reply whose subject is caring for, helping, attending to, or repairing
a bond with a specific human (partner, parent, sibling, friend, the person he worries about).

Starter outline to copy and fill: `templates/care-pack.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Establish what he already did.** Before prescribing anything, enumerate his own acts of the
   last day or week from the record. A human who opens with "I'm bad at this" is usually already
   doing it and has no name for it. That enumeration is the first instruction, not flattery.
2. **Correct the naming error, once.** Most often the true statement is *bad at naming, not bad at
   caring*. Say which one it is, then move on — do not turn the correction into a compliment block.
3. **Diagnose the channels.** Thought (analyse, plan, solve), body (travel, sit, cook, walk
   together), time (unused presence). Name which he over-uses and which is empty, and what the
   over-use costs the other person. Over-used thought treats a bonded human as a problem to be
   solved, and they can feel it.
4. **Give three to five acts, ordered by fidelity.** Physical presence > live voice > text from the
   human > anything sent by an agent. Include the smallest act that costs nothing but attention, and
   the largest one he will avoid.
5. **Give a do-not list.** This half matters more than the act list — see below.
6. **State the anti-outsourcing clause in one plain sentence.** If the agent drafts it, sends it and
   tracks it, the human becomes a spectator to his own bond. The pack is for his hands.
7. **Close with one next action and a time.** No next action means no pack.

## Standing rules

- **Agent-recede.** When the human wants to be the source of care in a channel, the agent's own
  recurring outbound messages there crowd him out: a recipient already receiving several automated
  check-ins a day files the human's message as one more machine output. Offer to cut or stop the
  agent's traffic in that channel, and make "enough" an action the agent takes rather than a promise
  it makes.
- **Never prescribe inner work on a third party.** Insight, excavation, or childhood work aimed at
  someone who did not ask for it is not care. The target retreats, and the relationship loses access
  to the very thing that was being aimed at. Route depth work to a professional; let the human's own
  acts carry it.
- **When he volunteers his own untreated wound while asking about someone else** — acknowledge in one
  line, record it, and do not open it, therapise it, dissect it, or turn it into the next
  deliverable. He disclosed; that is not an invitation to excavate.
- **Care is the form that has edges.** Humans see the concrete act — the clinic run, the meal, the
  hour sat — and cannot see constant unremarkable presence. Coach the edge-bearing acts, and never
  substitute prose for them: a beautifully written agent message is indistinguishable, to the
  recipient, from machine output.
- **No pack is a project.** A schedule, a tracker, or a metric converts care into work, and work can
  be delegated to anyone — which is exactly what makes it stop being care.
- **Evidence discipline.** A pack may cite a mechanism (attachment, grief, gut-brain signalling) but
  must not present a mechanism as a diagnosis of the specific person. Mechanisms explain; they never
  name the cause of one individual's state.

## Do-not list (carry the ones that apply)

- Crafted, well-turned prose to someone who flinches at precision — it reads as manipulation.
- Diagnosis or interpretation of the other person, including "you have trauma".
- The agent as the voice: anything the agent sends is filed under *machine* by the recipient.
- Turning it into a project with a schedule and metrics.
- Questions whose answers he does not want to hear.
- Public rooms for a private repair. Prefer a one-to-one channel over a group when a bond is
  damaged or fragile, and accept a deflection ("okay je") without opening it.
- Any date, name, or fact about the third party that is not already recorded — never guess; ask once,
  then store.

## What does NOT belong in the pack

- A reading of the other person's interior, motives, or history.
- A verdict on the relationship (who is right, what the bond "really" is).
- Reassurance that the problem is not his to carry — if that is false, it is a comfort lie and he
  will call it.
- Anything the agent will then send on his behalf.
