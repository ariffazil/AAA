# Malaysian Commercial Premise Sourcing

"Nak tapak bisnes" is three different questions with wildly different capital.
Classify BEFORE advising — never answer with all three at once, and never
answer with only the one that matches your first guess.

| Route | Modal | Speed | Who holds the power |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sublet a lot inside an existing foodcourt | rendah | weeks | the foodcourt operator |
| PBT / medan selera tapak (majlis perbandaran) | rendah | months | local authority (tender/ballot, local-resident priority) |
| Own premises (rent a shoplot, build the foodcourt) | ratus ribu | 6-12 months | landlord + contractor + Bomba |

## Step 1 — classify from the message

Read scale, do not interrogate. Someone with no capital asking "macam mana nak
dapat tapak foodcourt" means route 1 or 2. Route 3 only if they already name a
location, a renovation budget, or say they want to own it.

## Step 2 — the sublet route (the usual answer)

- Go to the mall management office or the foodcourt operator directly with
  concept, menu, and an estimated sales figure — not "berapa sewa".
- What the operator is really screening: can this tenant carry rent through the
  quiet months, does the concept collide with existing tenants, is there a track
  record, will they walk away in three months.
- Expect a company profile request, deposit 3+3, and often revenue sharing
  15-20% on top of base rent.

## Step 3 — the licence and permit stack (every route)

SSM registration · lesen perniagaan PBT · lesen premis makanan · kursus
pengendali makanan (one day, cheap) · typhoid injection for everyone who touches
food · halal via JAIS/JAKIM only if halal is being claimed · Bomba sign-off on
kitchen layout and exhaust.

Malls usually handle building structure. Grease trap, exhaust ducting and waste
removal are commonly the tenant's — confirm in writing. That is the single most
expensive surprise for a first-time F&B tenant.

## Step 4 — due diligence before any signature

- **Traffic count yourself**, three different days, at the hours the operator
  promises. Their number is marketing; yours is evidence.
- **Exclusivity clause** — they must not be free to put three of your category
  side by side the month after you open.
- **Utility and repair split** — who pays electricity, water, aircond service,
  and who eats a break-down that is nobody's fault.
- **The operator's own head-lease** — is the signatory the landowner or a
  sublessor? Ask for their tenancy agreement with the landlord and check the
  remaining term. If their lease has two years left, never sign five years with
them: your sublease dies with their head-lease.
- **Contracting entity vs the signboard** — the plaque says one company, the
  agreement often names another. The agreement governs.
- **Deposit burn** — do not take a big lot at high rent for an untested concept.
  Small lot, six months, prove the menu, then migrate.

## Step 5 — counter-party entity check

When a signboard or "DIMILIKI OLEH" plaque arrives with or without a caption,
read the entity and verify it before saying anything about them.

**Decode the registration number.** New 12-digit format is `YYYY` + 2-digit
entity-type code + 6-digit serial — the first four digits are the incorporation
year, and that alone is safe to state. Legacy format is 6-7 digits plus a state
letter (`M` Wilayah Persekutuan KL, `B` Selangor, `P` Pulau Pinang, `J` Johor,
`A` Melaka, `D` Kelantan, `K` Kedah, `N` Negeri Sembilan, `T` Terengganu).
Modern plaques print both: new number in front, old in brackets. The number
tells you age and place of registration — nothing about ownership, payment
history, or capital.

**Verify at the authority.** SSM e-Search / MyDATA SSM
(`mybizdata.malaysiabiz.gov.my`), company profile about RM15, or the MySSM app
for a status check. Asking the counter-party to hand over their SSM profile is
free and diagnostic: a serious operator hands it over without drama, and
reluctance is itself information. CTOS only when the exposure is large.

**Read in the profile:** status (active vs striking-off, check first),
incorporation date, directors and shareholders (is the person negotiating one
of them?), paid-up capital, and registered address (a residential unit or
virtual office tells you the real scale).

**Match test:** signboard name = SSM name = the entity on the tenancy
agreement = the person signing is a director of it. Any mismatch is a question
to ask before signing, not a scam verdict — branding one company and holding
the lease in another is common and usually innocent.

## Pitfalls

- Public aggregator sites index companies by NAME. Failure to resolve a
  registration number on an aggregator does not mean the company does not
  exist. Report "belum disahkan", quote only what the plaque shows, and never
  fill the gap with inferred detail.
- Never state SSM data you did not get from SSM. Incorporation year is
decodable from the number; directors, capital and status are not.
- A recently registered operator (reg year = this year or last) is a real fact
  with two sides: terms are usually more negotiable, and survival risk is real.
  Say it as negotiating posture, never as a verdict on the people behind it.
- Do not answer the question from the licence list alone. Permits are the easy
  part; the tenancy terms are where the money is lost.
- Residential rental disputes (deposit, aircond, who repairs) belong to
  `malaysia-tenancy-agreement`. This skill is commercial premises only.

## Reply shape

- Plain BM in paragraphs. No tables, no jargon, no numbered system-talk.
- If the question is relayed from someone else (a friend, a client), answer that
  person's question in their own register. Do not address the relay as the
  subject and do not add commentary about them.
- No moralising about risk. State the two or three checks that actually move the
  outcome and let the person decide.
- Close with one concrete next step (what to photograph, which document to
  request) — never a menu of options.
