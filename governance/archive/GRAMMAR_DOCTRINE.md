## 3. Bilingual Dimension

English and Bahasa Melayu compress information differently:

| Feature | English | Bahasa Melayu | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time/aspect | Grammaticalized (*went, has gone*) | Lexical (*sudah pergi, akan pergi*) | BM needs stronger discourse tracking |
| Agreement | *He runs* vs. *they run* | *Dia pergi, mereka pergi* | English has extra local error signals |
| Word formation | Moderate inflection | Productive affixation (meN-, peN-, ber-, -kan) | BM tokenization must preserve roots + affixes |
| Negation | *don't, won't, never* | *jangan, belum, tidak, bukan* | BM negation is richer in pragmatic scope |
| Code-switching | Single regime | BM-English within one clause | Language-ID and intent parsing can drift |

**For agents:** BM-English code-switched input must be normalized into a **language-neutral action schema** before planning. Keep original utterance as evidence; execute on typed fields.

**Dual validation model:**
- English-brained (structural): check schema, types, explicit rules
- BM-brained (contextual): check coherence, pragmatics, implicit context
- Bilingual agents need BOTH
