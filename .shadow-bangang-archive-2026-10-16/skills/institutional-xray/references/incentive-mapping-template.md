# Incentive Mapping — 7-Layer Template

## How to Use

When analyzing ANY corporate or government communication, map these7 layers in order. Each layer reveals a different audience and a different purpose.

## The7 Layers

### Layer1: Narrative Inoculation
**Question:** What attack is being pre-empted?

**Pattern:** The speaker addresses a criticism BEFORE it becomes viral. This is defense, not information.

**Example:** "Petronas bukan syarikat biasa... RM1.6T sumbangan" — pre-empting the "Petronas untung, rakyat menderita" attack.

**Detection:** Look for defensive framing that appears BEFORE the question is asked.

### Layer2: Soft Warning
**Question:** What is being prepared for without saying so?

**Pattern:** Repeated timeline guarantees with built-in expiration dates. The guarantee IS the warning.

**Example:** "Bekalan terjamin sehingga akhir Mei" — said THREE times. The repetition is the signal.

**Detection:** Count how many times a specific commitment is repeated. Frequency = anxiety level.

### Layer3: Policy Seeding
**Question:** What future policy change is being normalized?

**Pattern:** Comparative data that makes the current policy look generous. The comparison IS the seed.

**Example:** "Thailand diesel RM6+/liter tanpa subsidi" — seeding the idea that Malaysia's subsidy is abnormally generous.

**Detection:** Look for "atas dasar perbandingan" — comparisons that serve a specific policy agenda.

### Layer4: Institutional Mythology
**Question:** What origin myth is being constructed?

**Pattern:** Historical parallels that position the institution as INEVITABLE and INDESTRUCTIBLE.

**Example:** "1973-74 → Petronas lahir. 1980 → survive. 1991 → survive. COVID → survive."

**Detection:** Count how many crisis-to-survival stories are told. Each one reinforces the institution's claim to permanence.

### Layer5: Market Signal
**Question:** What message is for international audiences?

**Pattern:** English phrases inserted into non-English communication. Each English phrase targets a specific international audience.

**Example:** "We are long" (gas) — signals to LNG traders. "No regret move" — signals to analysts. "Efficiency is the first fuel" — signals to WEF.

**Detection:** Map each English insertion to its likely international audience.

### Layer6: The Silences
**Question:** What topics are deliberately absent?

**Pattern:** The most important operational data is NEVER mentioned. The silences are the strategy.

**Example:** No post-May supply detail. No actual inventory levels. No subsidy cost in ringgit. No downstream loss admission.

**Detection:** List what a genuine expert WOULD say in this context. Anything missing from that list is a deliberate silence.

### Layer7: Venue Choice
**Question:** Why THIS channel, not another?

**Pattern:** The venue reveals the actual audience. Domestic state media = domestic audience. Bloomberg = international investors. ASEAN forum = regional partners.

**Example:** RTM/Astro AWANI = Malay middle class + political class + bureaucrats. NOT investors. NOT international.

**Detection:** Ask: if the message were for a different audience, where would it be delivered? The DIFFERENCE between actual and alternative venues reveals the intent.

## The Meta-Pattern

Every communication satisfies 6 of7 layers simultaneously. The7th layer is usually the most revealing because it is the LEAST obvious to casual observers.

The layers are not sequential — they are CONCURRENT. One sentence can serve 3 layers at once.
