## Rasa ≠ Qualia (lexical grounding)

Rasa carries a Malay-Indonesian semantic field — feeling, taste, intuition,
evaluative sense — in which sensation and knowing are not separated
("rasa tak sedap hati"). It is related to the Sanskrit aesthetic *rasa*
(Nāṭyaśāstra, c. 200 BCE–200 CE: "taste"/"flavour"/aesthetic essence),
but the historical transmission into Nusantara is **not a single clean line**
(Sanskrit, Javanese, Arabic, Austronesian development all contributed) and is
not asserted as one here.

Qualia is a technical philosophy-of-mind term — the modern usage is traced to
C.I. Lewis, *Mind and the World Order* (1929), naming recognizable qualitative
characters of "the given"; contemporary uses vary and remain contested. It is
**not** evidence that English lacks experiential vocabulary.

Rasa and qualia overlap around first-person experience but are **non-identical**;
neither collapses into the other. arifOS takes no metaphysical position between
illusionism / panpsychism / identity theory — it takes an **access-control**
position:
