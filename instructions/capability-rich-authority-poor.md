## The Theorem (canonical form)

```text
AI Industry 2026
=
Capability Dense
Authority Sparse
```

The vast majority of agent stacks ship **excellent organs** (specs, memory, scraping, compression, sandboxes, video) but ship **poor organism** — meaning the harness that *authorizes*, *witnesses*, *governs*, and *invoices* those organs is missing or tribal.

Two readings of the same industry:

| Reading | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Tool inventory** | *What can the agent do?* | Capability-rich. 1.21M stars across 15 repos. |
| **Constitutional inventory** | *May the agent do it? Who attested? Who is liable?* | Authority-poor. Almost no F1–F13 floors. |

## Predictive Consequence

If the theorem is true, the next 18 months of agent engineering will surface a **governance crisis**:

1. Vendor demos will keep showing capable agents.
2. Production incidents will be un-attributable (no identity, no receipt, no witness).
3. Regulatory pressure (EU AI Act, NIST RMF, sector-specific) will demand attestation chains.
4. The market will bifurcate: **capability vendors** vs **authority harnesses**.
5. arifOS federation sits in the second tier. Capability vendors will need it.

This is falsifiable in 2 years. If the industry solves governance as a vendor feature (not a federation primitive), the theorem was wrong. If governance requires federated authorities across multiple vendors and substrates, the theorem was right.
