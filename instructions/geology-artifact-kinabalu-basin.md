# Kinabalu Basin Structural Genesis & Petroleum System Map (Acts 1–5)

## Executive Summary

This artifact provides a comprehensive tectonic and petroleum system interpretation of the NW–SE transect through the Kinabalu Basin. It connects the structural evolution to the stratigraphic architecture and hydrocarbon plays, transforming the cross-section from a static visualization into a dynamic geological narrative.

## 1. Tectonic Acts Timeline (40 Ma → Present)

### Act 1: Sarawak Orogeny (~40–22 Ma)
- **Event**: Rajang Group folding, Crocker accretion initiation
- **Structural Consequence**: Formation of extended continental crust and initial half-graben structures
- **Petroleum Consequence**: Oligocene syn-rift deposits in the Layang-Layang Basin

### Act 2: Sabah Orogeny (22–20 Ma)
- **Event**: Dangerous Grounds collision, BMU cut
- **Structural Consequence**: Development of Sabah Trough foredeep
- **Petroleum Consequence**: Syn-rift hydrocarbon accumulation in the Layang-Layang Basin

### Act 3: Cagayan Arc / MMU–DRU (15.5 Ma)
- **Event**: End of rifting, inversion begins
- **Structural Consequence**: Angular unconformity truncating older units
- **Petroleum Consequence**: Reset of stratigraphic architecture, creation of new traps

### Act 4: Kinabalu Granite Emplacement (10–8 Ma)
- **Event**: Granodiorite intrusion
- **Structural Consequence**: Thermal and mechanical modification of basin architecture
- **Petroleum Consequence**: Formation of Kinabalu closure and Stage IV reservoirs

### Act 5: Meliau Orogeny (L. Plio. – Recent)
- **Event**: NW–SE strike-slip transpression
- **Structural Consequence**: Development of W-vergent thrust sheets
- **Petroleum Consequence**: Creation of major petroleum traps (Kinabalu closure)

## 2. Structural Architecture Map

### Five Basin Provinces:
1. **Dangerous Grounds** - Extended continental crust with syn-rift half-graben
2. **Sabah Trough** - Sediment-filled foredeep (~2.9 km)
3. **Crocker Range** - Accretionary prism (L. Eocene–Oligocene)
4. **Kinabalu Basin** - Post-MMU fold-thrust system
5. **Sulu Sea** - Deep basin fill (1,500–4,000 m)

### Key Fault Systems:
- Listric growth fault (Dangerous Grounds)
- Strike-slip fault system (Meliau Orogeny)
- Thrust sheets (T1–T4) in Kinabalu Basin

## Scientific Narrative

The NW–SE transect through the Kinabalu Basin represents a complete tectonic evolution from rift to inversion to active deformation. Each tectonic act built upon previous structures, creating a complex but predictable petroleum system.

The basin's evolution began with the Sarawak Orogeny, which initiated the formation of extended crust and syn-rift basins. The Sabah Orogeny created the Sabah Trough foredeep, providing accommodation space for sedimentation. The Cagayan Arc marked the end of rifting and initiation of inversion, creating the angular unconformity that resets the stratigraphy.

The Kinabalu granite emplacement introduced thermal modification and structural complexity, while the ongoing Meliau Orogeny drives the development of W-vergent thrust sheets that form the major petroleum traps.

This systematic evolution explains why the basin architecture produces multiple hydrocarbon plays with distinct characteristics, from syn-rift carbonates to post-MMU clastics, all organized within a coherent structural framework.
