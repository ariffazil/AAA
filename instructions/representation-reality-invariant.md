## The invariant

**Representation ≠ reality — and it fails in BOTH directions.**

A claim that something **exists** is falsifiable. A claim that something is **absent** is equally
falsifiable, and the auditor is structurally less likely to double-check the negative. Ghost
capability and phantom absence are the same defect with the sign flipped.

## The four tests

1. **Present tense is a claim, not a status.** *"X implements Y"* is a state claim and needs a probe,
   not agreement. The more precise the present tense, the **more** it needs one — specificity is
   exactly what makes a false state-claim feel already verified.
2. **A negative needs the same warrant as a positive.** Before writing *"zero"*, *"none"*, or
   *"does not exist"*: state the population, show the method, and name what you did **not** look at.
   A negative confirmed once is a hypothesis; twice, by different means, it is a finding.
3. **Read the hit in its enclosing scope.** A grep line is not a capability. It may be a test fixture,
   a proposal awaiting wiring, a docstring, or an unrelated substring. Open it.
4. **Read every layer of a control before trusting or condemning it.** A gate has a policy table, a
   decision function, and callers. Measured: a gate "failing open" had an explicit override parameter
   its single caller never passed — the defect was one unplumbed argument, not a missing policy
   (full case: `agent-finding-verification` v1.1.0).

## Why this is a *constitutional* observation, not a technical one

The industry statement's most dangerous line was not about machines feeling. It was about machines
**acting**: systems that can set their own goals, earn money, and own assets. That is wrote access
without a gate — an **authority** question, not a consciousness question.

`CanMutate = AuthorityGranted ∧ ScopeMatches ∧ TargetPermitted ∧ BoundaryActive`

The expression contains **no confidence term**. Therefore no model capability, no benchmark score,
and no internal state may widen it. Stated industry-side, the missing organ is exactly this:

> **The executor may never issue its own envelope.**

document authored by the party it governs, with no independent issuer — which is why arifOS's
answer is structural rather than declarative: F13 holds the veto, VAULT999 witnesses, the floors bind
the sovereign too. Not because a document claims they do, but because the mechanism refuses when a
party tries.
