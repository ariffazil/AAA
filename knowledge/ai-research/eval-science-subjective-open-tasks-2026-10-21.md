## 1. Source digests (one dense block each)

**Lilian Weng — human data.** "Everyone wants to do the model work, not the data work." Rater pipeline = task design → rater selection/training → collection/aggregation; every step leaks quality. Descriptive vs prescriptive annotation paradigms (Rottger 2021): descriptive models many beliefs (disagreement = signal), prescriptive applies one belief consistently (disagreement = error). Disagreement taxonomy (Zhang 2023): separate stochastic error from legitimate diversity — only the first is noise. Annotator identity is statistically significant for subjective labels (toxicity). Expert–crowd agreement swings 0.96→0.25 by topic. Training dynamics find mislabels: Data Maps (confidence × variability — hard-to-learn + low-variability ⇒ suspect), AUM (margin under fake-class threshold), unforgettable examples. Aggregation: MACE-style competence weighting beats raw majority vote; spammers exist and must be downweighted.

**Eugene Yan — labeling guidelines.** A guideline must answer Why (motivates effort) / What (task + define EVERY term) / How (decision process + calibrated examples with explanations + logistics + tie-breaks). Decision trees and worked examples raise inter-rater agreement. Give an "Unsure" escape hatch — forced wrong judgments poison labels. Make subjective criteria objective ("Is this adult content?" → "Is this nudity?"); Google says "use your judgment" 19× — judgment is allowed but only after structure. Cohen's kappa is the iteration compass: rising kappa across guideline revisions = right track. Binary responses beat numeric scales for precision and throughput.

**Wolfe — LLM-as-a-Judge core.** Three setups: pairwise (stable but O(n²)), pointwise (scalable but noisier — absolute scores drift), reference-guided (reference helps hardest domains). Strong judge ≈ 80% human agreement ≈ human–human agreement — the ceiling is human consistency itself. Named biases: position, verbosity, self-enhancement, capability (can't grade what it can't solve), distribution (score clumping). Mitigations: position switching + average, few-shot calibration to the natural score distribution, references for hard domains, jury of different judges. CoT rationale must precede the score — post-hoc explanations don't support the conclusions they decorate. AlpacaEval length-controlled win rate = regression debiasing ("what if all outputs had baseline length?") — the canonical anti-verbosity trick.

**Hamel — Critique Shadowing (the operational core).** 30+ companies distilled to one loop: (1) find THE principal domain expert — one person whose judgment defines success; (2) build a diverse dataset over explicit dimensions (features × scenarios × personas; synthetic user inputs are fine, synthetic *AI behavior* is not); (3) expert judges binary PASS/FAIL + writes a detailed critique (detailed enough to be a few-shot example — "a new employee could understand"); (4) fix obvious errors first; (5) seed the judge prompt with expert critiques, iterate until >~90% agreement, then (6) error analysis: slice failure rates by dimension, classify error traces by root cause by hand, fix, write a regression test per fixed error; (7) specialized judges only where slices say so. Hard rules: 1–5 dashboards are a smell ("if someone says measure 8 things on a 1–5 scale, they don't know what they're looking for"); raw agreement misleads under class imbalance — report TPR/TNR separately vs human ground truth; ~30 examples until no new failure modes, ~100 per failure mode to validate a judge, <60 labels ⇒ CI too wide. **Criteria drift** (Shankar et al.): criteria cannot be fully specified before judging — the act of grading is what finalizes the criteria. And the kicker: the judge is "a hack to trick people into looking at their data" — the data review is the value.

**Eugene Yan — evaluators & process.** Off-the-shelf evals barely correlate with application-specific performance — task-specific evals or none. One dimension per prompt beats multi-dimension scoring. Simple direct scoring + CoT often beats elaborate rules/rubrics (nuance vs Autorubric findings below — both true: rubrics help *reliability*, simple CoT sometimes wins *accuracy*; the honest statement is configuration-dependent, no universal stack). Dev-time evaluator (API, few hundred samples, tolerate latency) ≠ production guardrail (fine-tuned classifier, cheap, fast). "An LLM-as-judge won't save the product — fixing your process will": evals = scientific method + eval-driven development + monitoring; automated evaluators AMPLIFY whatever annotation process exists — no annotation loop, no evaluator.

**Wolfe — rubrics as rewards.** Rubric = checklist of K self-contained criteria + weights (categorical critical/pitfall/important/optional > numeric for interpretability), often instance-specific, LLM-generated but expert-grounded (purely synthetic rubrics underperform). Explicit aggregation (weighted sum of per-criterion judge calls) vs implicit (judge aggregates) — implicit slightly better, explicit more controllable. Beyond weighted average: veto mechanisms, saturation-aware aggregation, pairwise interaction between criteria, targeted shaping. **Seesaw effect**: training on mixed rubric types creates conflicting objectives → multi-stage curriculum (instruction-following first, open-ended second). Anti-reward-hacking rubric: mine rollout data for abnormally-high-reward patterns, taxonomy them, add a rubric criterion per pattern, iterate. Rubric quality ≫ rubric quantity; ~5K curated samples sufficient. Constitutional AI precedent: derive reward from the written criteria themselves — when criteria change, you edit the constitution, not the dataset.

**Autorubric (Rao & Callison-Burch).** Rubrics structure evaluation but ADD consequential choices: criterion design, scale type, weighting, aggregation, abstention, calibration, reliability measurement. Judge failure modes persist under rubrics: position bias, stochastic inconsistency, criterion conflation, forced judgment under uncertainty, model-dependent calibration. Empirical result: "configuration effects that do not support a universal mitigation stack." Abstention is first-class — forcing a verdict without evidence is a bug. Per-criterion scores + explanations feed agent skill revision and reward modeling. The **reinvention tax**: every team rebuilds eval machinery instead of accumulating on shared infrastructure.

**Miller / Wolfe — statistics.** Evals are experiments; a benchmark score estimates a latent skill over a super-population — the dataset is one finite sample. Report mean + standard error + n always (CLT); Bernoulli shortcut for binary scores. **Clustered SE** when questions correlate (same doc/topic/language/host): naive SE underestimates up to 3×; effective n < n. Variance = question variance (immutable) + within-question variance (cut by K resamples until E[σ²]/K ≪ Var(x); or use token probabilities for σ²→0). Don't change temperature to reduce variance — it changes the eval target. **Paired differences** for model-vs-model on same questions: models correlate on difficulty, so the difference estimator is a "free" variance reduction; CI-of-difference excluding zero beats the conservative overlap-of-separate-CIs check.

**Wolfe — benchmark anatomy.** Curation patterns: crowd+manual (MMLU, 6.49% label-error rate — fixing errors flipped Llama-405B from 16th→1st in Virology), expert-written + expert-validated + non-expert-Google-proofed (GPQA Diamond), community PR review (BIG-Bench), model-in-the-loop difficulty filtering (BBEH: keep tasks where reference models <70%). IFEval→IFBench is the contamination law: 25 published constraints → industry synthetic-data generators adopted the same taxonomy → inflated scores; fix = fresh constraint set (58 new). Benchmarks need evolution machinery: difficulty/quality/diversity refinement, or continuous renewal (MathArena). IRT/psychometrics for item selection and informativeness.

**Wolfe — agent evals.** Vocabulary: task / trial / transcript / outcome / grader / eval-harness vs agent-harness (you evaluate model+scaffold together — decoupling is structurally hard). Scaffold effects are large; weaker model + accommodating scaffold can beat the reverse. Grade the **outcome state**, not the trajectory — rigid step-checks punish unanticipated valid solutions (τ2-bench: Opus found a policy loophole that was BETTER for the user and "failed"). Grader taxonomy: code-based (fast/objective/brittle), model-based (flexible/calibrate-against-humans), human (gold/slow). Multiple graders per task; partial credit for multi-component tasks. Context rot is universal → compaction, note-taking, tool-result clearing are eval-relevant capabilities.

**Anthropic — demystifying evals for agents.** Eval-driven development: write evals BEFORE the capability exists; capability evals should START at low pass rate (they're the hill), regression evals sit at ~100% (they're the guardrail); capability suites "graduate" into regression suites. 20–50 tasks from real failures is a great start; wait too long and you reverse-engineer success criteria from a live system. Task quality bar: two domain experts independently reach the same verdict; a reference solution exists that passes all graders; **0% pass@100 = broken task, not incapable agent**. Balanced sets: test should-occur AND should-not-occur (Claude.ai search evals — undertrigger vs overtrigger). Isolated clean environments: shared state causes correlated failures AND inflated scores (Claude read leftover git history from prior trials). Read transcripts — "we do not take eval scores at face value until someone digs in and reads some transcripts"; failures should feel fair. Opus 4.5 CORE-Bench 42%→95% purely from grading fixes (rigid "96.12" vs "96.124991", ambiguous specs, unreproducible stochastic tasks). pass@k (≥1 of k) vs **pass^k (all k)** — consistency is the customer-facing metric (0.75/trial ⇒ 0.42 pass^3). LLM judges: grade each dimension with an ISOLATED judge, give an escape hatch ("Unknown") to avoid hallucinated confidence. Swiss-cheese model: evals + monitoring + A/B + user feedback + transcript review + human studies — layers, not a single wall.

## 3. The five sharpest eurekas (contradiction-form)

1. **Judge calibration gap (MODEL vs OBS)** — arifOS judges constitutionally but not statistically; the label store (Arif's overrides) already exists and is unmined.
2. **Override = annotation event (TIME vs TIME)** — treating F13 corrections as exceptions guarantees recurrence; treating them as criteria formation closes the loop.
3. **Receipts without error bars (MODEL vs MODEL)** — a point-estimate SEAL on a stochastic task is a confidence claim with no n; pass^k exposes it.
4. **Two paradigms, one organism (DOMAIN vs DOMAIN)** — prescriptive floors and descriptive rasa are not in conflict; they are the two annotation paradigms the literature says must never be merged.
5. **Audit contamination (SCALE vs SCALE)** — what works locally (published checklist → compliance) fails globally (published checklist → theater).
